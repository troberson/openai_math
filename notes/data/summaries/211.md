## undergraduate

A **random planar map** is a graph drawn on a sphere
with edges at random — think of a network of roads
on Earth, where the roads are generated by a
random process. The **Fortuin–Kasteleyn (FK)
model** is a way to assign "weights" to the edges of
this map based on a parameter $q$ (the number of
"colors" in a color-assignment game). As the number
of edges $n$ grows, the rescaled map (dividing all
distances by $\sqrt{n}$) converges to a limiting
geometric object. The **critical** case (a specific
value of the edge weight) is where the transition
happens: for $q \leq 4$ the limit is a
**Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) sphere** (a
"fractal" surface with curvature concentrated at
random points), and for $q > 4$ the limit is the
**Brownian continuum random tree** (a fractal tree
— all the "surface" has collapsed into a
1-dimensional object). This is the **surface-to-tree
transition**. The family also studies **random
walks** on these maps: as $n \to \infty$, the walk
converges to **Liouville Brownian motion** on the
LQG sphere [S3:55–63, S4:44–49, S2:507].

## graduate

**The surface-to-tree transition** [S2:507]. For
critical FK planar maps with $0 < q \leq 4$, the
rescaled metric-measure space converges in
distribution to the LQG sphere in the Gromov–
Hausdorff–Prokhorov topology. For $q > 4$, the
limit is the Brownian continuum random tree. The
transition at $q = 4$ is sharp.

**The FK–Ising walk limit** [S3:55–63,
S2:507]. Stationary random walks on critical
spherical FK–Ising planar maps converge to
Liouville Brownian motion on the ordinary unit-area
$\sqrt{3}$-quantum sphere. The walk chooses
uniformly among all incident half-edges, retaining
loops and multiple edges. With the corner measure as
the stationary law, the deterministic time
acceleration is exactly the number of map edges.

**The Brownian CRT limit** [S4:44–49, S5:9].
For every fixed $q > 4$, after rescaling graph
distances by a constant depending on $q$ times
$n^{-1/2}$ and using normalized degree measure, the
metric-measure space converges in distribution to
the Brownian continuum random tree in the
Gromov–Hausdorff–Prokhorov topology.
The convergence holds through all positive integer
sizes.

**The scope of the formalization.** The scope note
covers only the Brownian CRT limit for $q > 4$
[S5:9]. The FK–Ising walk, spectral, linear-
clock, conformal-limit, critical-sphere, and metric-
measure manuscripts are outside the formalized scope.
The scope note names one comparator [S5:15]:
`FKCRT.lean`.

## history

The **Fortuin–Kasteleyn (FK) model** (1969)
generalizes the Ising model and the q-state Potts
model. The **critical FK planar maps** are the
discrete approximations to the **Liouville quantum
gravity (LQG)** surface in two dimensions. The
**surface-to-tree transition** at $q = 4$ is a
fundamental phase transition in random geometry:
below $q = 4$ the geometry is a surface (2-
dimensional), above it the geometry collapses to a
tree (1-dimensional). This is analogous to the
**BKP** (Bardhan–Kane–Prahladakumar)
transition in polymer physics. The **Liouville
Brownian motion** is the canonical diffusion process
on the LQG surface, defined via the **Liouville
measure** (a random measure arising from the
Gaussian free field). The seven manuscripts attack
the transition from complementary directions: the FK–
Ising walk limit, the spectral convergence, the
linear clock for tree-weighted maps, the subcritical
conformal limits, the critical sphere at $q = 4$,
the FK and spanning-tree metric-measure limits, and
the Brownian CRT limits for $q > 4$ [S3:55–63,
S4:44–49, S2:507].

## consequences

**The surface-to-tree transition is proved.**
Critical FK planar maps converge to LQG spheres for
$q \leq 4$ and to the Brownian CRT for $q > 4$
[S2:507].

**The FK–Ising walk limit is proved.** Stationary
random walks on critical FK–Ising maps converge
to Liouville Brownian motion [S3:55–63, S2:507].

**The spectral convergence is proved.** Eigenvalues
and heat traces of the FK–Ising Laplacian
converge to those of the Liouville Brownian motion
[S2:507].

**The Brownian CRT limit holds through all sizes.**
The convergence for $q > 4$ is not restricted to a
subsequence [S4:44–49, S5:9].
