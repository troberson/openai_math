## undergraduate

Imagine you want to cover a shape in high-dimensional space with as few
"balls" (translates of a fixed shape) as possible. The **covering
number** records how many you need. **Metric entropy** is the log of
that number — a measure of the shape's "complexity."

**Pietsch's duality conjecture** proposed a deep symmetry: the
complexity of a convex body should be comparable (up to universal
constants) to the complexity of its **polar** (a kind of "dual" shape
built from all the hyperplanes touching it). If true, you could bound
the covering numbers of a complicated body by looking at its polar
instead — a powerful dimension-free tool.

This family **disproves** the conjecture. For every proposed pair of
universal constants, the authors build an origin-symmetric convex body
whose complexity and its polar's complexity disagree by an arbitrarily
large factor — even when the covering body is the simplest one (a cube).
A further construction makes the ratio between the two complexities
shrink toward zero as the dimension grows, so no universal constant can
fix the gap.

## graduate

**Main theorem (formalized)** [S3:9–14, S2:756, S4:9].
For every $`a, b \ge 1`$, there exist a positive integer $`n`$ and an
origin-symmetric convex body $`K`$ in a positive finite dimension such
that
$`\log N(K, B_\infty) > b \cdot \log N(B_\infty^\circ, a^{-1} K^\circ)`$,
where $`N`$ is the least number of translates in a finite cover,
$`B_\infty`$ is the unit cube, and $`K^\circ`$ is the polar of $`K`$.
In other words, the conjectured dimension-free comparison between the
covering numbers of a body and its polar fails for every proposed pair
of universal constants.

**The further construction** [S4:9].
The scope note records a second, stronger construction: one in which the
dual-to-primal logarithmic entropy ratio tends to zero as the dimension
grows. This shows the failure is not merely a constant-factor issue —
the two entropies can be made arbitrarily far apart in the limit.

**What the counterexample body looks like** [S3:9–14, S5].
The comparator stub states the theorem over the finite-dimensional
real space `RealSpace (Fin n)`, with a `cube` (the unit cube
$`[-1,1]^n`$), a `polar` predicate (sets defined by pairing ≤ 1), a

`IsSymmetricConvexBody` structure (compact, convex, origin-symmetric,
with nonempty interior). The counterexample body is required to be
coverable by the cube, the polar of the cube coverable by a scaled
polar of $`K`$, and the logarithmic entropy inequality reversed.

## history

**Pietsch's dimension-free duality conjecture** for metric entropy is
a classical open problem in the geometry of numbers and functional
analysis. Metric entropy — the log of the covering number — is a
fundamental invariant in approximation theory, probability in Banach
spaces, and information geometry. A duality between a convex body and
its polar would have been a dimension-free tool: it would let estimates
for a body be transferred to its polar (and back) with only universal
constants of loss.

The conjecture was proposed in connection with **Pietsch's
factorization theorem** and the theory of **entropic widths** of convex
bodies. Positive partial results existed for special classes (polytopes
with bounded complexity, bodies close to ellipsoids), and the
conjecture was used heuristically in several asymptotic Banach-space
estimates.

The present result is a **dimension-free disproof**: a single
explicit family of origin-symmetric bodies, parametrized by dimension,
violates the comparison for every pair of universal constants, with a
further construction killing the ratio in the limit. The construction
uses a **matrix-contract / compression / partition** machinery (the
manuscript's section titles) to build a cube whose polar has
drastically different covering complexity.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Pietsch's factorization
and the geometry of polar bodies are classical. The specific
dimension-free counterexample family, with the cube-covering-body
reduction and the vanishing-ratio limit construction, is the novel
contribution claimed here; I cannot verify the matrix-contract /
compression / partition argument independently from training alone.

## consequences

- **Pietsch's metric-entropy duality conjecture is disproved**
  [S3:9–14, S2:756, S4:9]: for every pair of universal constants
  $`a, b \ge 1`$, there is a finite-dimensional origin-symmetric convex
  body $`K`$ with
  $`\log N(K, B_\infty) > b \log N(B_\infty^\circ, a^{-1}K^\circ)`$.
  This is the named counterexample the ledger's `counterex_broad` flag
  records.
- **The failure holds even when the covering body is a cube**
  [S3:9–14, S4:9]: the simplest possible covering body (the Euclidean-
  infinity cube) already exhibits the violation, so the conjecture is
  not rescued by restricting the cover type.
- **A vanishing-ratio limit construction** [S4:9]: a further family of
  bodies makes the dual-to-primal logarithmic entropy ratio tend to
  zero as the dimension grows, ruling out any uniform comparison in
  the asymptotic regime.
- **No dimension-free estimate is possible** [S3:9–14, S2:756, S4:9]:
  the disproof shows that any estimate purporting to compare the
  covering numbers of a body and its polar with dimension-independent
  constants is false.
