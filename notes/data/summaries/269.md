## undergraduate

The **Laughlin state** is the idealized "quantum fluid" of a
two-dimensional gas of electrons in a strong magnetic field at a
particular filling (one-third, here) — a **Fermi
liquid** whose particles repel each other so strongly that they
avoid one another. The **spectral gap** is again the minimum energy
you must supply to create an excitation above the ground state. The
**Laughlin gap conjecture** says this gap stays **uniformly positive**
as the system grows — the fluid stays "rigid" at every scale. The
first manuscript [S4:89–97] proves the gap directly (at least
$1/25$ for large systems) and, more generally, a **Fock-space
inequality** that makes the gap uniform in the particle number. The
second [S3:27–37] goes one step further: it shows the
condensate **survives** a weak, bounded, "classical" (scalar) disorder
field projected to the flat band, so the unique gap survives realistic
perturbations.
