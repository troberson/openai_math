## contents

[S1:8674] (family entry):

> **357. Bi-Lipschitz coordinates at every regular RCD point.** Proves that every regular point of a noncollapsed $\mathop{\mathrm{RCD}}\nolimits (K,n)$ space, for <i>K</i> ∈ ℝ and integer <i>n</i> ≥ 2, has an open neighborhood bi-Lipschitz to an open subset of ℝ<sup><i>n</i></sup>. Regularity requires all pointed tangents to be Euclidean, the reference measure is exactly $\mathcal H^n$, and the chart compares ambient distances with a point-dependent finite constant.

The family has **no** formalization in the release [S4].

## undergraduate

Take a space that is "roughly" a smooth Euclidean space but with no
smooth structure to look at. The question is: at the *nice* points —
the points where, if you zoom in hard enough, the space looks exactly
like flat $\mathbb R^n$ — can you put down an actual coordinate
chart, one that doesn't distort distances by more than a fixed
factor?

This is the **regular-point bi-Lipschitz conjecture**. The result
here says **yes**, in the *noncollapsed* setting (where the natural
volume measure is the right one, $\mathcal H^n$): every regular point
of a $\operatorname{RCD}(K,n)$ space has a little open neighborhood
that is **bi-Lipschitz** to a piece of $\mathbb R^n$. "Bi-Lipschitz"
means distances up to a fixed multiplicative constant — the chart is
a homeomorphism that neither stretches nor compresses by more than
that constant. The constant depends only on the dimension $n$, and
it compares the space's own ambient distance, so the chart is honest
about the geometry it lives in.

## graduate

**Main theorem** [S3:44–49, S2:814].
For every integer $`n \ge 2`$ and every real $`K`$, every regular
point of a noncollapsed $`\operatorname{RCD}(K,n)`$ space has an
open neighborhood bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic to an open subset of
$`\mathbb R^n`$. The bi-Lipschitz constant depends only on $`n`$,
and the neighborhood is measured with the restricted ambient
distance [S1:8674].

**What "regular" means here** [S1:8674].
Regularity is the **pointed-tangent** condition: every tangent cone
at the point (in the pointed/Gromov–Hausdorff sense) must be
Euclidean. The reference measure is exactly the $`n`$-dimensional
Hausdorff measure $`\mathcal H^n`$ — the noncollapsed part. The chart
is a comparison of the ambient distance in the space with the
Euclidean distance in `R^n`'s chart, with a finite constant that may
depend on the point but the bi-Lipschitz constant itself is
dimensional [S2:814].

**The neighborhood is local and distance-restricted** [S1:8674].
The chart uses the *restricted* ambient distance — the distance
measured within the neighborhood, not a global re-metrization — so
the bi-Lipschitz statement is a local geometric one, point by point.

## history

The **regular-point structure** of Ricci-lower-bounded metric
measure spaces is a central program in metric geometry, developing the
smooth-side regularity theory (the Cheeger–Naber type results) into
the synthetic $\operatorname{RCD}$ setting. The **bi-Lipschitz**
strong form — a genuine chart, not just a measure-theoretic
rectifiability statement — is the load-bearing upgrade over the
classical results, which give $L^p$-approximation or rectifiability
but not an honest invertible map with a controlled Lipschitz
constant.

The **novel contribution** is the **noncollapsed** resolution: with the
reference measure pinned to $`\mathcal H^n`$ and regularity read as
pointed-tangent Euclideanity [S3], every regular point carries a
bi-Lipschitz chart [S2:814]. The dimensional dependence of the
constant and the use of the restricted ambient distance are the
precision of the statement [S1:8674].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** As of my training cutoff,
the regular-point rectifiability and tangent-cone structure of
$\operatorname{RCD}(K,n)$ spaces were well studied, and a genuine
**bi-Lipschitz** chart at *every* regular point of the *noncollapsed*
space was a strong structural claim at the frontier of the synthetic
theory. I cannot verify the metric-geometric analysis independently
from training alone.

## consequences

- **Every regular point of a noncollapsed $\operatorname{RCD}(K,n)$
  space is bi-Lipschitz to a piece of $\mathbb R^n$**
  [S3:44–49, S2:814]: the regular-point bi-Lipschitz conjecture is
  resolved in the noncollapsed setting.
- **The bi-Lipschitz constant is dimensional** [S1:8674, S2:814]: it
  depends only on $n$, not on the particular space or point.
- **The chart is local and distance-restricted** [S1:8674]: the
  bi-Lipschitz statement compares the restricted ambient distance
  within the neighborhood, a point-by-point local geometry.
- **Regularity is pointed-tangent Euclideanity with
  $\mathcal H^n$ reference measure** [S1:8674]: the noncollapsed
  hypothesis (measure exactly $\mathcal H^n$) and the
  pointed-tangent condition are the exact hypotheses under which the
  chart exists.
