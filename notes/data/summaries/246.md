## undergraduate

A **hyperbolic group** is a group whose Cayley graph has a special
geometric property: any two paths connecting the same endpoints
fork into a "thin triangle" (the third side stays close to the
other two). The **boundary** of a hyperbolic group is a topological
space attached to it at "infinity". **Cannon's conjecture**
(1981) asks: if the boundary is a two-sphere, does the group
act "nicely" (properly and cocompactly) on three-dimensional
hyperbolic space? The answer is **yes** — every such
group admits a proper cocompact isometric action on
$\mathbb H^3$ with finite kernel
[S3:70–73, S2:581].

## history

**Cannon's conjecture** (1981) was one of the central open
problems at the intersection of geometric group theory and
three-manifold topology. It asked whether the boundary
condition $\partial G \cong S^2$ characterizes the groups
acting geometrically on $\mathbb H^3$. The conjecture was
resolved for specific classes (e.g. surface groups, free
products) but remained open in general. The result
[S3:70–73] proves the full conjecture using a
**modulus**-based proof technique, establishing the
geometric action directly from the hyperbolicity and
boundary assumptions.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Cannon's
conjecture, hyperbolic groups, visual boundaries, and the
geometric group theory framework are standard. The full
resolution of Cannon's conjecture for all word-hyperbolic
groups with $S^2$ boundary, via a modulus proof, is the
novel contribution. I cannot confirm this independently
from training alone.

## consequences

- **Cannon's conjecture resolved**
  [S3:70–73]: every word-hyperbolic group with
  boundary homeomorphic to $S^2$ admits a proper cocompact
  isometric action on $\mathbb H^3$ with finite kernel.
- **The three-manifold consequence**
  [S3:70–73]: every torsion-free word-hyperbolic
  group with $S^2$ boundary is the fundamental group of
  a closed hyperbolic three-manifold.
