## undergraduate

A **Bose–Einstein condensate** forms when a gas of bosons
(particles that can all pile into the same quantum state) is cooled
until a macroscopic fraction collapses into the lowest energy level
— all moving in lockstep. The **hard-sphere gas** is the simplest
model: the particles are tiny solid balls that cannot overlap, so their
only interaction is the volume they exclude. The
**condensate fraction** is how many particles sit in that lowest level.
The first manuscript [S3:59–66] proves that at a **positive
temperature** (not just absolute zero), a dilute hard-sphere gas still
condenses. The next two [S4:41–51, S5:50–61] turn to
**quantum depletion**: at absolute zero, even a perfect condensate has a
small fraction of particles kicked out of the lowest level by interactions
— the Bogoliubov law says that fraction grows like the square root of
the gas density times the particle size. The fourth
[S6:39–46] gives a condensate bound that is **uniform in density**;
the fifth [S7:59–65] is the **zero-temperature** (ground-state)
condensation result.

## graduate

**Positive-temperature condensation**
[S3:59–66, S2:625].
For each fixed exclusion distance $a>0$ and every sufficiently small
fixed density $ ho$, there is a strictly positive temperature $T_0$,
independent of the volume, such that the **exact canonical Gibbs state**
of the three-dimensional dilute hard-sphere Bose gas at temperature
$T_0$ has a positive condensate fraction in the thermodynamic limit,
with the condensate occupying the constant orbital [S3:59–66].

**Hard-sphere momentum distribution and depletion** [S4:41–51, S2:625].
For the three-dimensional hard-sphere Bose gas at zero temperature, the
scaled Bogoliubov momentum distribution of the depleted particles is
determined in full: the thermodynamic limit at each fixed density $ ho$
is taken before the dilute limit, uniformly over pure and mixed ground
states, and every thermodynamic accumulation value of the nonzero-
momentum occupation measure has total mass $8/(3\sqrt{\pi})$, giving the
depletion fraction $(8/(3\sqrt{\pi}))\sqrt{\rho
a^3} +
o(\sqrt{\rho a^3})$.

**Repulsive-potential depletion** [S5:50–61, S2:625]. For a fixed
bounded, nonnegative, radial interaction of finite range and positive
scattering length $a$, every thermodynamic accumulation value of the
ground-state fraction outside the constant mode is
$(8/(3\sqrt{\pi}))\sqrt{\rho
a^3} +
o(\sqrt{\rho a^3})$
as
$\rho \downarrow 0$,
the limit taken at each fixed density before the dilute limit, uniformly
over ground-state density matrices.

**Density-uniform condensate bound** [S6:39–46, S2:625]. For each
bounded, measurable, nonnegative, radial finite-range interaction in
three dimensions not zero almost everywhere, there is a positive lower
bound on the constant-orbital condensate fraction, uniform over all
sufficiently small densities and all temperatures between zero and the
square of the density.

**Ground-state condensation (formalization source)** [S7:59–65, S8:9].
There are absolute constants
$\varepsilon_0, c_0 > 0$
such that, whenever the density $ ho$ and hard-sphere radius $a$ satisfy
$\rho a^3 < \varepsilon_0$,
the condensate occupation fraction has limit inferior at least $c_0$
along every thermodynamic sequence with
$N/L^3 \to \rho$
[S8:9].

## history

The **Bose–Einstein condensation** of dilute gases had been proved at
**zero temperature** for various interactions (the ground-state hard-
sphere result traces to the programs of Lieb, Seiringer, Solovej, and
Yngvason, and to the later sharp hard-sphere work). The **positive-
temperature** version — that the condensation survives at a strictly
positive temperature for the **exact canonical Gibbs state**, not just
in the grand-canonical thermodynamic limit — and the **full scaled
Bogoliubov momentum distribution** for the depleted particles had
remained open for the hard-sphere gas. The first manuscript [S3:59–66]
resolves the positive-temperature condensation for the exact canonical
state; the second [S4:41–51] determines the full hard-sphere depletion
momentum distribution; the third [S5:50–61] extends the depletion law to
general bounded repulsive potentials; the fourth [S6:39–46] gives the
density-uniform condensate bound; the fifth [S7:59–65] is the zero-
temperature ground-state condensation that the formalization targets
[S8:9].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Bose–Einstein condensation,
hard-sphere gases, the Bogoliubov depletion law, and the Lieb–Seiringer
framework are standard in rigorous statistical mechanics. The positive-
temperature condensation for the exact canonical Gibbs state, the full
hard-sphere scaled Bogoliubov momentum distribution with the
$8/(3\sqrt{\pi})\sqrt{\rho a^3}$
depletion, its extension to general bounded repulsive potentials, and
the density-uniform condensate bound are the novel contributions. I
cannot confirm these independently from training alone.
