## contents

> **243. Separating choiceless counting from polynomial time and witnessed choice.** Confirms the Blass–Gurevich–Shelah noncapture conjecture: consistency of a linear system over $\mathbb F_3$ defines a polynomial-time query on unordered finite structures that choiceless polynomial time with counting cannot express. A separate result shows that adding witnessed symmetric choice strictly increases expressive power. Both separations hold for the full counting formalism, allowing hereditarily finite sets of arbitrary finite rank. ([Lean](../../../lean/docs/243.md))

## undergraduate

**Polynomial time** is the class of problems a computer can solve
in time proportional to a polynomial in the input size. A
**logic** captures a complexity class if the logic's definable
queries are exactly the problems in that class. **Choiceless
polynomial time with counting** (CPT) is a logic for unordered
finite structures that extends basic polynomial time with a
counting quantifier. The **Blass–Gurevich–Shelah
conjecture** (1990s) predicted that CPT does **not**
capture polynomial time — there exist polynomial-time
queries that CPT cannot express. The first manuscript
confirms this conjecture with an explicit counterexample:
a linear-consistency query over $\mathbb F_3$
[S3:36–40, S2:573]. The second manuscript
shows that adding **witnessed symmetric choice** to CPT
strictly increases its expressive power
[S4:65–68, S2:573].
