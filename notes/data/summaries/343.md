## undergraduate

In **symplectic geometry**, a branch of geometry that studies
"phase spaces" from physics, there is a kind of rigidity that Euclidean
geometry lacks. You cannot freely squash and stretch a symplectic
space the way you can a rubber sheet — there are **symplectic
invariants** that are preserved.

A **symplectic ball** is a ball in this phase-space geometry. The
**ball-packing problem** asks: how many balls of given sizes can you
fit inside a bigger ball without them overlapping? In the symplectic
world, the answer is not just "do the volumes fit" — there is an extra
constraint.

The **Siegel–Yao conjecture** proposed the exact condition: a
collection of closed symplectic balls of capacities $R_1,\ldots,R_k$
fits inside a ball of capacity $R$ if and only if (1) the sum of their
$n$th powers is less than $R^n$ (a volume condition), and (2) no pair
of balls has capacities summing to more than $R$ (a pairwise
condition). The answer here is **yes** — in every dimension $2n
\ge 6$.

## graduate

**Main theorem (formalized in part)** [S3:26–36, S2:786, S4:9].
For all integers $`n \ge 3`$ and $`k \ge 1`$ and all positive real
capacities $`R_1, \ldots, R_k`$, the closed standard symplectic
$`2n`$-balls of these capacities embed disjointly into the interior of
a ball of capacity $`R > 0`$ if and only if
$`\sum_{i=1}^k R_i^n < R^n`$ and $`R_i + R_j < R`$ for all
$`i \ne j`$. Capacity is $`\pi`$ times the squared Euclidean radius,
and each embedding is defined on a neighborhood of its closed source
ball. This proves Siegel and Yao's Conjecture A.

**The two formalized statements** [S4:9, S5, S6].

full equivalence: disjoint symplectic embeddings exist **exactly
when** the volume and pairwise capacity inequalities hold. The second
[S4:9] is the **necessity** direction: if disjoint embeddings exist,
then the inequalities must hold. The two comparator stubs correspond:

## history

The **Siegel–Yao ball-packing conjecture** (Conjecture A) is a
central conjecture in higher-dimensional symplectic topology,
proposed by Siegel and Yao in the 2010s–2020s. It asks for a sharp
criterion for when a collection of symplectic balls fits inside a
larger ball, in dimensions $`2n \ge 6`$.

The problem is significant because symplectic ball packing is one of
the key "rigidity" phenomena in symplectic geometry: unlike Euclidean
geometry, where ball packing is governed by volume alone, symplectic
packing has extra obstructions. The **Gromov non-squeezing theorem**
(1985) established the first such obstruction in dimension 4. The
higher-dimensional case ($`2n \ge 6`$) was the open problem.

The present result resolves the conjecture in every dimension
$`2n \ge 6`$ with the criterion being the conjunction of a volume
condition ($`\sum R_i^n < R^n`$) and a pairwise condition
($`R_i + R_j < R`$ for all $`i \ne j`$). The proof draws on
symplectic comparison techniques, surface constructions, and a
six-dimensional reduction (the manuscript's section titles).

## consequences

- **Siegel and Yao's Conjecture A is resolved in every dimension
  $`2n \ge 6`$** [S3:26–36, S2:786, S4:9]: the exact ball-packing
  criterion is the volume condition plus the pairwise capacity
  condition.
- **The criterion is an iff, not just a sufficient condition**
  [S4:9, S5, S6]: both the sufficiency (the inequalities imply
  packability) and the necessity (packability implies the
  inequalities) are proved.
- **Arbitrary capacities** [S3:26–36]: the criterion holds for
  positive real capacities, not just special discrete values.
- **Embeddings on a neighborhood** [S4:9]: each embedding is
  defined on a neighborhood of its closed source ball, a
  regularity condition stronger than pointwise embedding.
