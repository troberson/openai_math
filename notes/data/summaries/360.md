## undergraduate

On a smooth curved surface, the shortest paths (geodesics) can
"focus" — nearby paths can come back together. The points where
they meet again are **conjugate points**, and the region where they
don't focus is the **injectivity domain**. For spaces with good
enough curvature (a condition called **MTW curvature**, after
Ma–Trudinger–Wang), the question is: are those injectivity domains
**convex**? That is, if you take any two nearby points in the
domain, is the shortest path between them also in the domain?

**Villani's conjecture** says yes, under the weak MTW curvature
condition. The result here proves it, in every dimension at least
two, for every closed connected smooth manifold: weak MTW curvature
implies convexity of every tangent injectivity domain.

As a bonus, the same curvature condition gives **regularity** of
optimal transport: if you move one probability distribution to
another using the "squared distance" cost, the optimal map and its
inverse are both **Hölder continuous** (a strong smoothness
condition, weaker than differentiability but stronger than mere
continuity), with constants that work uniformly across the whole
class of allowed source and target densities.

## graduate

**Theorem 1 (convexity, manuscript 1)** [S3:48–55, S5:10].
On a compact connected smooth Riemannian manifold $`M`$ of dimension
$`n \ge 2`$ satisfying weak MTW curvature, every open tangent
injectivity domain is convex. Prior convexity or nonfocality is not
assumed [S5:10]. Additional formalized results give global support
at ordinary subgradients, compact convex lifted gap sections with
the stated diameter bound, and scale and local-injectivity estimates
for finite target families; the finite-family estimate does not cover
the paper's arbitrary-potential local-injectivity assertion [S5:10].

**Theorem 2 (bi-Hölder transport, manuscript 2)** [S4:43–50, S5:12].
For squared-distance transport on a compact connected smooth
Riemannian manifold $`M`$ of dimension $`n \ge 2`$ satisfying weak
MTW, between probability densities $`\mu, \nu`$ with
$`c_0 \le d\mu/d\mathrm{vol}, d\nu/d\mathrm{vol} \le C_0`$,
the optimal map $`T_\mu^\nu`$ and its inverse $`(T_\mu^\nu)^{-1}`$
satisfy a global Hölder bound $`|x - y|^{\alpha} \le C |T(x) - T(y)|`$
with $`\alpha, C`$ uniform over the density class on the fixed
manifold, not over varying metrics [S5:12]. The optimal map is a
homeomorphism, unique a.e., and both it and its inverse satisfy the
global bound [S5:12].

## history

The **Ma–Trudinger–Wang (MTW) curvature** condition is the
Riemannian curvature hypothesis under which the squared-distance
cost in optimal transport is **regular** — the optimal map is smooth
and the transport problem is well-posed without conjugate points.
The **strong** MTW condition (all sectional curvatures nonpositive)
is classical; the **weak** MTW condition [S3] relaxes it to a
specific inequality involving the Riemann curvature tensor that
allows some positive sectional curvatures while preserving the key
convexity and regularity properties.

**Villani's conjecture** posits that weak MTW curvature suffices for
convexity of the tangent injectivity domains — a condition that, in
the classical strong-MTW setting, follows from the nonfocality of
geodesics. The **novel contribution** is the proof under the *weak*
hypothesis alone [S3:48–55]: no prior convexity or nonfocality is
assumed [S5:10].

The **regularity consequence** (manuscript 2) is that the same weak
MTW curvature gives a **uniform** bi-Hölder bound on the optimal
transport map [S4:43–50]: the exponent and constant depend only on
the manifold's geometry and the density bounds, not on the specific
densities — a uniformity stronger than the classical pointwise
Hölder estimates.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** As of my training
cutoff, the convexity of injectivity domains under weak MTW
curvature was Villani's conjecture, stated in his 2009 book on
optimal transport. It was open in the form presented here — the
strong-MTW case is classical, but the weak-MTW reduction to
convexity was the claimed novelty. The uniform bi-Hölder transport
result is also consistent with the frontier of the regular
transport theory at my cutoff. I cannot verify the
Riemannian-geometric analysis independently from training alone.

## consequences

- **Villani's convexity conjecture is resolved under weak MTW
  curvature** [S3:48–55, S5:10]: on every closed connected smooth
  Riemannian manifold of dimension at least two, weak MTW curvature
  implies convexity of every open tangent injectivity domain, with
  no prior convexity or nonfocality assumed.
- **Uniform bi-Hölder regularity of optimal transport**
  [S4:43–50, S5:12]: under weak MTW curvature, the squared-distance
  optimal map and its inverse are globally Hölder continuous with
  constants uniform over the bounded density class on the fixed
  manifold.
- **The constants are dimensional and manifold-geometric, not
  density-specific** [S5:12]: the Hölder exponent and constant work
  for every pair of densities in the class, so the regularity is a
  property of the manifold's curvature, not of the particular
  transport problem.
- **The finite-family local-injectivity estimate has a stated gap**
  [S5:10]: the formalized result covers finite target families but
  does not cover the paper's arbitrary-potential
  local-injectivity assertion.
