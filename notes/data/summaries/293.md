## undergraduate

An operator $T$ on a Hilbert space has an **invariant subspace** if there is
a closed subspace that $T$ maps into itself. The **hyperinvariant-subspace
problem** asks something stronger: does every bounded operator have a
nontrivial closed subspace that is invariant under *every operator that
commutes with it*? Such a subspace is "hyperinvariant" — it is preserved not
just by $T$ but by the whole algebra of operators that get on with $T$
(the **commutant** of $T$).

This problem had resisted a solution for decades. The answer turns out to be
**no**, and these two papers give the explicit counterexample.

**Manuscript 2** [S4:33–39] is the main event: on *every* infinite-
dimensional separable complex Hilbert space, it builds a nonzero
**quasinilpotent** operator (one whose spectrum is just $\{0\}$) whose
commutant is **transitive** — the only closed subspaces left invariant by
*every* operator in the commutant are $0$ and the whole space. So the
operator has no nontrivial closed hyperinvariant subspace. The
commutant is moreover a proper, strongly-closed, unital operator algebra.

**Manuscript 1** [S3:41–48] is the spatial-companion result: for every
irrational rotation angle, it constructs a weighted rotation operator of the
same kind (nonzero, norm-quasinilpotent) that has **no nontrivial invariant
projection** inside the hyperfinite type II$_1$ factor. This answers a
question of Zhu, Fang, and Shi with the angle prescribed in advance.

## graduate

**Main theorem (Manuscript 2, formalized)** [S4:33–39, S2:680].
On every infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space $H$ there is a
nonzero bounded norm-quasinilpotent operator $T \in B(H)$ with **no
nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace**. Equivalently, its
commutant $T' = \{ X \in B(H) : XT = TX\}$ is a **transitive** operator
algebra: the closed subspaces invariant under every $X \in T'$ are exactly
$0$ and $H$. The commutant is proper, unital, and closed in the strong
operator topology. This is a negative answer to the hyperinvariant-subspace
problem in full generality over the separable infinite-dimensional setting.

**The invariant-projection companion (Manuscript 1)** [S3:41–48].
For every irrational $\theta \in (0,1)$, the construction yields a
continuous nonnegative circle weight $w$ with a single zero and logarithmic
integral $-\infty$, so that the weighted rotation $T_w$ on the associated
hyperfinite type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor is nonzero, norm-quasinilpotent, and
has no nontrivial invariant projection: for any projection $p$ in the
factor, $(1-p) T_w p = 0$ forces $p = 0$ or $p = 1$. This answers the
Zhu–Fang–Shi question negatively with the rotation angle prescribed
in advance.

**Why quasinilpotent?** Both operators have spectrum $\{0\}$, so they
cannot be diagonalized, and their dynamics are governed by the off-diagonal
structure rather than point spectrum. This is what makes the commutant
transitive: there is no spectral decomposition to exploit for finding an
invariant subspace.

## history

The hyperinvariant-subspace problem (also called the **invariant-subspace
problem for the commutant**) sits at the boundary of operator theory and
functional analysis. The classical invariant-subspace problem asks whether
every operator on a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space has a
nontrivial invariant subspace; that problem was answered negatively for
separable Hilbert spaces by a nonconstructive argument. The
*hyperinvariant* version is stronger: it asks whether the commutant
algebra alone already forces an invariant subspace.

The negative answer here is explicit. Manuscript 2 [S4:33–39] constructs
the operator directly on every separable infinite-dimensional $H$, and
manuscript 1 [S3:41–48] gives the spatial refinement inside the hyperfinite
factor. The release formalizes manuscript 2's statement as the
`main_results` target (`OAI.BackwardIntertwiners.direct_algebra_corollary`),
with the full 23-file OAI tree under
`lean/OAI/Analysis/BackwardIntertwiners/`.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** The hyperinvariant-subspace
problem is a classical open problem in operator theory, closely related to
the unilateral shift and to the Brown measure of normal operators in finite
factors. The explicit quasinilpotent counterexample with a transitive
commutant, and the irrational-rotation weighted-shift refinement, are the
novel contributions claimed here; I cannot verify the functional-analytic
constructions independently from training alone.

## consequences

- **The hyperinvariant-subspace problem is answered negatively** [S4:33–39,
  S2:680]: on every infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space
  there is a nonzero bounded norm-quasinilpotent operator with no nonzero
  proper closed hyperinvariant subspace. This is the named resolution the
  ledger's `resolves_named` flag records.
- **A transitive commutant is realizable** [S4:33–39]: the commutant of the
  constructed operator is a proper, unital, strongly-closed, transitive
  operator algebra — an explicit example of such an algebra.
- **The irrational-rotation invariant-projection result** [S3:41–48]: for
  every irrational angle $\theta$, a weighted rotation in the hyperfinite
  type $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor has no nontrivial invariant projection,
  answering the Zhu–Fang–Shi question with $\theta$ prescribed.
- **A Brown measure $\delta_0$** [S5:10]: the selected product weighted
  shift has Brown measure $\delta_0$, confirming the quasinilpotent nature
  at the level of the finite-factor invariant.
