## undergraduate

Suppose a group acts on a probability space (a way of "moving" points
around while preserving a notion of "how much space" each region has).
The **cost** of such an action is a number measuring, roughly, "how
many moves on average" it takes to wander through the space —
the cost of the most efficient way to cover it. The **fixed-price
problem**, posed by Gaboriau, asked whether each group has a single
"fixed" cost that every one of its nice (essentially free) actions
must share. The answer is **no** — the result constructs a
finitely generated group with two essentially free actions of
**different** costs: its Bernoulli (random) action has cost bounded
away from one, while a sequence of "finite height extension" actions
has costs tending to one [S3:25–30, S2:607].
