## undergraduate

A **scalar curvature** condition says the total curvature at every
point of a manifold is at least some threshold. A **spectral scalar
curvature** condition is weaker: it says the *operator* $-4\Delta +
\text{Scal}$ is non-negative, where $\Delta$ is the Laplacian. A
pointwise condition requires the scalar curvature to be positive at
every single point; a spectral condition allows the scalar curvature
to dip negative somewhere, as long as the "oscillatory" part (the
Laplacian) compensates.

**Urysohn width** (or **macroscopic dimension**) measures how close a
space is to having small dimension. A space has Urysohn width at most
$C$ in dimension $d$ if there is a continuous map to a $d$-dimensional
complex such that every fiber (the set of points mapping to the same
target point) has diameter at most $C$. Gromov's conjecture says:
positive scalar curvature forces the macroscopic dimension to drop by
2 — an $n$-manifold with positive scalar curvature has Urysohn width
at most $n-2$.

The three manuscripts in this family attack the conjecture from three
directions. Manuscript 1 [S3] extends the conclusion from a
*pointwise* scalar-curvature bound to a *spectral* one (weaker
hypothesis, same conclusion, for $n\ge4$). Manuscript 2 [S4] handles
the dimension-three case with a different, more geometric
construction. Manuscript 3 [S5] proves the pointwise version in every
dimension $n\ge4$ and also gets the universal-cover version for
*$n\ge2$*.

## graduate

**Main theorem (manuscript 3)** [S5:68–77, S2:772].
Every complete connected smooth boundaryless $n$-manifold with scalar
curvature at least one admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex
of dimension at most $n-2$ whose entire fibers have diameter bounded
only in terms of $n$. The bound is measured in the original metric.
Additionally, the universal covers of closed manifolds with positive
scalar curvature have continuous macroscopic dimension at most $n-2$
for every $n\ge2$.

**The spectral extension (manuscript 1)** [S3:42–50, S2:772].
For every $n\ge4$, a complete connected smooth Riemannian $n$-manifold
without boundary satisfying $-4\Delta+\text{Scal}\ge1$ as a
quadratic-form inequality admits a continuous map to a simplicial
complex of dimension at most $n-2$ whose entire fibers have diameter
bounded only in terms of $n$. The bound is measured in the original
metric. This extends the uniform Urysohn width theorem from a
pointwise scalar-curvature lower bound to a spectral lower bound.

**The dimension-three case (manuscript 2)** [S4, S2:772].
The third manuscript [S4] handles the dimension-three case with a
distinct construction (weighted surfaces, cutting, doubling bubbles,
slabs, graph gluing). No abstract is shipped; the title states the
scope: "Spectral scalar curvature and Urysohn width in dimension
three."

## history

**Gromov's scalar-curvature and width conjecture** is one of the
central open problems in geometric measure theory. It asks: does
positive scalar curvature force the **macroscopic dimension**
(Urysohn width, Lebesgue covering dimension of the "large-scale"
geometry) to drop by 2? Equivalently: does an $n$-manifold with
$\text{Scal}\ge1$ admit a continuous map to an $(n-2)$-dimensional
complex with fibers of uniformly bounded diameter?

The conjecture is significant because scalar curvature is a
*local* quantity while Urysohn width is a *global* topological
invariant. A local geometric condition forcing a global dimensional
drop would be a deep rigidity statement. The conjecture is known in
various special cases (3-manifolds, 4-manifolds under additional
hypotheses) but was open in general.

The three manuscripts attack the conjecture from complementary
directions: the pointwise bound in general dimensions [S5], the
spectral (weaker) bound in general dimensions [S3], and the
dimension-three case [S4]. Together they cover the conjecture in its
pointwise form for $n\ge4$ [S5], its spectral form for $n\ge4$ [S3],
and the $n=3$ case [S4].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Gromov's width
conjecture is a genuine open problem in geometric measure theory as
of my training cutoff. The specific constructions (pressure
arguments, stretching rounds, finite cuts, countable partitions) used
in manuscripts 1 and 3, and the dimension-three cut-and-glow

## consequences

- **Gromov's scalar-curvature width conjecture is resolved in
  general dimensions** [S5:68–77, S2:772]: the pointwise form
  ($\text{Scal}\ge1$) holds for every $n\ge4$.
- **The spectral form is also resolved** [S3:42–50, S2:772]: the
  weaker spectral hypothesis $-4\Delta+\text{Scal}\ge1$ suffices for
  the same codimension-two width conclusion, for $n\ge4$.
- **The dimension-three case is covered** [S4, S2:772]: a
  dimension-specific construction handles $n=3$.
- **Universal covers of closed positive-scalar-curvature manifolds
  have low macroscopic dimension** [S5:68–77]: for every $n\ge2$,
  the universal cover has continuous macroscopic dimension at most
  $n-2$.
- **The bound is uniform in the metric** [S3:42–50, S5:68–77]: the
  fiber diameter bound depends only on $n$, not on the specific
  metric — a "uniform" conclusion.
