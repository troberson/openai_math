## undergraduate

Paving asks how small the pinching of an operator can be made after subtracting a diagonal element [S15:7]. Norm paving requires the difference itself to be small in operator norm, and "{{7}}" [S15:7] The strong-operator formulation relaxes this: the pinching is small only after compression by a projection whose complement is arbitrarily small in the strong topology.

The definition fixes the number of projections before the test vectors and the neighborhood are chosen: "{{8}}" [S15:10] The projection $q$ compresses the pinching, and $\|q(S_P(x)-a)q\|\le\eps\|x\|$ is the bound [S15:11–14]. "{{9}}" [S15:15]

The main theorem gives a quadratic bound: "{{10}}" [S15:25] The partition count $r_\eps$ is at most $5\times10^8\eps^{-2}$ [S15:19–24]. "{{11}}" [S15:28]

The companion manuscript proves a different paving property for the same class of masas. It norm-paves a nearby bounded operator $y$ with $\|y\|\le3\|x\|$, with error relative to $\|y\|$'s norm [S6:38–57]. "{{17}}" [S6:39] The partition count is $C\varepsilon^{-6}$, a universal sixth power. "{{18}}" [S6:60–61] "{{19}}" the approximant's norm; a clipping argument ensures the normalization [S6:62–66]. The two results are independent, and the companion claims no quadratic rate: "{{20}}" quadratic strong-operator paving bound for the original operator. {{21}} [S6:88–93]

## graduate

**Manuscript B (quadratic strong-operator paving).** The proof reduces to a faithful central state and decomposes the operator into a Cartan term (normalizer algebra) and a kernel term (atomless kernels) [S16] [S17]. The Cartan term is treated on a relative Bernoulli extension of the measured equivalence relation: each relation class receives independent site labels, and a measurable coloring has uniform conditional color marginals [S18] [S19] [S20]. The finite-dimensional input is the mixed determinantal bound of Ravichandran–Srivastava [S15:79]. The kernel term is handled by a free-product matrix dilation: uniform conditional color marginals give a free compression bound on $\ker E_N$ [S21]. The completion section clips to bounded approximants and transfers a sufficiently high fixed moment to produce the projection $q$ [S22].

**Manuscript A (approximation paving).** The measured argument develops one-sided matrix paving for nonsingular equivalence relations, using the finite paving-polynomial framework of Ravichandran–Luo and Ravichandran–Srivastava [S8] [S9]. A buffered pinning lemma selects a color while controlling positive-root mass [S8:492–530]. Nonsingularity is handled by passing to the Maharam extension and weighting by the height variable [S9:63–165]. The operator-algebra argument paves the normalizer algebra using the Feldman–Moore representation, adapts the free-product matrix dilation of Popa–Vaes with random diagonal phases, and removes the normal-expectation hypothesis through a three-corner construction [S11] [S12].

**The two manuscripts are companions.** Each cites the other as a separate companion. Manuscript B's introduction names the approximation theorem: "{{25}}" [S15:65] Manuscript A's introduction names the quadratic theorem: "{{20}}" [S6:88] The dependency is symmetric at the statement level: each proves the other's conjecture's companion assertion, and the two proofs are independent [S6:90–93] [S15:65].

## history

The classical context is Kadison–Singer and its diagonal paving counterpart, resolved by Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava [S15:48]. "{{12}}" [S15:48] The restriction to general masas is sharp: norm paving characterizes type I algebras with a normal expectation.

Popa and Vaes introduced the strong-operator and approximation versions and conjectured both for every masa [S15:50]. "{{13}}" [S15:50] Their prior bounds were quartic ($\eps^{-4}$) for arbitrary masas in type I algebras and for Cartan inclusions in amenable algebras, and quadratic only for tracial ultraproducts of singular masas. They "explicitly identified the need to unite the Cartan and singular arguments" [S15:50]. The finite-dimensional input is the multipaving bound of Ravichandran–Srivastava [S15:50].

The exponent two is necessary: "{{14}}" [S15:54] The argument uses a centered self-adjoint unitary from Popa–Vaes's $L^2$ obstruction proposition and shows that the obstruction persists with the varying diagonals and compressions allowed by strong-operator paving. "{{24}}" [S15:63]

Manuscript A's positioning is the same conjecture from the approximation side: "{{15}}" diagonal masa of $\mathcal B(\ell^2)$ [S6:15–16]. Anderson formulated its diagonal paving counterpart, and Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava resolved it [S6:17–19]. For general masas, norm paving is too restrictive and characterizes type I algebras [S6:20–21]. Popa and Vaes "introduced strong-operator and approximation versions of paving and conjectured that every MASA has both properties" [S6:22–24]. "{{16}}" [S6:23–25] The restricted cases (type I, Cartan in amenable algebras, singular ultraproducts) do not provide the expectation-free theorem [S6:27–35].

## consequences

**Quadratic norm paving in ultrapowers.** The strong-operator result implies norm paving in the Ocneanu ultrapower for countably decomposable expected masas: "{{23}}" [S15:32–39] The forward transfer of Popa–Vaes converts the so-paving to norm paving in the ultrapower with the same error and partition count [S15:40–43].

**Quadratic approximation paving for expected MASAs.** When the masa is the range of a normal conditional expectation, the approximation-paving partition count improves from $\varepsilon^{-6}$ to $\varepsilon^{-2}$: "{{22}}" [S6:95–101] in place of $R(\varepsilon)$ [S6:102].
