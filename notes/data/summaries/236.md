## undergraduate

**Factor of IID** means you can build a random object from one
independent random label at each vertex, using a deterministic
rule that is the same everywhere. The question (raised by
Lyons): for the **free Ising state** on an infinite
regular tree, at what inverse temperature $\beta$ does such
a rule exist? The answer: the threshold is
$\tanh\beta=(d-1)^{-1/2}$ — exactly the
**critical temperature** where the tree Ising model
phase-transitions. Below it (low temperature), no
local rule can produce the spins; above it (high
temperature), a simple rule works. The
construction uses no root and commutes with
every tree symmetry [S3:39–46, S2:557].

## graduate

**Factor-of-IID threshold**
[S3:39–46, S2:557]. For every $d\ge3$ and
$\beta\ge0$, the free zero-field ferromagnetic
Ising measure on the infinite $d$-regular tree is a
factor of IID vertex labels exactly when
$\tanh\beta\le(d-1)^{-1/2}$, including equality.
The positive implication (below threshold,
including the critical point) is proved here; the
strict converse is from the prior
literature. The factor commutes with
every tree automorphism on every
label input.

## history

The **factor-of-IID question** for Gibbs states on
trees asks when a spin system can be generated
from independent random labels by a vertex-local
rule. For the **Ising model** on the regular
tree, the phase transition at
$\tanh\beta=(d-1)^{-1/2}$ (the Nam–Sly–Zhang
critical temperature) was conjectured to be
the factor-of-IID threshold. This result
[S3:39–46] proves the positive
implication including equality,
resolving the ferromagnetic case of
Lyons's question. The
construction is root-free and
equivariant, giving a
stronger statement than
a mere existence
argument.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** The
Ising model on trees, the factor-of-IID
concept, and the critical temperature
$\tanh\beta=(d-1)^{-1/2}$ are
well-known. The positive
implication including
equality, with the
equivariant root-free
construction, is the
novel contribution. I
cannot confirm this
independently from
training alone.
