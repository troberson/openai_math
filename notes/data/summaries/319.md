## contents

The release's blurb [S1:7831] and the catalog abstract [S2:732] state
the family's claim: the Hahn--Wilson conjecture is refuted at chromatic
height two. For every sufficiently large prime $p$, there is a
connective $p$-complete spectrum of exact fp-type two that cannot be
built from the standard form of $\BP\langle2\rangle_p^\wedge$ by
finitely many sums, shifts, cones, and retracts, yet it satisfies both
localization comparisons. The manuscript's abstract
[S3:56–63]: "{{1}}"

## undergraduate

The Hahn--Wilson conjecture asks which infinite spectra can be "built"
from a truncated Brown--Peterson spectrum using finitely many of the
usual operations — cofiber sequences, shifts, sums, and retracts. The
conjecture proposed a cohomological answer: that finite presentation
of the spectrum's Steenrod cohomology, together with a bound on its
chromatic height, should be exactly the condition for such a finite
construction to exist. A spectrum's "type" records the lowest chromatic
height it is detected at; "fp-type two" means its cohomology is
finitely presented over the Steenrod algebra and it is detected at
height two but not below. The conjecture predicted that the type-two
spectra are precisely the finite constructions from
$\BP\langle2\rangle$ [S3:67–102]:

> {{2}}

The counterexample shows the prediction is false: the spectrum is of
the right type and has the right cohomological finiteness, so the
conjecture's condition is met, and yet it cannot be assembled from
$\BP\langle2\rangle$ by any finite number of the allowed operations.
What is remarkable is that its chromatic localizations behave as if it
had been constructed — both the finite and telescopic localizations
agree with the expected targets — so the obstruction lives not in any
local piece but in how the local pieces are joined.

## graduate

**The statement and its prime range.** The theorem is a counterexample
to the universally quantified conjecture, valid for every sufficiently
large prime $p$, and the underlying spectrum is independent of the
standard quotient generators of the regular $\BP$-module used
throughout, by a uniqueness result of Angeltveit--Lind with the
corrected argument and the additional $p$-local input of Lee
[S3:114–134]. The two localization comparisons
$L_2^fX\simeq L_2X$ and $L_{T(2)}X\simeq L_{K(2)}X$ are part of the
theorem, so the example satisfies the finite and telescopic localization
comparisons even though it lies outside the thick subcategory
[S3:114–126].

**Algebraic core and gluing.** The algebraic argument comes first:
the geometry section defines the reduced setting and proves its chart
geometry; the completion section computes complete operations using
compatible maps on finite reductions and realizes perfect modules as
actual complexes, so the calculation controls maps and cones as well as
endomorphism groups. The operator and gluing sections construct the
Laurent operator and the gluing lattice, and the obstruction section
proves a finite-tower criterion and verifies its hypothesis for the
glued object. Together these explain how complete operations control
finite constructions and how the gluing obstructs them globally
[S3:297–314].

**Transfer and exact type.** The comparison and transfer sections
establish the comparison with spectra and prove the transfer
proposition, which takes the connective cover of the resulting
$E(2)$-local spectrum using the methods of Mahowald--Rezk, proving the
total-finiteness test needed for the fp condition. The consequences
section establishes the exact type-two assertion and both localization
comparisons, and proves the separate obstruction for the connective
cover of Morava $E$-theory [S3:297–314].

## history

The Hahn--Wilson conjecture is a finite-presentation question at the
boundary between chromatic homotopy theory and the algebra of the
Steenrod algebra. Its written formulation appears in
Lee--Pstrągowski, who report learning it from Dylan Wilson in 2021, and
the fp framework rests on Mahowald--Rezk's duality theory and the
equivalence between Steenrod-algebra finite presentation and the
total-finiteness test for a type-$n+1$ finite spectrum. Hahn--Wilson's
own redshift theorem supplies the multiplicative-form examples whose
$K$-homology has fp-type $n+1$. The height-two case was the natural
place to look for counterexamples because the local algebra is
explicit enough to compute complete operations, while the gluing of the
local pieces is complex enough to escape finite construction
[S3:137–293].

## consequences

**The Hahn--Wilson conjecture is false at height two.** For every
sufficiently large prime $p$, there is a connective $p$-complete
spectrum $X$ of exact fp-type two with
$X\notin\Thick(\BP\langle2\rangle_p^\wedge)$ for the standard form of
$\BP\langle2\rangle_p^\wedge$, refuting the universally quantified
conjecture [S3:114–126].

**The localizations still agree.** The same spectrum satisfies
$L_2^fX\simeq L_2X$ and $L_{T(2)}X\simeq L_{K(2)}X$, so the obstruction
to finite construction is invisible to the finite and telescopic
localization comparisons and lies only in the global gluing
[S3:56–63].

**A finite-tower criterion is established.** The obstruction section
proves a finite-tower criterion for when a complete-operations
computation controls finite constructions, and verifies its hypothesis
for the glued object, giving a reusable obstruction principle
[S3:297–314].
