## undergraduate

A **molecule** is atoms (positively charged nuclei) surrounded by
negatively charged electrons that repel and attract each other by the
Coulomb (electric) force. Finding the **ground state** — the
lowest-energy arrangement of the electrons — is one of the
hardest problems in physics, because the electrons cannot be treated
one at a time; they're all entangled. **QMA-hardness** is a computer-
science way of saying a problem is "as hard as the hardest problems a
quantum computer can verify": you can't efficiently compute the
ground energy, and the difficulty is provably at least as great as any
other such problem. These two manuscripts prove that computing the
ground energy of a 3D molecule — with the electrons in the full
**continuum** (not pinned to a fixed set of orbitals) — is
QMA-hard. The surprise in the second [S4:28–37] is that the
hardness holds even in the **simplest** case: every nucleus has charge
one (a proton), positioned at rational coordinates, with no external
fields — the hardest cases are not exotic.
