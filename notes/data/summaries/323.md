## undergraduate

**The separable quotient problem (SQ)** asks a simple question about
infinite vector spaces used to model continuous quantities: if your space
is infinite-dimensional, does it at least *contain a copy of something
simple* — namely, an infinite-dimensional quotient that is **separable**
(that is, has a countable dense subset, so it can be approximated by a
countable list of points)?

You might guess "yes": pass to a quotient, make it smaller, and you
should land on something manageable. The answer here is that the
question **cannot be decided from the usual axioms of mathematics
(ZFC)**. In one consistent universe of sets (built using a very large
assumption, a **measurable cardinal**), every infinite-dimensional Banach
space over the reals *and* the complexes has a separable infinite-
dimensional quotient. In another universe (one satisfying the **
continuum hypothesis**, a smaller assumption), there are concrete
infinite-dimensional Banach spaces with **no** such quotient. So ZFC
simply does not know the answer, in both directions.

## graduate

**Main theorem (formalized in part)** [S3:38–45, S2:744].
Relative to the consistency of ZFC with a measurable cardinal, the
separable quotient assertion
$`SQ: \forall X \text{ (inf.-dim. Banach space)}, X \text{ admits a separable inf.-dim. quotient}`$
is independent of ZFC, over both the real and complex fields. For spaces
of norm density $`\aleph_1`$, independence is relative to the
consistency of ZFC alone.

**The formalization covers the CH negative direction only** [S4:9].
The scope note selects the conditional counterexample: under the
continuum hypothesis, over both `ℝ` and `ℂ`, there exists an
infinite-dimensional Banach space admitting no bounded linear surjection
onto an infinite-dimensional separable Banach space. The positive
consistency direction — the measurable-cardinal model in which SQ holds —
and the paper's full relative-independence conclusion are **outside** the
selected statement. In particular, the formalization contains no
`main_results` entry and no proved OAI tree.

**The construction** [S3:38–45].
The negative direction runs through a classical Banach-space machinery
— norming functionals, branchings, exact pairs, preduals, and a
model-theoretic forcing step that yields a Banach space in a CH-forcing
extension with no separable infinite-dimensional quotient. The positive
direction uses the measurable cardinal to build a forcing extension in
which every infinite-dimensional Banach space, real and complex, has a
separable infinite-dimensional quotient. The density-$`\aleph_1`$ result
drops the measurable cardinal and reads independence off ZFC's own
consistency.

## history

The **separable quotient problem** is a classical open problem in Banach
space theory, usually attributed to Pełczyński (1968) and long standing
among the prominent unresolved questions of the area. The problem sits
alongside other independence-flavored questions in functional analysis
where set-theoretic assumptions are known to matter.

The present result is a **relative independence theorem**: it does not
decide SQ in ZFC, but shows that the two natural directions — the
positive direction forced by a measurable cardinal and the negative
direction forced by the continuum hypothesis — are each consistent with
the axioms of set theory. This is the standard shape of an independence
result: you do not prove the statement, you prove that ZFC cannot.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** The separable quotient
problem has been open since its formulation; partial results and partial
answers for specific classes of spaces (Hilbert spaces, $`L^p`$ spaces,
classical sequence spaces) are known. A full relative-independence
theorem over both fields, with the measurable-cardinal positive model
and the CH negative model, is the novel contribution claimed here; I
cannot verify the forcing-and-Banach-space construction independently
from training alone.

## consequences

- **SQ is independent of ZFC, relative to a measurable cardinal**
  [S3:38–40, S2:744]: the positive direction (SQ holds for every
  infinite-dimensional Banach space over `ℝ` and `ℂ`) is consistent
  relative to a measurable cardinal.
- **CH yields counterexamples over both fields** [S3:40–41, S2:744,
  S4:9]: under the continuum hypothesis, there exist infinite-dimensional
  Banach spaces over `ℝ` and `ℂ` admitting no separable
  infinite-dimensional quotient. This is the named counterexample the
  ledger's `counterex_broad` flag records.
- **Density-$`\aleph_1`$ independence relative to ZFC alone**
  [S3:43–44, S2:744]: restricting to spaces of norm density
  $`\aleph_1`$, the independence of SQ is relative to the consistency of
  ZFC alone, with no measurable-cardinal hypothesis.
- **The formalization is partial** [S4:9]: only the CH negative
  direction is captured; the positive measurable-cardinal model and the
  full relative-independence conclusion are not formalized in the
  release.
