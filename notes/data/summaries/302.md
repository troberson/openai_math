> **302. Radius of comparison equals half the mean dimension.** For every minimal homeomorphism *h* of an infinite compact metrizable space *X*, the radius of comparison of $`C(X)\rtimes_h\mathbb Z`$ equals $`\tfrac12\mathrm{mdim}(X,h)`$, including infinite values. ## undergraduate

A **minimal homeomorphism** is a map of a compact space with no proper
closed piece it leaves invariant — iterating it from any point wanders
through the whole space. The **crossed product**
$C(X) \\rtimes_h \\mathbb{Z}$ is a $C^*$-algebra encoding both the
functions on $X$ and the dynamics of $h$.

Two invariants measure such algebras. The **mean dimension**
$\\mathrm{mdim}(X, h)$ is a topological-entropy-like number describing
how complicated the dynamics is. The **radius of comparison** $\\mathrm{rc}$
is an $K$-theoretic number measuring how far the algebra is from being
"comparatively simple."

The **Phillips–Toms conjecture** predicted that, for these integer
actions, the radius of comparison is exactly **half** the mean dimension:
$\\mathrm{rc} = \\tfrac{1}{2} \\mathrm{mdim}$. Manuscript 2 [S3:63–71]
proves this, including the case where both sides are infinite. It also
shows that zero mean dimension, the small boundary property, Jiang–Su
stability, and finite nuclear dimension are all the same condition, and
that in this case the nuclear dimension is at most one.

Manuscript 1 [S4:63–74] supplies a topological tool the proof needs: a
"boundary-preserving compression" in complex cobordism theory, which lets
one place a positive proportion of variables in a smash product onto their
boundaries.

## graduate

**Main theorem (Manuscript 2)** [S3:63–71, S2:698].
For every minimal homeomorphism $h$ of an infinite compact metrizable
space $X$,
$\\mathrm{rc}(C(X) \\rtimes_h \\mathbb{Z}) = \\tfrac{1}{2}
\\mathrm{mdim}(X, h)$,
with equality at infinity. This is the integer-action case of the
Phillips–Toms conjecture.

**The zero-mean-dimension characterization** [S3:66–71].
For these systems, the following are equivalent: $\\mathrm{mdim}(X, h)
= 0$, the small boundary property, Jiang–Su stability
($C(X) \\rtimes_h \\mathbb{Z}$ absorbs the Jiang–Su algebra), and finite
nuclear dimension of the crossed product. In this case the nuclear
dimension is at most one.

**The cobordism tool (Manuscript 1)** [S4:63–74].
In sufficiently large smash powers, an $MU$-null restriction to a finite
pointed subcomplex becomes stably null on the union of products with a
fixed positive proportion of restricted factors. When the torus slot
bundle embeds into a trivial bundle of rank less than twice the source
rank, the compression places a positive proportion of slots on their
boundaries in arbitrarily large powers and fixes every original boundary
slot exactly; an example at equality makes the strict rank inequality
sharp.

## history

The Phillips–Toms conjecture relates the radius of comparison of a
crossed product by a minimal dynamical system to the mean dimension of
that system. The radius of comparison, introduced in the study of
$K$-theoretic comparison in $C^*$-algebras, had been expected to be
controlled by the dimension-like invariants of the underlying dynamics,
but the precise formula — the factor of $\\tfrac{1}{2}$ — and the
infinite case were open.

The proof rests on a compression argument in the cobordism-theoretic
language: the boundary-preserving compression of Manuscript 1 supplies
the stable vanishing needed to control the radius from below and above,
and the dynamical comparison then yields the equality. The
zero-mean-dimension side ties the dynamical small boundary property to
the operator-algebraic Jiang–Su stability and nuclear dimension.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Mean dimension (Lindenstrauss
and others), the radius of comparison, and the small boundary property
are all established invariants in the literature; the Phillips–Toms
conjecture for integer actions, the $\\tfrac{1}{2}$ formula including
infinity, and the compression mechanism are the novel contributions
claimed here. I cannot verify the cobordism-theoretic compression
independently from training alone.

  S2:698]: $\\mathrm{rc}(C(X) \\rtimes_h \\mathbb{Z}) = \\tfrac{1}{2}
  \\mathrm{mdim}(X, h)$ for every minimal homeomorphism of an infinite
  compact metrizable space, including infinite values. This is the

- **A zero-mean-dimension characterization** [S3:66–71]: zero mean

  finite nuclear dimension are equivalent, with nuclear dimension at
  most one.
- **A boundary-preserving compression in complex cobordism** [S4:63–74]:
