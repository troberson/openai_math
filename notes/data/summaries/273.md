## undergraduate

**Photons** are the quanta of light. A **bosonic** system (the kind
that describes light) can have any number of photons in each mode
(a "mode" is a single frequency/direction channel). The **entropy
photon number** is a way to measure how "mixed" a quantum state of
light is, expressed as an effective number of photons. When you mix
two beams of light on a **beam splitter** (a half-silvered mirror),
the output should be at least as "mixed" as a weighted average of the
two inputs. That's the **entropy photon-number inequality**: the
output's effective photon number is at least the transmissivity-
weighted average of the two inputs'. This is the quantum analogue of
a classical fact (entropy is non-decreased by mixing), and it had
been conjectured but never proved for the full generality of
multi-mode inputs with arbitrary internal entanglement. This
manuscript [S3:34–36] proves it.

## graduate

**The entropy photon-number inequality** [S3:34–36, S2:637, S4:9]. For
two independent bosonic inputs $ ho_A,

ho_B$ with finite mean energy in any finite number $n$ of modes, write
$N( ho)
= g^{-1}(S(
ho)/n)$ where $S$ is von Neumann entropy and $g(x)
= (x+1)\log(x+1)
- x\log x$
is the thermal entropy per mode. For a beam splitter of transmissivity
$0 \le \eta \le 1$,
its output $ ho_C$ satisfies $N( ho_C)
\ge \eta N(
ho_A)
+ (1-\eta)
N( ho_B)$ [S4:9]. Entanglement among modes within either input is
allowed; product thermal inputs attain equality even when their
entropies differ [S3:34–36].

## history

The **entropy photon-number inequality** is the quantum-information
analogue of the classical fact that entropy is non-decreased by convex
mixing. It had been conjectured in the single-mode case and proved for
special input classes (thermal states, coherent states), but the
**general finite-energy multi-mode setting with arbitrary internal
entanglement** had remained open. The manuscript [S3:34–36] closes it.
As a consequence, the manuscript also determines the exact minimum
output entropy at fixed input entropy for every finite tensor power of
an identical thermal attenuator, and the exact classical capacity region
of the degraded two-receiver pure-loss bosonic broadcast channel under a
mean photon constraint [S3:34–36].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** The entropy photon number,
the thermal entropy function $g(x)$, beam splitters, and bosonic input-
output relationships are standard in quantum information theory and
quantum optics. The full finite-energy multi-mode proof with arbitrary
internal entanglement, and its two consequences (thermal-attenuator
minimum-output-entropy and broadcast capacity), are the novel
contributions. I cannot confirm these independently from training alone.
