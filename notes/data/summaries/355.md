## contents

[S1:8633] (family entry):

> **355. Unique tangent flows at the first surface singularity.** Proves the first-singular-time case of tangent-flow uniqueness for smooth compact connected embedded surfaces without boundary in $\mathbb R^3$. At every singular point, all fixed-center backward tangent flows agree as area measures at every negative time in the original ambient coordinates, without mean-convexity or a prescribed tangent model.

[S2:810] carries the `\resultentry` with the single manuscript [S3].
The abstract states the result:

**Uniqueness of tangent flows** [S3:71–77]: at each singular point of
the first singular time of the mean-curvature flow of a smooth compact
connected embedded surface without boundary in $\mathbb R^3$, all
fixed-center rescalings converge locally smoothly on compact
negative-time intervals to one multiplicity-one homothetic
self-shrinker flow. The limit is unique in the original ambient
coordinates, including its position and axes. No mean-convexity
assumption or prescribed tangent model is required.

## undergraduate

Imagine a soap film shrinking under its own surface tension — this is
**mean-curvature flow**. As it shrinks, it eventually develops a
**singularity**, a point where the surface pinches or develops a
corner (think of a ring collapsing to a point).

To understand what happens at that pinching, you "zoom in" on the
singular point as the moment of collapse approaches, watching what
shape the surface settles into. This zoomed-in picture is called a
**tangent flow**.

**The question** is: is that zoomed-in picture *unique*? Does the way
you zoom in (from which direction, centered where) change the picture
you see? This paper says **no** — at the *first* moment a singularity
appears on a smooth shrinking surface in 3D space, the zoomed-in
picture is the same no matter how you look at it. It is always a
**self-shrinker** (a shape that shrinks to itself, like a flat plane
collapsing to a line), and it is unique even down to its exact
position and orientation in space.

## graduate

**Main theorem** [S3:71–77, S2:810].
Let $`t_0`$ be the first singular time of the mean-curvature flow of a
smooth compact connected embedded surface without boundary in
$`\mathbb R^3`$. At each singular point $`p`$ at time $`t_0`$, the
fixed-center backward tangent-flow rescalings (parabolically rescaled
about `p` and pulled back in time) converge, on compact
negative-time intervals, to a **single multiplicity-one homothetic
self-shrinker flow**, in the $`C^\\infty`$-locally-uniform sense. The
limit is **unique in the original ambient coordinates** — including
its position (translation) and axes (rotation). No mean-convexity
assumption and no prescribed tangent model are required.

## history

The **uniqueness of tangent flows** in mean-curvature flow is a
central regularity question. For **convex** (or mean-convex)
submanifolds, uniqueness of the tangent flow at the first singular
time is classical (Huisken's convexity preservation, and the
classification of self-shrinkers in the convex setting). The hard case
is the **non-convex embedded surface**, where the limit of the rescaling
can in principle depend on the approach (center, rate) to the
singularity.

The **novel contribution** here is the removal of the mean-convexity
and tangent-model hypotheses for embedded surfaces: the result is
stated for an arbitrary smooth compact connected embedded surface
without boundary, and the limit is not merely a self-shrinker up to
congruence but is **fixed in the ambient coordinates** (position and
axes). The proof draws on finite-jet obstructions, contour and gauge
analysis, and a comparison of the limiting flows — as the section
titles indicate.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** As of my training

## consequences

- **Tangent-flow uniqueness at the first surface singularity**
  [S3:71–77, S2:810]: at every singular point of the first singular
  time, the backward tangent flow is independent of the
  rescaling center and rate.
- **The limit is a multiplicity-one self-shrinker** [S3:71–77]: the
  zoomed-in picture is a single self-shrinker (not a weighted sum of
  several).
- **Ambient-coordinate rigidity** [S3:71–77]: the limit is unique
  including its position and axes in $\mathbb R^3$, not merely up to
  congruence.
- **No mean-convexity, no tangent model** [S3:71–77]: the result
  holds for an arbitrary smooth compact connected embedded surface
  without boundary.
