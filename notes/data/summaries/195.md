## undergraduate

A **local ring** is the algebraic analogue of the functions
defined at a single point of a geometric object
— you zoom in on one point and look only at what
happens right there. A **module** over that ring is a way
the ring acts on some collection of "arrows" or "vectors."
A module is **Cohen–Macaulay** when its "depth"
— a measure of how many independent directions you
can probe without hitting a zero divisor — matches
the dimension of the ring. A **maximal Cohen–
Macaulay** (MCM) module is one that achieves this
maximum. The **small Cohen–Macaulay module
conjecture** asked: does every complete Noetherian local
**domain** (no zero divisors) have at least one nonzero
finitely generated MCM module? This manuscript says
**no**: it constructs a specific 3-dimensional domain
over the complex numbers where *every* nonzero finitely
generated module fails to be MCM
[S3:38–41, S2:473].

## graduate

**The main theorem** [S3:38–41, S2:473]. There
exists a three-dimensional complete Noetherian normal
local domain $R$ containing $\mathbb{C}$, with residue
field $\mathbb{C}$, such that no nonzero finitely
generated $R$-module is maximal Cohen–Macaulay
[S3:38–41]. Equivalently: every nonzero finitely
generated $R$-module has depth $< 3 = \dim R$. The
construction also yields a three-dimensional local domain
**essentially of finite type** over $\mathbb{C}$ with the
same property [S2:473].

**The scope of the formalization.** The scope note states
the linked formalization **verifies only the ring-
theoretic candidate**: there exists a three-dimensional
complete Noetherian integrally closed local domain over
$\mathbb{C}$ whose residue field is $\mathbb{C}$
— existence and the ring properties, no more
[S4:9]. The proof uses a constructed completed surface
cone [S4:9]. The scope note explicitly excludes the full
counterexample: *"The paper's main counterexample requires
that no nonzero finitely generated module over the ring
has depth three. That conclusion, the numerical
obstruction used to prove it, and the counterexample
essentially of finite type are outside this comparison"*
[S4:13]. The scope note names one comparator [S4:17]:
`SurfaceConeCandidateSupport.lean`.

## history

The **small Cohen–Macaulay module conjecture** was
posed independently by several groups in the 1990s and
asks whether every complete Noetherian local domain admits
a nonzero finitely generated maximal Cohen–Macaulay
module. The conjecture is known to be **false** for
general complete local rings (non-domains): Hochster
constructed 2-dimensional complete local rings with no
nonzero MCM modules. The **domain** form — where
the ring has no zero divisors — was the remaining
open case, and was widely believed to be true because
the absence of zero divisors was expected to prevent the
pathological behavior in Hochster's examples. This
manuscript **disproves the domain form** by constructing
a 3-dimensional complete normal local domain over
$\mathbb{C}$ where every nonzero finitely generated
module has depth $< 3$, using an explicit Kummer surface
construction and a numerical obstruction to the
existence of MCM modules. The essentially-of-finite-type
variant extends the counterexample beyond the complete
setting [S3:38–41, S2:473].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** I know the
small Cohen–Macaulay module conjecture as a named
open problem in commutative algebra, Hochster's
counterexamples for non-domains, and the general belief
that the domain form was true. A **disproof of the
domain form** via a Kummer surface construction, with the
specific 3-dimensional complete normal local domain over
$\mathbb{C}$ and the depth-obstruction argument, is a
result I cannot independently verify from training alone.

## consequences

**The domain form of the small Cohen–Macaulay module
conjecture is disproved.** There exists a 3-dimensional
complete Noetherian normal local domain over
$\mathbb{C}$ with no nonzero finitely generated MCM module
[S3:38–41, S2:473].

**The counterexample extends to the essentially-of-finite-
type setting.** A 3-dimensional local domain essentially
of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$ has the same property
[S2:473].

**The formalization covers only the ring, not the
counterexample.** The registered Lean result asserts
existence of the ring with its properties; the depth
obstruction and the MCM-absence claim are outside the
formalization [S4:9, S4:13].
