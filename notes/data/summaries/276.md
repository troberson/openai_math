## undergraduate

A **quantum channel** is a physical process that takes a quantum state
and turns it into another, the way a noisy wire corrupts a signal.
The **classical capacity** of a channel is the maximum rate at which
reliable classical bits can be sent through it. **Generalized amplitude
damping** (GAD) is a specific noisy channel: it describes a qubit that
randomly flips, with the environment also having a thermal component
(not just pure decoherence). These results compute the exact capacity
of every GAD channel — for every damping strength and every
thermal occupation — showing it equals a simple one-variable
maximum, and that a binary code (just two states) achieves it at every
block length [S3:39–48].
