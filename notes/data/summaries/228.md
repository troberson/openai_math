> **228. Continuum phase transitions for radial pair potentials.** Constructs stable distance-dependent pair interactions for three-dimensional classical particles with a first-order phase transition: the canonical free energy has a derivative jump at one inverse temperature throughout an open density interval. One potential has a divergent repulsive core; another is bounded and continuous with an integrable power-law tail, realizing the type of transition sought in Simon's continuum problem.

## undergraduate

A **pair potential** describes the energy cost of placing two particles a
distance $r$ apart. In a **classical particle system**, many particles in
three-dimensional space interact through this pairwise rule, and the
**canonical free energy** measures the system's thermodynamic state at a
given temperature and density. A **first-order phase transition** is a
sudden change of state (like water freezing): the free energy is continuous
but its slope with respect to temperature jumps. This family constructs
two explicit pair potentials where a real three-dimensional gas shows such
a transition at a specific temperature, for a whole range of densities
[S3:40–44, S4:54–59, S2:541].

## graduate

**Divergent-core potential** [S3:40–44, S2:541]. A
stable radial pair potential in three dimensions with a
divergent repulsive core, a nontrivial attractive interval,
and an integrable tail satisfying
$\phi(r)=o(r^{-3})$. The canonical free energy has a
strict downward derivative jump at one common finite
positive inverse temperature throughout an open interval
of positive densities. The free-cube canonical free
energy is finite at every positive inverse temperature
and density.

**Bounded algebraic-decay potential** [S4:54–59,
S2:541]. A bounded, continuous, stable radial pair
potential with $|\phi(r)|\le Cr^{-3-1/32}$ for $r
\ge 1$. Throughout an open interval of positive densities,
the canonical thermodynamic free energy is finite at
every positive inverse temperature and has a strict
downward derivative jump at one common finite positive
inverse temperature. The scope note [S5:12] places
this interval around $5p/3$ (where $p$ is the
unit-separated packing-density limit) with the common
jump at $\beta_c\in[7/8,9/8]$.

## history

**Simon's continuum problem** (Simon, 1980) asked for a
pair-potential system in three-dimensional space showing
a genuine first-order phase transition with a
discontinuous equation of state — the lattice gas (Tonks
gas) and the lattice Ising model give transitions, but
on a lattice; the continuum analogue with a continuous
density interval was open. Earlier constructions used
non-radial potentials or non-integrable tails. The
**divergent-core** potential [S3:40–44] provides
the first radial example with a
$\phi(r)=o(r^{-3})$ integrable tail; the
**bounded algebraic-decay** potential
[S4:54–59] improves to a bounded, continuous
potential with the explicit tail bound
$Cr^{-3-1/32}$. The density interval around
$5p/3$ [S5:12] is the packing-density
regime the construction targets.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Simon's
problem, the lattice-gas/lattice-Ising transitions,
and the stability conditions for pair potentials are
standard. The two explicit radial constructions with
the stated tail bounds and the open density interval
are the novel contributions. I cannot confirm these
independently from training alone.

## consequences

- **A radial potential with a divergent core and an
  $o(r^{-3})$ tail** [S3:40–44]: a first-order
  transition at a common $\beta_c$ across an open
  density interval.
- **A bounded continuous radial potential with an
  explicit $Cr^{-3-1/32}$ tail**
  [S4:54–59, S5:12]: the same transition, with
  the jump at $\beta_c\in[7/8,9/8]$ near the
  packing-density regime $5p/3$.
- **Simon's continuum problem** [S2:541]: both
  constructions realize the type of transition
  sought — a radial, stable, three-dimensional pair
  potential with a derivative jump over a density
  interval.
