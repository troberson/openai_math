## undergraduate

Hilbert's tenth problem asks for an algorithm that, given a polynomial with integer coefficients, decides whether it has an integer solution. That question was closed in 1970: no such algorithm exists. Family 004 answers the rational version — same input, same question, with a solution in ℚ replacing a solution in ℤ. The manuscript states its result as [S5:8–10]:

> {{5}}

The clause "{{6}}" [S5:8–10] is load-bearing. If the number of variables were fixed in advance, the question would be a different one; the theorem is about the full problem, where the polynomial arrives with any number of variables attached.

The proof is a reduction, not a machine that fails. It shows that the integer problem — already known to be undecidable — can be answered by asking the rational problem a bounded number of times. The mechanism is a parallel search [S5:181]:

> {{7}}

So the entire burden is one implication: if a polynomial has no integer zero, then at least one of a finite list of effectively computable tests fails. The reduction produces that list as the equivalence at [S5:176–180], and everything else in the paper exists to force a failure.

The tests are comparisons of size. Each candidate solution is attached to a point on an elliptic curve, and the proof needs an upper bound on the height of the ring element representing that point — "{{8}}" [S5:192], with the estimate "{{9}}" [S5:194] displayed just below it — and a lower bound coming from the canonical height of the attached point. The two bounds collide unless the modelled root coordinates are an ordinary finite interval of integers, which is the elementarity the reduction needs [S5:213]:

> {{10}}

The arithmetic that makes the height bound uniform is a 2-converse statement about Selmer groups, and that is why the family's second manuscript sits in this family rather than beside it.

**The shape of the proof, in six moves.** (1) Encode the integers inside a ring whose elements are built from ℚ, and attach to each candidate root a point on an elliptic curve. (2) Produce the finite test family: "{{11}}" [S5:173–174], each a finite computation in a residue ring, a height box, and local solvability conditions. (3) Prove the pole-parity condition, which is where two-descent data becomes rational points [S5:232–234]. (4) Prove the five-point height estimate, whose modularity step is a Fontaine–Mazur theorem at 2 [S5:236–237]. (5) Force the contradiction \(B^2\ll_f B\) [S5:224–225]. (6) Read off undecidability, and record the exact complexity [S6:9–12].

## history

Prior (my training data, not this repo.) Hilbert's tenth problem dates to 1900. The chain that settled the integer case runs Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich, with Matiyasevich's 1970 theorem on recursively enumerable sets supplying the last step; after that, the rational case became the standard open transfer problem. Julia Robinson's own work on the rationals predates the integer resolution. Poonen's 2009 conditional result — ℤ Diophantine in ℚ assuming squarefree rank for a specific elliptic curve — is the form the question took in the twenty-first century, and the Mazur–Rubin conjecture on ranks in quadratic-twist families became the standard hypothesis under which the transfer goes through.

## consequences

**The complexity is pinned, not just the undecidability.** The corollary fixes the exact degree [S6:9–12] — $\mathrm{H10}(\Q)$ has Turing degree $0'$ — and the same corollary recasts the problem in a bounded-degree form, with the caveat attached [S6:21–22]: "{{53}}" The caveats paragraph is the honest boundary of the whole construction [S6:100–107], quoted above: no many-one completeness, no single existential definition of ℤ in ℚ.

**The chain inside the release.** Family 004 sits downstream of two other families, and the citations are in the manuscript's own bibliography. The undecidability manuscript cites family 006's Goldfeld-form 2-converse as `UnrestrictedTwoConverse` — "{{54}}" [S7:26] — and family 010's Fontaine–Mazur theorem as `OddRegularFontaineMazurAtTwo` — "{{55}}" [S7:34] — using both in the parity and height steps [S5:232–234], [S5:236–237]. Its own second manuscript cites the same family-006 paper as `OpenAIGoldfeldTwoConverse2026` [S11:349–355], under the same title [S11:351], and says that companion is not an input to its proof [S10:39–43]. The pair pages are cross-linked the way 001 and 032 are: [006 — Goldfeld's conjecture](006-goldfelds-conjecture.md) is the upstream link, and [002 — the full Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula from low Selmer corank](002-full-birch-swinnerton-dyer-formula-from-low-selmer-corank.md) is the BSD-side neighbour of the same Selmer machinery. A chain is only as strong as its weakest link, and every link in this one is unformalized.

**What the result changes for the transfer problem.** The number-field case is settled by a Diophantine definition of ℤ; this family settles ℚ without one, so the two routes are now visibly different, and the manuscript's own framing of the difference is the finite-test interface [S5:176–180] rather than a definition.
