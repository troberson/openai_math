## undergraduate

**SLE** (Schramm–Loewner evolution) is a random curve drawn
in the plane, indexed by a parameter $\kappa$ that controls how
"rough" the curve is. For $0<\kappa<8$, the curve has a well-defined
**Hausdorff dimension** $d=1+\kappa/8$. The **Hausdorff measure** is
a way of measuring the "size" of the curve at that dimension, analogous
to how length measures a 1D curve. The question Schramm posed: does a
natural gauge (a slightly modified power of the radius) give the curve
positive, finite measure? The answer is **yes** — and the gauge
is explicit [S3:30–35, S5:1–8, S2:545].

## graduate

**Moment-integral construction**
[S3:30–35, S2:545]. For each $0<\kappa<8$, constructs a
deterministic Hausdorff gauge that almost surely assigns positive
finite measure to every nontrivial compact positive-time segment of
chordal SLE. The gauge is defined through a moment-integral and
finite-batch construction. The entire trace has finite expected gauge
measure in each bounded box.

**Explicit gauge** [S4:20, S4:23, S5:1–8,
S2:545]. For each fixed $0<\kappa<8$, let $d=1+\kappa/8$. The
gauge $h(r)=r^d(\log\log(1/r))^{(2-d)/2}$ at sufficiently small
radii almost surely gives positive finite Hausdorff measure to every
nontrivial positive-time compact segment of chordal SLE. This is an
explicit solution to Schramm's Hausdorff-measure problem. The entire
trace has finite expected measure in every bounded disk. The
exponent-one iterated-logarithm gauge suggested by Schramm is not
sigma-finite on any such segment.

## history

**Schramm's Hausdorff-measure problem** asked whether chordal SLE
$\kappa$ for $0<\kappa<8$ admits a natural Hausdorff gauge giving
positive finite measure to its trace. The
**moment-integral construction** [S3:30–35] provides an
existence proof via a finite-batch method. The
**explicit gauge** [S4:20, S4:23, S5:1–8]
identifies the gauge in closed form:
$h(r)=r^d(\log\log(1/r))^{(2-d)/2}$, and shows the simpler
exponent-one iterated-logarithm gauge
$r^d(\log(1/r))$ is not sigma-finite, so the
$(2-d)/2$ correction is necessary.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** The Hausdorff
dimension of SLE, $\dim_H = 1+\kappa/8$, is a classical
result. The existence of a positive finite Hausdorff measure
for the trace was an open question posed by Schramm. I
cannot confirm the explicit gauge formula independently
from training alone.

## consequences

- **The explicit Hausdorff gauge**
  [S4:20, S4:23, S5:1–8]: the closed-form gauge
  $r^d(\log\log(1/r))^{(2-d)/2}$ gives positive
  finite measure to every trace segment, $0<\kappa<8$.
- **The non-sigma-finiteness of the exponent-one
  gauge** [S5:7–8]: the simpler
  $r^d(\log(1/r))$ gauge is not sigma-finite on
  any segment, so the correction is essential.
- **Finite expected measure** [S3:34–35,
  S5:6–7]: the entire trace has finite expected
  gauge measure in every bounded disk.
