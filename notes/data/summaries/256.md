## contents

[S1:6317] (family entry):

> **256. Nonsingular systems of equations over arbitrary groups.** Proves that every finite system of equations over an arbitrary group whose exponent-sum matrix has full row rank over $\mathbb Q$ has a simultaneous solution in an overgroup, resolving Howie's conjecture. The coefficient group embeds in the presented quotient. A companion proves Kervaire's conjecture: adjoining one generator and one relation cannot trivialize a nontrivial group. ## undergraduate

An **equation over a group** is an expression built from group
elements and variables, equal to the identity. A **system** of
such equations is **nonsingular** if the variables appear in a
"well-constrained" way (the exponent-sum matrix has full rank
— no variable can be canceled out). **Howie's
conjecture** asked: if a finite system is nonsingular, does it
always have a simultaneous solution, once you allow the group to
grow (pass to a larger "overgroup")? The first manuscript
proves **yes** [S3:36–38, S2:601]. The second manuscript
proves a related classic, **Kervaire's conjecture**: if you
adjoin one new generator and one new relation to a nontrivial
group, you cannot collapse the whole group to the trivial
group [S4:35–37, S2:601].

## history

**Howie's conjecture** (1970s) connected the algebraic
nonsingularity of a system of equations over a group with the
existence of a simultaneous solution in an overgroup. The
"coefficient group embeds in the presented quotient" form
recorded in the catalog blurb [S1:6317] is the sharper statement
that the overgroup construction does not collapse the original
group. The first manuscript [S3:36–38] proves the general
full-row-rank case for arbitrary (not necessarily finitely
generated) coefficient groups. **Kervaire's conjecture**
(1950s) had been open for arbitrary groups for decades; the
second manuscript [S4:35–37] resolves it, and the scope
note records the stronger coefficient-injectivity statement the
formalization proves [S5:9].
