## undergraduate

A **Lipschitz-free space** is a Banach space built from a metric space
so that every distance-measuring function on the metric space extends
to a bounded linear functional on the Banach space. It is the "smallest"
Banach space that remembers all the 1-Lipschitz functions on the metric
space.

Two approximation properties matter for operators on a Banach space.
The **approximation property (AP)** says the identity can be
approximated on every compact chunk by simpler (finite-rank) operators.
The **bounded approximation property (BAP)** is stronger: it asks that
those simpler operators all have norms bounded by a *single* uniform
constant. AP is weaker — you can approximate, but the approximation
operators might need arbitrarily large norms.

**Kalton's question** (1970s) asked: if your metric space is
**uniformly discrete** (every two distinct points are at distance at
least 1 apart), does its Lipschitz-free space have the BAP? The answer
here is **no**. The authors build a countable uniformly discrete metric
space whose Lipschitz-free space has the AP (you can approximate on
each compact set) but fails the BAP (no uniform bound on the
approximation operators' norms exists). The counterexample space is
unbounded and not proper.

## graduate

**Main theorem (formalized in part)** [S4:7–13, S2:758, S5:9].
There exists a countable metric space $`(X,d)`$ with all distinct
points at distance at least one (uniformly discrete), unbounded, and not
proper, such that its real Lipschitz-free space $`\mathcal F(X)`$ has
the approximation property but fails the bounded approximation
property. Equivalently, the identity on $`\mathcal F(X)`$ can be
approximated by finite-rank operators on every compact subset, but no
single finite constant bounds the norms of those operators uniformly.

## history

**Kalton's Lipschitz-free approximation question** (N. J. Kalton,
1970s) is a long-standing open problem in the geometry of Banach
spaces, specifically in the theory of **Lipschitz-free (Amenity)
spaces**. Given a metric space $`(X,d)`$, its Lipschitz-free space
$`\mathcal F(X)`$ is the predual of the space of 1-Lipschitz functions
vanishing at a basepoint. The question asks whether uniform
discreteness of the underlying metric space is sufficient to guarantee
that $`\mathcal F(X)`$ has the bounded approximation property.

The problem is significant because the BAP is a key structural
property: it is inherited by closed subspaces and quotients, and it
controls the behavior of operator ideals. Many natural metric spaces
(hyperfinite, proper, bounded) are known to give Lipschitz-free spaces
with the BAP, so the question probed how little geometric structure is
needed.

The present result is a **negative answer**: a countable uniformly
discrete (hence very "simple" as a metric space) whose Lipschitz-free
space has the AP but not the BAP. The construction is unbounded and not
proper, and uses a **block / operator / walk / partition** machinery
(the manuscript's section titles). The companion quantitative
renorming of real $`\ell_1`$ shows the phenomenon is not isolated to
the counterexample: explicit quantitative norm-equivalence statements
with sharp BAP thresholds are available.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Kalton's question and the
positive results for restricted metric-space classes are classical.
The specific uniformly-discrete counterexample with the AP-but-not-BAP
Lipschitz-free space, together with the quantitative real-$`\ell_1`$
renorming carrying the explicit $`B_p = 300p^2+150p+5`$ threshold, is
the novel contribution claimed here; I cannot verify the block /
operator / walk construction independently from training alone.

## consequences

- **Kalton's question is answered negatively** [S4:7–13, S2:758,
  S5:9]: there is a countable uniformly discrete metric space whose
  real Lipschitz-free space has the approximation property but fails
  the bounded approximation property. This is the named counterexample
  the ledger's `counterex_broad` flag records.
- **Uniform discreteness is insufficient for the BAP** [S5:9]: the
  hypothesis of uniform discreteness, on its own, does not force the
  Lipschitz-free space to have the bounded approximation property.
- **The counterexample is unbounded and not proper** [S5:9]: the
  constructed metric space is not bounded and not proper, so the
  failure persists outside the proper/bounded settings where the BAP
  is known to hold for many classes.
- **A quantitative real-ℓ1 renorming is retained** [S5:11, S6]: for
  each $`p \ge 1`$, an equivalent complete norm on real $`\ell_1`$
  has the $`2B_p`$-BAP and fails the $`p`$-BAP, with
  $`B_p = 300p^2 + 150p + 5`$ explicit.
