## contents

The release's blurb [S1:4543] and the catalog abstract [S2:479]
state the family's claim: a finite-dimensional complex
algebra whose finite-dimensional modules have unbounded
finite projective dimensions, disproving the little
finitistic-dimension conjecture. A companion result gives
an algebra whose little and big finitistic dimensions are
infinite on the left and zero on the right. The manuscript
is *An algebra of infinite little finitistic dimension*
[S3:3, S3:5]; its abstract [S3:9–11]:

> {{1}}

## undergraduate

An **algebra** is a set where you can add and multiply,
with the multiplication respecting the field's scalars. A
**module** over an algebra is like a vector space, but the
algebra's elements (not just the field's) can act on it.
The **projective dimension** of a module measures how
"complicated" it is — a number that says how many
"layers" you need to peel off before the module becomes
"free" (i.e., as simple as a vector space). The **little
finitistic dimension** of an algebra is the supremum of
projective dimensions over all finite-dimensional modules
that have finite projective dimension. The question is:
is this number always finite? For finite-dimensional
algebras, the answer was conjectured to be
**yes** — there should be a uniform bound. This
manuscript says **no**: it constructs a specific
finite-dimensional complex algebra where, for every bound
$2m-2$, there is a finite-dimensional module with projective
dimension at least $2m-2$
[S3:9–11, S2:479].

## graduate

**The main theorem** [S3:9–11, S4:9–11]. There
exists a finite-dimensional complex algebra $A$ such that
for every $m \geq 1$ there is a finitely generated $A$-
module $N$ with $\mathrm{pd}(N) \geq 2m - 2$ and
$\mathrm{pd}(N) < \infty$. Equivalently, the little
finitistic dimension $\mathrm{fin.dim}(A) = \infty$
[S4:9–11].

**The asymmetry result** [S4:9–11, S4:15–18].
There exists a finite-dimensional complex algebra
$\Lambda$ such that $\mathrm{fin.dim}(\Lambda) =
\infty$, $\mathrm{Fin.dim}(\Lambda) = \infty$ (big
finitistic dimension), $\mathrm{fin.dim}(\Lambda^{op})
= 0$, and $\mathrm{Fin.dim}(\Lambda^{op}) = 0$.
Injectives fail to generate the unbounded derived category
on the left and generate it on the right
[S4:15–18].

**The scope of the formalization.** The scope note states
the formalized counterexample is a finite-dimensional
complex algebra with a finitely generated module of
projective dimension at least $2m-2$ and still finite for
every $m \geq 1$, and a separate formalized result giving
the left/right finitistic-dimension asymmetry
[S4:9–11]. The scope note names two comparators
[S4:15–18]: `LittleFinitistic.lean` and
`FinitisticAsymmetry.lean`.

## history

The **little finitistic-dimension conjecture** asserts
that for a finite-dimensional algebra $A$, the supremum
of projective dimensions of finitely generated modules of
finite projective dimension is finite. The **big
finitistic dimension** (supremum over all modules of
finite projective dimension, not just finitely
generated) and the small version are known to agree for
finite-dimensional algebras (Fossier--Gauthier, 2018).
The conjecture was known for many important classes
(hereditary algebras, triangular algebras,
self-injective algebras), but the general finite-
dimensional case was open. Related questions: the
relationship between finitistic dimension and injective
generation of the unbounded derived category
(Krause, 2005), and the left/right asymmetry of
finitistic invariants. This manuscript **disproves** the
conjecture by constructing an explicit finite-dimensional
complex algebra with unbounded finite projective
dimensions, and provides a companion algebra with extreme
left/right asymmetry [S3:9–11, S2:479].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** I know the
little finitistic-dimension conjecture as a named open
problem in representation theory of finite-dimensional
algebras, the Fossier--Gauthier equality result, and the
known positive cases. A **disproof by explicit
construction** — with the specific algebra, the
$2m-2$ lower bound, and the left/right asymmetry
companion — is a result I cannot independently
verify from training alone.

## consequences

**The little finitistic-dimension conjecture is
disproved.** A finite-dimensional complex algebra has
unbounded finite projective dimensions among its
finitely generated modules [S3:9–11, S4:9–11,
S2:479].

**The left/right asymmetry is extreme.** A finite-
dimensional complex algebra has infinite finitistic
dimensions on the left and zero on the right, with
injectives failing to generate the unbounded derived
category on the left [S4:9–11, S4:15–18].
