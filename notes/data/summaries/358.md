## undergraduate

Two properties of a smooth shape's geometry are related in a way
that long stood as an open question. **Property A**: the shape can
be given a metric where no two "nearby" points become focused
together by the geometry (no **conjugate points** — in optics,
conjugate points are where light rays cross). **Property B**: the
shape can be given a metric where the sectional curvature is
everywhere nonpositive (the geometry "spreads out" in every
direction, like a saddle surface everywhere).

The open question was: does property A *imply* property B? In other
words, if you can find a metric with no conjugate points, does that
force you to also be able to find a metric with nonpositive
curvature?

This result says **no** — already in dimension three. It constructs
a specific closed three-manifold (think: a smooth shape you can walk
around without ever hitting an edge) that *can* carry a metric with
no conjugate points but *cannot* carry any metric with nonpositive
sectional curvature. The two properties are genuinely independent.

## graduate

**Main theorem** [S3:28–30, S2:816].
There exists a closed, connected, orientable smooth three-manifold
$`M`$ admitting a smooth Riemannian metric $`g`$ with no
conjugate points, such that $`M`$ admits no smooth Riemannian
metric of everywhere nonpositive sectional curvature. The
no-conjugate-points assertion ranges over all geodesics and
tangent-bundle Jacobi fields, including constant geodesics and
unbounded time intervals [S5:9].

## history

The **conjugate-point** and **nonpositive-curvature** properties are
classical invariants in Riemannian geometry. Conjugate points arise
when Jacobi fields (infinitesimal geodesic variations) vanish again
after being nonzero — in physics, they mark caustics where geodesics
focus. Nonpositive sectional curvature is the "spreading" condition
that characterizes CAT(0) spaces and, in the smooth setting, is
stronger than nonpositive Ricci curvature.

The question "does no-conjugate-points imply nonpositive curvature?"
is a dimension-specific implication question in the theory of
Riemannian metrics on fixed topologies. In dimension two, the answer
is yes (a compact surface with no conjugate points for some metric
must admit a metric of nonpositive curvature, by the uniformization
theorem). The **novel contribution** is the **dimension-three
counterexample** [S3]: the implication fails in the first dimension
where it was open. The construction is a specific closed orientable
three-manifold (involving a punctured-torus building block [S3:40])
admitting a no-conjugate-points metric but no nonpositive-curvature
metric.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** As of my training
cutoff, the question of whether no-conjugate-points metrics imply
the existence of nonpositive-curvature metrics on the same manifold
was open in dimensions $\ge 3$ in the smooth setting. The
Cheeger–Gromov and Gromov–Lawson programs give obstructions to
nonpositive curvature via index and fundamental-group arguments, but
a direct construction separating the two metric-existence properties
in dimension three was the claimed novelty. I cannot verify the
Riemannian-geometric construction independently from training alone.

## consequences

- **The no-conjugate-points and nonpositive-curvature metric-existence
  properties are independent in dimension three**
  [S3:28–30, S2:816, S5:9]: the first does not imply the second,
  already for a closed orientable smooth three-manifold.
- **The construction is a specific manifold** [S3:28–30]: a closed,
  connected, orientable smooth three-manifold (with a
  punctured-torus building block in the construction [S3:40]).
- **The no-conjugate-points assertion is strong** [S5:9]: it ranges
  over all geodesics and Jacobi fields, including constant geodesics
  and unbounded time intervals.
- **The separate no-focal-points and CAT(0) consequences are not
  included** [S5:9]: the formalized result is the two-metric
  separation, not the stronger rigidity statements that would follow.
