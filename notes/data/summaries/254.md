> Artin $K(\pi,1)$

## undergraduate

An **Artin group** is built from a set of generators and a symmetric table of labels: for each pair of generators, a label says how long the alternating product of the two generators must be before it equals the reversed product. A label of 2 gives the braid relation, a label of 3 gives the braid relation on three letters, and an infinite label means no relation at all. When every label is 2, 3, 4, or 6, the group is a Coxeter group; Artin groups are the larger family that maps onto Coxeter groups.

The **Salvetti complex** is the standard topological space built from an Artin group: one cell for each spherical subset of generators, glued together. The **Artin $K(\pi,1)$ conjecture** says this complex is aspherical — its universal cover is contractible, so the group's higher homotopy vanishes and the complex is the classifying space for the group. It is open in general. [S11:11]

The first manuscript proves it. "{{3}}" The theorem covers every Coxeter matrix on a finite generating set, including empty and disconnected ones, and both finite and infinite labels. [S2:46–50]

The second manuscript is an obstruction in the other direction. "{{4}}" A CAT(0) space is a space with nonpositive curvature, where geodesic triangles are thinner than Euclidean ones; many groups act geometrically on such spaces, and this one provably cannot. [S4:8–12]

The third manuscript settles a structural conjecture about subgroups. "{{5}}" Parabolic subgroups are the standard subgroups generated by subsets of the generators, and the conjecture says intersections stay inside that family. [S6:51–55]

**Proof sketch.** [S3:22–30]

1. The theorem is stated for every Coxeter matrix on a finite set, with arbitrary labels, and concludes that the universal cover of the Salvetti complex is contractible. [S3:22–30]

## graduate

**The asphericity theorem.** For every Coxeter matrix $M$ on a finite generating set $S$, the universal cover of the Salvetti complex is contractible. The statement is unconditional on the labels — finite, infinite, connected, or disconnected diagrams are all covered. [S3:22–30]

**The CAT(0) obstruction.** There is an Artin matrix on 116 generators, with every off-diagonal label in $\{2,3,\infty\}$, such that the associated Artin group admits no geometric action: no proper, cocompact, isometric action on any nonempty proper CAT(0) space, in any dimension. [S5:61–66]

**The parabolic intersection theorem.** For every Coxeter matrix, every pair of subsets of generators, and every pair of group elements, the intersection of two conjugates of standard parabolic subgroups is itself a conjugate of a standard parabolic subgroup. [S7:21–28]

## history

The Artin $K(\pi,1)$ conjecture is the standard statement that the Salvetti complex is aspherical, posed for the class of Artin groups of finite type and extended to arbitrary Coxeter labels. The Parabolic Intersection Conjecture is the statement that parabolic subgroups are closed under intersection, posed for Artin groups of finite type. The release's blurb states both as conjectures the manuscripts prove, and the scope note records the formalized versions as covering every Coxeter matrix on a finite generating set. [S1:595]

The CAT(0) side sits in the geometric-action literature: the manuscript's README records the citation key `ArtinNoCAT0`, and the theorem is stated as a nonexistence over all nonempty proper CAT(0) spaces, in any dimension. [S9:11–12]

## consequences

The consequence the manuscripts state for themselves is that two long-standing structural statements are settled across the full class: asphericity for every finite-rank Artin group, and closure of parabolic subgroups under arbitrary intersections with arbitrary labels. [S2:46–50]

The CAT(0) result is a negative consequence: a specific Artin group is not geometrically bounded by nonpositive curvature, so the standard geometric tools for Artin groups do not apply to it. The scope note states the conclusion as excluding every nonempty proper CAT(0) space, in any dimension. [S11:17]

The formalization's record adds what the manuscripts' statements do not: the asphericity theorem is proved for the degenerate cases too, and the parabolic result extends to acylindrical hyperbolicity for irreducible Artin groups with an infinite Coxeter label. The CAT(0) manuscript is the one with no proved declaration, so its claim is readable only from the manuscript. [S11:19]
