## undergraduate

An **Artin group** is a generalization of a braid group: you start
with a diagram (a graph with labels on the edges) and define a group
from it. The **Salvetti complex** is a topological space built from
this diagram — think of it as the group's "classifying
space." The **$K(\pi,1)$ conjecture** asks: is this space
"aspherical" (its higher-dimensional holes are all trivial, so
it encodes only the group's fundamental group)? The first
manuscript proves **yes** for every finite-rank Artin group
[S3:47–49, S2:597]. The second manuscript shows that
not every Artin group can "act geometrically" on a
nonpositively curved space — a specific 116-generator
group cannot [S4:9–11]. The third manuscript proves that
intersections of "parabolic subgroups" (subgroups associated
to sub-diagrams) are again parabolic
[S5:52–54, S2:597].
