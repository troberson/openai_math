# 258 — Gersten's conjecture for one-relator groups

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

The release's blurb [S1:6349]:

> Proves Gersten's conjecture: every finitely generated one-relator group containing no Baumslag–Solitar subgroup $`\mathrm{BS}(m,n)`$, with $`m,n\ne0`$, is word-hyperbolic. It also proves that every word-hyperbolic one-relator group is virtually compact special.

The first manuscript's own blurb [S1:6358]:

> We prove Gersten's conjecture: every finitely generated one-relator group containing no subgroup isomorphic to a Baumslag–Solitar group $`\mathop{\mathrm{BS}}\nolimits (m,n)`$, for nonzero integers

The second manuscript's blurb [S1:6367]:

> We prove that every word-hyperbolic one-relator group is virtually compact special. Combined with the work of Kielak–Linton, this resolves Wise's virtual free-by-cyclic conjecture for hyperbolic one-relator groups: every such group is virtually free-by-cyclic, with the free kernel allowed to have infinite rank.

The catalog abstract [S2:603] says the same two things in one entry:
"Proves Gersten's conjecture: every finitely generated one-relator group containing no Baumslag--Solitar subgroup $\mathrm{BS}(m,n)$, with $m,n\ne0$, is word-hyperbolic." The manuscripts' own titles are
"Baumslag–Solitar-free one-relator groups are hyperbolic" [S3:1] and
"Virtual compact specialness of hyperbolic one-relator groups" [S4:1].

## Undergraduate summary

A one-relator group is a free group on finitely many generators modulo the normal closure of one word. The Baumslag–Solitar groups are the standard obstructions: "The Baumslag--Solitar groups" with $t a^m t^{-1}=a^n$ "are the basic obstructions to hyperbolicity among one-relator groups." [S6:4–10] Gersten asked in 1992 whether they are the only subgroup obstructions: "Gersten asked whether they are the only subgroup obstructions" [S6:11–12] The first manuscript answers that question: "We prove the following statement, resolving Gersten's conjecture positively." [S6:13]

The theorem is stated with no hypothesis on the relator: "Let $X$ be a finite set and let $r\in F(X)$. If the one-relator group $G=\langle X\mid r\rangle$ contains no subgroup isomorphic to $\mathrm{BS}(m,n)$ for any nonzero integers $m,n$, then $G$ is word-hyperbolic." [S6:18–21] The signs matter. "The parameters in this statement may have either sign. In particular, the hypothesis excludes $\mathrm{BS}(1,1)=\mathbb Z^2$. We call a group with this exclusion _Baumslag--Solitar-free_." [S6:24–26] The formulation that excludes only the $\operatorname{BS}(1,n)$ is equivalent, because every Baumslag–Solitar group contains one of those. [S6:27–29] The statement "includes trivial relators and proper powers; it has no torsion-free hypothesis." [S6:30–31]

The second manuscript proves a different property of the same class. Its theorem: a word-hyperbolic one-relator group "is word-hyperbolic. Then $G$ is virtually compact special. More precisely, there are a finite-index subgroup $H\leq G$, a finite connected nonpositively curved cube complex $C$, and a finite simplicial graph $\Lambda$ such that" the cube complex is isomorphic to $H$ and maps to the Salvetti complex of the right-angled Artin group defined by $\Lambda$. [S17:10–16] "The compactness assertion is part of the conclusion. An abstract embedding of a finite-index subgroup in a right-angled Artin group would not by itself supply the model required in" that theorem. [S17:19]

The consequence the release names is Wise's conjecture. "Wise's Conjecture~17.8 asks whether every hyperbolic one-relator group is virtually free-by-cyclic, as recalled by Kielak--Linton" [S17:79–80] Their Corollary 1.3 makes virtual compact specialness a sufficient additional hypothesis: "Their Corollary~1.3 identifies virtual compact specialness as a sufficient additional hypothesis." [S17:81–83] Combining it with the second manuscript's theorem gives a positive answer. The first manuscript records the same combination on its own side: "Thus $G$ is virtually free-by-cyclic, with the rank of $K$ allowed to be infinite and $Q$ allowed to be trivial." [S14:20–21] It states the stronger form too: "There are a finite-index subgroup $H\leq G$, an integer $d\geq0$, an automorphism $\alpha$ of the rank-$d$ free group $F_d$, and an embedding" of $H$ in a semidirect product of a finitely generated free group by $\mathbb Z$. [S14:10–14]

The cube-complex conclusion buys finite-quotient information. "Thus $G$ is linear over $\mathbb Z$ and residually finite. The conclusion concerns the whole group, including the one-relator groups with torsion; it is not merely a property of the chosen cover." [S29:20–23] It also gives conjugacy separability: "Minasyan--Zalesskii's theorem applies to every hyperbolic virtually compact special group, so $G$ is hereditarily conjugacy separable" [S29:35–36] and cohomological goodness — the comparison map from continuous cochains on the profinite completion to group cohomology is an isomorphism for every finite module. [S29:45–54]

## Graduate summary

**Manuscript 1: the combination theorem.** The technical core is a combination theorem for a restricted class of HNN extensions. "Let $H$ be a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group, let $A,B\leq H$ be finitely generated free quasiconvex subgroups, and let $\phi\colon A\to B$ be an isomorphism." [S8:13–15] Two hypotheses are imposed: conjugates of $A$ and $B$ intersect in at most a cyclic group outside the double coset, and the overlap subgroups satisfy a strong reduced-rank inequality summed over double cosets. [S8:21–32] Under them, "If $G$ contains no subgroup isomorphic to $BS(m,n)$ for any nonzero integers $m,n$, then $G$ is word-hyperbolic." [S8:33–34] The manuscript is explicit that these are assumptions, not consequences: "All the intersection restrictions in this statement are hypotheses. In particular, the assertion concerns a restricted class of HNN extensions." [S8:37–39]

The flaring criterion it uses is the algebraic form of Bestvina–Feighn: "Let $H$ be word-hyperbolic, let $A,B\leq H$ be finitely generated, and let $\phi:A\to B$ be an isomorphism such that both attaching maps $A\to H$ are quasi-isometric embeddings." [S8:249–251] "This is the algebraic formulation of the Bestvina--Feighn combination theorem in" Kapovich's explicit annular statement. [S8:260–262]

**Manuscript 1: the current argument.** Flaring is proved by contradiction. "We prove flaring by contradiction, using free-group currents: invariant measures on pairs of distinct boundary points" [S6:147–148] Long nonflaring annuli produce a bounded bilateral sequence of currents; atoms are removed, and a stationary current is produced: "Under the assumptions of Proposition~ , there exist a nonzero current $\nu$ on $I$ and a nonzero atomless current $\mu$ on $P$ such that" the two transfer maps agree with $\mu$. [S9:224–229] The path stabilizers of long windows are represented by finite core graphs, and the rank inequality bounds the number of edges once degree-two vertices are suppressed — it does not bound label lengths. [S6:153–157] Along a subsequence the bounded-label edges form one fixed graph, the small subgraph, and its noncyclic components give a finite cofinal family of carriers: "The noncyclic component subgroup classes of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{sm}}$ belong to $\mathcal C$ and form a finite cofinal subset: every $[Q]\in\mathcal C$ is contained, up to $P$-conjugacy, in one of them. In particular $\mathcal C$ has only finitely many maximal elements, and they are exactly the maximal members of this finite family." [S10:282–287] A return along a carrier induces an atoroidal automorphism, so expansion forces zero mass on each maximal carrier: "For every maximal carrier $M$ supplied by Proposition~ ," the line set has zero current mass. [S10:545–550] Zero mass does not remove a carrier line from the closed support, and that gap is the main issue of the expansion section. [S6:164–166] The section closes by excluding periodic support lines outright: "The support $\Omega$ contains no periodic line." [S11:547] The rays section then forces the special rays into the transfer domain: "For each fixed $s>n_0$, there is $N_s$ such that, whenever $N\geq N_s$, every line of $\Omega$ containing the directed segment $[r_s,r_N]$ contains $r_{n_0}$ and belongs to the domain of $\Theta$." [S12:176–178] A return at one of those endpoints puts infinitely many disjoint translates of a positive-mass set inside a compact set, contradicting local finiteness. [S6:185–191]

**Manuscript 1: the Magnus induction.** The combination theorem is applied to graph presentations. "Let $\Gamma$ be a finite connected graph, and let $\lambda\colon S^1\to\Gamma$ be a nontrivial immersed circuit representing a non-power in $\pi_1\Gamma$. If $\pi_1(\Gamma\cup_\lambda D^2)$ is $BS$-free, then it is word-hyperbolic." [S13:390–394] The induction runs on a lexicographic complexity, and the splitting is produced by a domain in a cyclic cover. [S13:398–405] The subgroup statement the induction needs is recorded separately: a Magnus subgraph's inclusion is injective by the Freiheitssatz, and its quasiconvexity follows from Linton's theorem once ambient hyperbolicity is known. [S6:215–219] The preliminaries carry the intersection, commensurator, and rank lemmas the combination theorem consumes. [S7]

**Manuscript 2: the filling criterion.** The geometric engine is a criterion for a group already known to be hyperbolic. "Let \(G_*\) be a torsion-free hyperbolic group satisfying the following conditions." [S18:36–37] It must be locally indicable, coherent, of type $F$, with integral cohomological dimension at most two; have a division ring satisfying the subgroup-field hypotheses with acyclic chains over it; and admit arbitrarily deep peripheral fillings along a finite malnormal family of quasiconvex hyperbolic virtually compact special subgroups, the fillings and their inherited peripheral images both hyperbolic and virtually compact special. [S18:39–51] "In \textup{(iii)}, arbitrarily deep means that the peripheral kernels can avoid any prescribed finite subset of" the nonidentity elements of the peripherals. [S18:53–54] The criterion is applied to the primitive extension group after the tower is rebuilt.

**Manuscript 2: rows, gates, closure, transfer, boundary.** The reduction is Linton's: "it suffices to prove that every torsion-free hyperbolic primitive extension group in the two forms of" Linton's Section 5 is virtually compact special. [S17:258] The vertex and edge spaces at specified heights in the developed Bass–Serre tree are the rows, and long paths with noncyclic stabilizers are controlled by a joint injection into one finite-dimensional homology space: "In \(T\) and \(T_0\), every path with noncyclic pointwise stabilizer is monotone." [S20:365–366] Expansion gates capture the periodic ends: "Unless $G$ is already virtually compact special by the acylindrical case, there is an invariant system of actual objects, finite modulo $G$, with full stabilizers $A$ and kernels $S=A\cap L$, having these properties:" [S23:1019–1021] Cyclic closure and end enlargement then terminate: "Starting with the system of Proposition~ , finitely many cyclic-closure and end-enlargement rounds produce an admissible strict malnormal quasiconvex virtually compact special system $\{D_d\}$, with $S_d=D_d\cap L$, and a folded tree $\widehat T$" with the captured stabilizers still detected and the joint homology budget at most $k-1$. [S24:1102–1110] Exact intersection transfer makes the filled splitting acylindrical: "Before filling, Lemma~  gives exact relative embeddings for finite-path stabilizers into the incident rows." [S25:543–544] The coefficient and analytic sections keep the joint injections alive through the fillings, and the kernel and fan sections build the height-fiber kernels and the panel attachments. [S21] [S22] [S26] [S27] The boundary section separates boundary pairs and closes the argument: "For the terminal row and peripheral data constructed in Sections~ -- , one can choose the height-fiber kernels arbitrarily deep, simultaneously satisfying the finite-path tests, so that every filled vertex group $\bar J$ is hyperbolic and virtually compact special." [S28:927–931] The final theorem is the primitive case: "Every hyperbolic torsion-free primitive extension group of either of Linton's two forms specified in the introduction is virtually compact special." [S28:984–986]

**The two manuscripts are companions.** Each cites the other. The hyperbolicity manuscript's bibliography names the specialness paper: "Virtual compact specialness of hyperbolic one-relator groups" [S15:297], and its introduction says the companion theorem "provides that hypothesis." [S6:43–44] The specialness manuscript's bibliography names the hyperbolicity paper: "Baumslag--Solitar-free one-relator groups are hyperbolic," [S30:359] and its introduction is explicit about what it borrows: "In the proof we use the hyperbolicity and Magnus-subgroup results of   at two specific points: embedded primitive extension groups are themselves hyperbolic, and their actual Magnus rows have the required hyperbolic geometry. The criterion in that paper excludes every Baumslag--Solitar subgroup $\mathrm{BS}(m,n)$ with $mn\ne0$. No virtual-specialness conclusion is imported from it." [S17:89–94] The dependency is one-directional in the proofs: hyperbolicity feeds specialness, and the free-by-cyclic corollary on the hyperbolicity side consumes the specialness theorem. [S6:43–48]

## History of the conjecture

The classical theory already split the torsion case off. "The classical theory already separates the torsion case from the main difficulty. Magnus's Freiheitssatz identifies free subgroups generated by suitable proper subsets of a one-relator generating set" [S6:52–54] and "Newman's spelling theorem for proper-power relators gives a Dehn algorithm and hence hyperbolicity" [S6:55–56] So the new hyperbolicity assertion concerns non-power relators, although the theorem covers all relators. [S6:58–60] The inductive route is the Magnus–Moldavanskii hierarchy, realized geometrically through domains in cyclic covers. [S6:62–65]

The recent results the manuscript positions itself against are named. "Several recent results identify substantial classes where this induction preserves hyperbolicity. Louder and Wilton characterized negative immersions for one-relator presentation complexes by primitivity rank greater than two" [S6:69–72] That characterization leaves the rank-two case open: "This characterization does not settle hyperbolicity alone for Baumslag--Solitar-free groups whose relator has primitivity rank two." [S6:90–92] Linton's hierarchy criterion sharpens the obstruction: "Linton's hierarchy criterion makes the remaining obstruction more specific: a one-relator hierarchy is quasiconvex with hyperbolic ambient group if and only if it is $\mathbb Z$-stable and the group is Baumslag--Solitar-free" [S6:94–98] and the stability hypothesis is "an additional condition, not a consequence supplied by that criterion." [S6:99–101] A complementary reduction isolates primitive-extension groups as the terminal class. [S6:102–105] On the expansion side the manuscript cites Bestvina–Feighn, Kapovich's annular formulation, Brinkmann's automorphism criterion, and Mutanguha's ascending-torus criterion: "Mutanguha extended the hyperbolicity criterion to ascending HNN extensions of finite-rank free groups: they are hyperbolic exactly when they contain no Baumslag--Solitar subgroup" [S6:119–122]

The second manuscript's history runs through residual finiteness and cubulation. "A second line of inquiry concerns finite quotients. A group is _residually finite_ if each nonidentity element survives in some finite quotient." [S17:33–35] "Baumslag's 1967 Conjecture~A asks for this property for one-relator groups with nontrivial torsion" [S17:35–36] and Wise's hierarchy theory already proved virtual compact specialness in the torsion case. [S17:38–40] The cubical machinery is Haglund–Wise, Agol, and Bergeron–Wise: "Wise's hierarchy theorem makes quasiconvex splittings a way to obtain this structure \cite[Theorem~13.1]{Wise2021}; Agol's theorem supplies it for hyperbolic groups acting properly and cocompactly on CAT(0) cube complexes" [S17:48–51] In the torsion-free direction Linton had proved hyperbolicity and virtual specialness when all two-generator subgroups are free. [S17:57–59] Wise's Problem 13.15 singled out hyperbolic one-relator groups. [S17:67–69] Hagen and Wise posed the cubulation problem for ascending HNN extensions defined by general monomorphisms [S17:126–127], and Kielak's virtual-fibering method realizes division-field coefficients along an RFRS filtration [S17:152–154] — a method the manuscript cannot use directly, because "Here the original group is not yet known to be virtually RFRS: the selective switch supplies the required finite coefficient data before the two-sided Novikov argument." [S17:156–158] Its closing line names the two open questions it answers: the negative answer to the question about nonlinear ascending HNN extensions [S29:27–29] and the fibering conclusion for ascending tori. [S17:132–134]

Prior (my training data, not the manuscript's record): Gersten's 1992 problem was the standard open case in one-relator geometry — the Baumslag–Solitar subgroups were known to be the only obstructions in the torsion case, for proper-power relators, and in several restricted classes, but the general conjecture was open. Wise's virtual free-by-cyclic conjecture was the parallel open problem for hyperbolic one-relator groups, and virtual compact specialness was the known sufficient route to it. As far as my prior goes, neither had been settled for the full class. The manuscript's own citations are the record; this paragraph is my prior on the problems' standing, and the manuscripts do not state it.

## Consequences

Gersten's conjecture is true: the Baumslag–Solitar subgroups are the only subgroup obstructions to hyperbolicity among finitely generated one-relator groups. [S6:18–21] Every word-hyperbolic one-relator group is virtually compact special, and that buys integral linearity, residual finiteness, hereditary conjugacy separability, and cohomological goodness for the whole group, torsion included. [S29:20–23] [S29:35–36] [S29:45–54] Combining the two manuscripts with Kielak–Linton resolves Wise's Conjecture 17.8 for this class, with the free kernel allowed infinite rank. [S17:81–84] [S14:20–21] The ascending case is settled in the same paper, answering Hagen–Wise's Problem C for general monomorphisms and Drutu–Sapir's Problem 6 in the negative. [S19:17–18] [S17:126–127] [S29:27–29]

Open directions the manuscripts leave. The combination theorem is stated for a restricted class of HNN extensions, and the manuscript says plainly that the intersection restrictions are hypotheses. [S8:37–39] The free kernel in the free-by-cyclic conclusion is not asserted to be Kielak–Linton's canonical height kernel. [S14:64] The canonical kernel of a strict ascending torus is presented as an increasing union of free groups, and "freeness of that union is not an input to our argument." [S17:140–141] The fibering character produced for the ascending torus is "not identified with the canonical ascending height." [S17:148] The primitive reduction covers Linton's two specified forms, so the conclusion is delivered through that reduction, not as a classification of one-relator groups. [S17:257–258]
