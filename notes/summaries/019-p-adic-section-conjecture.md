# 019 — The $p$-adic section conjecture

[Index page](../index/number-theory/019-p-adic-section-conjecture.md)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary



> **019. The local *p*-adic section conjecture and global consequences.** Proves that rational points on every smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus at least two over a finite extension of ℚ*p* correspond bijectively to conjugacy classes of sections of its full arithmetic étale fundamental group. It also proves Grothendieck's section conjecture over ℚ for the modular curves $`X_0(N)`$ and $`X_1(N)`$ of genus at least two. [S1:529]

The blurb compresses two manuscripts into one sentence, and only the second states the conjecture. The first is a geometric construction: 

> Let *X* be a smooth proper hyperbolic curve over an algebraic closure of a *p*-adic local field. Given finitely many disjoint small open disks, we construct one connected finite étale cover with a sheet isomorphic to their entire exterior and with degree divisible by *p* on every connected component above each disk. [S1:538]



> We prove the local *p*-adic section conjecture for smooth proper geometrically connected curves of genus at least two, over every finite extension of ℚ*p*. Combined with established finite-descent theorems, this also yields the global section conjecture for smooth proper geometrically connected curves of genus at least two over number fields when their finite-cover descent locus equals their rational points; this includes $`X_0(N)`$ and $`X_1(N)`$ of genus at least two over ℚ. [S1:547]

## Undergraduate summary

Grothendieck attached a conjecture to a letter he wrote to Faltings on 27 June 1983, and this family proves its local version. Over a number field, the conjecture says that the splittings of a curve's arithmetic fundamental group come only from rational points. Over a $p$-adic field, the same statement is asked of the full arithmetic étale fundamental group of a curve of genus at least two. The manuscript states the local claim plainly “The local $p$-adic section conjecture asserts that this construction accounts for all sections, and distinguishes all rational points. [S13:16–18]” and explains what kind of section is being hunted down “A section whose class comes from a rational point is called _geometric_. The conjecture thus asks whether rational points can be recovered from the splittings of the arithmetic fundamental group. [S13:20–22]”

The family's two manuscripts play different roles, and the release presents them in the order of construction. The first builds the geometry a section needs: “Let $X$ be a smooth proper hyperbolic curve over an algebraic closure of a $p$-adic local field. Given finitely many disjoint small open disks, we construct one connected finite étale cover with a sheet isomorphic to their entire exterior and with degree divisible by $p$ on every connected component above each disk. [S4:28–32]” The second manuscript is the one that proves the bijection, and it uses the standard patching machinery to turn analytic covers into algebraic ones.

The reason a $p$-adic statement is not a formal corollary of the number-field one is that the local field has valuations to spare. A section of the arithmetic fundamental group can be localized at a valuation of the function field, and over $\mathbb Q_p$ there are many rank-one valuations extending the $p$-adic one, including valuations whose center is a closed point of the curve rather than a rational point. The proof has to show that the sections coming from those extra valuations do not exist, which is where the geometry of the first manuscript is used.

**Proof sketch (the section-conjecture manuscript).** Start with the compositum $\widetilde K$ of the function fields of all connected finite étale covers of the curve, so that $\Gal(\widetilde K/K)=\Pi_X$. Enlarge it to a field $L_\ell$ controlled by one auxiliary odd prime $\ell\ne p$, and prove in two stages that $L_\ell$, and then all of $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$, embed in the completion $H$ of $\widetilde K$ at a chosen valuation. Each stage needs roots of local coordinates on annuli, and the roots must live on every sheet above the annulus, which is exactly the shape of cover the first manuscript produces. Once $K^{\mathrm{sep}}\subset H$, a section localized at a rank-one valuation is forced into a decomposition group, the two-valuation principle kills the rank-one case, the type-$2h$ case is pinned to a rational closed point, and injectivity follows from a Kummer argument on the Jacobian.

## Graduate summary

The theorem is the local bijection “For every prime $p$, every finite extension $k/\mathbb Q_p$, and every smooth proper geometrically connected curve $X/k$ of genus at least two, the map [S13:32–34]” with the quotient taken as stated “is bijective. The quotient is by conjugation by the full geometric étale fundamental group. [S13:42–43]” The group involved is the full arithmetic group $\Pi_X=\pi_1(X)$ fitting into $1\to\Delta_X\to\Pi_X\to G_k\to1$, not a quotient of it.

The proof runs through a completion, in eight moves.

1. Fix the field data: $K=k(X)$, a separable closure, and $\widetilde K$ as the compositum of the function fields of all connected finite étale covers, so that $\Gal(\widetilde K/K)=\Pi_X$ after the base point identification [S13:100–104]. Norms are normalized by $|p|=p^{-1}$ [S13:105–107].
2. Introduce one auxiliary odd prime $\ell\ne p$ and the field $L_\ell$ built from extensions whose horizontal ramification indices divide $\ell$ [S14:38–40], noting that the universal étale field is unchanged by adding constants [S14:41–42]. Horizontal ramification is a separate bookkeeping device from wild ramification on a reduction model [S14:31–33].
3. Prove the first stage: patching over collars whose full inverse images carry $\ell$-th roots of the branch coordinates puts $L_\ell$ inside $H$ [S16:296–298][S16:286], with the algebraization step resting on base-extension invariance of finite étale covers [S16:306].
4. The patching proposition is the whole engine, and its hypothesis is a root on the whole inverse image of an annulus [S15:69–71], producing a curve over the target field with a map to the cover over the exterior of the small disks [S15:79–80].
5. Prove the second stage with an auxiliary branched cover of the line [S17:66–72], built from $y^\ell=T(T-1)/(T-\alpha)$, whose genus is computed as “2g(Z)-2=-2\ell+4(\ell-1),\qquad g(Z)=\ell-1. [S17:85]” Its horizontal indices divide $\ell$, so the first stage already accommodates it [S18:4–9], and roots pull back to every sheet of the normalized component [S18:40–42].
6. Conclude the containment “Then the specified subfield $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ of $E$ is contained in $H$. [S13:123]”, which identifies decomposition groups in the two towers [S19:17–18].
7. Rule out the rank-one localization: the projection of a decomposition group onto $G_k$ “has no continuous group-homomorphic section. [S19:61]”, so rank one is excluded “Rank one is excluded by Lemma  .  If $w$ has rank two, let $u$ be [S19:147–148]”, the rank-two case reduces to a closed-point valuation refined by the $p$-adic one, and surjectivity closes with the type-$2h$ conclusion “Equivalently, the type-$2h$ conclusion of \cite[preprint version, Section 6.1]PopStix gives $s=s_a$ after the corresponding geometric choice of base point.  This proves surjectivity. [S19:175–177]” The two-valuation input is an explicit F. K. Schmidt argument, including $p=2$ [S13:139–142].
8. Prove injectivity by Kummer theory on the Jacobian: the intersection $\bigcap nJ(k)$ vanishes because $J(k)$ has an open subgroup “The compact $p$-adic analytic group $J(k)$ has an open subgroup $U\cong\mathbb Z_p^{g(X)[k:\mathbb Q_p]}$ by the local logarithm. [S19:206–207]”, and a nonzero difference would give a degree-one map to the line “and hence $i_a(b)=0$.  If $a\ne b$, the linear equivalence of the degree-one divisors $a$ and $b$ would give a degree-one morphism $X\to\mathbb P^1$, forcing genus zero.  Hence $a=b$, as required. [S19:215–217]”

The companion manuscript supplies the geometric input, and it states its own theorem with the disk condition unweakened. The setup fixes disjoint open disks $V_i$ and the complement $U$ [S5:24–25], with the valuation normalized by “The additive valuation is normalized by $v(p)=1$, so that $v(\Omega^\times)=\mathbb Q$. [S5:7–8]”, and the conclusion is “there is a smooth proper connected curve $Y/\bar k$ and a finite étale surjective morphism $g:Y\to X$ such that: [S5:30–32]” with the two required behaviors [S5:34–36] “a connected component of $g^{-1}(U)$ maps isomorphically to $U$; for every $i$, every connected component of $g^{-1}(V_i)$ maps to $V_i$ with degree divisible by $p$.” The manuscript is explicit that the disks are not shrunk “The disks are the prescribed $V_i$; they are not shrunk in the conclusion. [S5:39–40]”

Its construction is a chain of four steps. Resolution of nonsingularities is the geometric input, in the lineage of Tamagawa and Lepage “Resolution of nonsingularities seeks finite étale covers whose stable reductions acquire components above prescribed points of a model. Tamagawa proved this for points of a stable marked model in mixed characteristic with residue field algebraic over a finite field [S5:48–51]”, and the positive-genus form it uses is the Mochizuki–Tsujimura theorem “The theorem of Mochizuki–Tsujimura provides the positive-genus formulation for arbitrary hyperbolic curves over $p$-adic local fields: a stable component with positive-genus normalization can be placed above any prescribed closed point of a model [S5:54–57]”, leaving the simultaneous requirement as the new part “This is our geometric input; the additional task is to impose the exterior and disk conditions simultaneously on one cover. [S5:59–60]” Two primes then do different jobs “Beyond the resolution covers, the construction uses two primes with different roles. In Section 2, positive-genus components give skeletal paths above each fixed disk, using the model–skeleton correspondence and retractions described by Baker–Payne–Rabinoff [S5:62–66]” and “An auxiliary prime $\ell\ne p$ supplies residue-component torsors: above each path the edges multiply faster than the endpoint vertices, forcing a cycle. [S5:67–69]” The cycle is produced as a proposition “There is a connected finite étale Galois cover $Z\to X$ whose stable skeleton contains, for each $i$, an embedded circle lying entirely above $V_i$. [S6:60–62]”, detected by cyclic graph covers “For every $i$ there is a connected cyclic finite étale cover of $Z$ of degree $p$ which is trivial over all of $U_Z$ and whose restriction to at least one component above $V_i$ is connected. [S7:8–10]”, and assembled by a quotient “The group $P$ is elementary abelian of exponent $p$, since it embeds in the product of the cyclic deck groups. [S8:12–13]” whose output is the required cover “The curve $Y$ is smooth, proper and connected, and the induced map $g:Y\to X$ is finite étale. [S8:51–52]”, with the divisibility coming from a rank count [S8:33–35] and the graph-cover connectivity from [S7:41–45].

The global half of the second manuscript is a criterion, not a theorem about every curve. It identifies the localization image “The finite-cover descent locus consists of the tuples that lift to some global twist of each torsor under a finite $F$-group scheme. Proposition   shows that this locus is exactly the image of global sections under localization. [S13:235–238]” and then, when that locus is exactly the rational points, quotes the finite-support theorem to get the bijection “When this finite-cover locus consists exactly of the diagonal rational points, Stix's finite-support theorem identifies every global section with a point section \cite[arXiv version, Corollary 5]StixFiniteSupport. The resulting bijection is Theorem  . [S13:242–246]” The criterion is stated as “then $\kappa_C:C(K)\to\mathrm{Sec}(C/K)$ is bijective. [S20:128]” under the displayed equality at 126.

What the family does not claim is recorded in the manuscript itself. The finite-cover equality stays a hypothesis “The general implication retains the finite-cover equality as a genuine arithmetic hypothesis; local point-theoreticity alone does not supply it. [S20:229–231]”, the localization statements are explicitly not injectivity or finiteness results “This asserts neither injectivity of localization nor finiteness of the global section set, and supplies no effective or uniform bound. [S20:275–276]”, and the surjectivity argument refuses to use an unrestricted equivalence between the type-$2h$ condition and non-rationality “Notice that a type-$2h$ valuation can lie over a non-rational closed point.  Rationality here follows from the surjection to $G_k$; no unrestricted equivalence between ``not type $2h$'' and ``not originating from a rational point'' is used. [S19:179–182]”

## History of the conjecture

The manuscript dates the conjecture itself “It is a local analogue of the section conjecture in Grothendieck's letter to Faltings of 27 June 1983, formulated over fields finitely generated over the prime field [S13:23–25]”, and it dates the local program precisely. Koenigsmann's theorem is the birational precedent “Koenigsmann proves that every birational section over a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ is contained in the decomposition group of a rational point [S13:53–55]”, and the manuscript is careful that the birational statement is not the étale one [S13:61–63]. Pop--Stix supply the localization step that reduces the question to valuations “Pop–Stix localize every étale section at a valuation extending the $p$-adic valuation \cite[preprint version, Theorem 26]PopStix. This reduces the problem to understanding which valuations can support [S13:65–67]”, Pajwani develops the valuative approach through étale homotopy and fixed points in Berkovich spaces [S13:68–70], and the global side builds on Pop's birational criterion [S13:56–59], the Harari–Stix descent bridge [S13:239–240], Stix's finite-support theorem , Bresciani's real section theorem [S13:232–233], Betts–Stix on finiteness of localizations [S13:267–269], and Stoll's finite-descent criteria [S13:247–254]. The gluing method is placed in the Harbater and Liu patching tradition [S13:169–172].

The companion manuscript's lineage is the resolution-of-nonsingularities line: Tamagawa for stable marked models in mixed characteristic, Lepage for semistable models of Mumford curves, and Mochizuki–Tsujimura for the positive-genus formulation over $p$-adic local fields [S5:48–58], with the model-skeleton machinery attributed to Baker–Payne–Rabinoff [S5:62–67] and the algebraization to GAGA and SGA1 [S5:76–80].

Prior (my training data, not this repo). Grothendieck's letter to Faltings of 27 June 1983 circulated as a manuscript and was published in 2023 in the collected correspondence; the section conjecture is Conjecture 1.2 there, and it has been proved for abelian varieties by Pop and Stix, while for curves it is open over number fields even for genus two. Koenigsmann's 2014 paper proved the birational section conjecture over $p$-adic fields, and Stix proved the section conjecture for curves with a solvable Jacobian action. The local $p$-adic section conjecture for the full arithmetic fundamental group, over every finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ and every genus-$\ge2$ curve, is not a statement I can name in the literature I remember; the closest published forms I recall are the birational conjecture and the valuative restrictions of Pop–Stix and Pajwani. That is a prior, not a citation, and the manuscript's own bibliography is the record.

## Consequences

The local theorem is what makes the global criterion usable, and the manuscript's corollaries are arithmetic in kind. For an abelian target the criterion applies under Stoll's hypotheses [S20:167–170] “there is a nonconstant $K$-morphism $C\to A$ to an abelian variety with $A(K)$ finite and $\mathrm{Sha}(A/K)_{\mathrm{div}}=0$; the Jacobian $J$ of $C$ has $J(K)$ finite and $\mathrm{Sha}(J/K)_{\mathrm{div}}=0$.” For an elliptic target it applies through the release's own companion result “Suppose that there is a nonconstant $\mathbb Q$-morphism $C\to E$ to an elliptic curve and that $s_p(E)=0$ for at least one prime $p$. Then $\kappa_C$ is bijective. [S20:196–198]”, whose proof cites the companion article directly “The corank-zero case of the companion Selmer converse \cite[Theorem 1.1]OpenAISelmerConverse2026 gives $\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb Z}E(\mathbb Q)=0$ and finiteness of the entire group $\mathrm{Sha}(E/\mathbb Q)$. The Mordell–Weil [S20:203–206]”. The modular-curve case is the headline global claim “For every positive integer $N$ such that the smooth projective geometrically connected curve $X_0(N)/\mathbb Q$ has genus at least two, the map $\kappa_{X_0(N)}$ is bijective. The same assertion holds for the smooth projective geometrically connected curve $X_1(N)/\mathbb Q$ whenever its genus is at least two. [S20:213–217]”, and it comes from Stoll's descent result rather than from the elliptic specialization [S20:229–233].

The citation is a real edge inside the release, and it runs one way. The bibliography entry is [S21:337–343], whose title line reads “title = The Selmer converse for elliptic curves at every prime, [S21:339]”, pointing at the family 002 manuscript `The-Selmer-converse-for-elliptic-curves-at-every-prime-October-7-2026/`; that manuscript in turn cites family 006, so the chain is 019 → 002 → 006, and all three families are T0. The introduction states the same dependency “The Selmer converse in the companion article \cite[Theorem 1.1]OpenAISelmerConverse2026 gives a further arithmetic test for an elliptic target over $\mathbb Q$: vanishing of its full $p$-power Selmer corank at one prime. [S13:255–258]” The first manuscript cites nothing in the corpus: its nine-entry bibliography has no release key [S9], so the chain leaves 019 through the second manuscript only. A chain of unformalized links is only as strong as its weakest, and here every link is unformalized, so the chain is a reading dependency, not a machine-checked one. See [002 — The full Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer formula in Selmer coranks zero and one](002-full-birch-swinnerton-dyer-formula-in-selmer-coranks-zero-and-one.md) and [006 — Goldfeld's conjecture](006-goldfelds-conjecture.md), the two pages this chain runs through.

The release presents the two as one family, and in the first edition both were dated the same day; the section-conjecture manuscript now ships as a re-issue, so the construction README carries “**Date:** September 24, 2026 [S3:5]” while the theorem's README carries “**Date:** October 6, 2026 [S11:5]”, with the theorem's abstract stating the two halves “We prove the local $p$-adic section conjecture for smooth proper geometrically connected curves of genus at least two, over every finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$. [S12:32–34]” and “Combined with established finite-descent theorems, this also yields the global section conjecture for smooth proper geometrically connected curves of genus at least two over number fields when their finite-cover descent locus equals their rational points; this includes $X_0(N)$ and $X_1(N)$ of genus at least two over $\mathbb Q$. [S12:35–39]” The release blurb in `overview.tex` says the same in one sentence “Proves that rational points on every smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus at least two over a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ correspond bijectively to conjugacy classes of sections of its full arithmetic étale fundamental group. [S2:114]”
