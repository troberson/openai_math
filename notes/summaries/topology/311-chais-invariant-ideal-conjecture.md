# 311 — Chai's invariant-ideal conjecture

[Index page](../../index/topology/311-chais-invariant-ideal-conjecture.md)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

The release's blurb [S1:7667] and the catalog abstract [S2:716] state
the family's claim: Chai's invariant-ideal conjecture is proved for
Lubin--Tate deformation rings over finite residue fields, at every
prime and positive height, and through the Barthel--Heard--Naumann
implication this proves the Hovey--Strickland conjecture — the
dualizable $K(n)$-local category has exactly $n+2$ thick tensor ideals,
and its Balmer spectrum is a chain of $n+1$ points. The manuscript's
abstract "We classify the prime and radical ideals of the Lubin–Tate deformation
ring invariant under an open Morava stabilizer subgroup, at every prime
and positive height.  This proves Chai's invariant-ideal conjecture in
the standard finite-residue-field formulation.  It also proves the
Hovey–Strickland conjecture: the dualizable \(K(n)\)-local category has
exactly \(n+2\) thick tensor ideals, and its Balmer spectrum is a chain
of \(n+1\) points. [S3:53–61]"

## Undergraduate summary

Chromatic height organizes two worlds at once: the deformation theory
of formal groups, and the tensor structure of stable homotopy theory.
Two separate classification problems ask whether height accounts for
all of the relevant closed subobjects in each world. The first is an
*arithmetic* question about the Lubin--Tate deformation ring
$E_0$: which prime and radical ideals are left fixed when the Morava
stabilizer group changes the marking of the formal group? The second
is a *spectral* question: which thick tensor ideals sit inside the
category of dualizable $K(n)$-local spectra? The manuscript proves
both classifications, and uses the arithmetic one as the main step
toward the spectral one [S4:3–24]:

> **theorem**
For every prime \(p\) and every \(n\geq 1\), the thick tensor ideals of
\(\mathcal D_{(n,p)}\) are exactly
\[
 \mathcal D_{(n,p)}=\mathcal D_0
 \supsetneq\mathcal D_1\supsetneq\cdots
 \supsetneq\mathcal D_n\supsetneq\mathcal D_{n+1}=0.
\]
In particular, there are \(n+2\) such ideals, and each \(\mathcal D_k\)
is independent of the chosen finite type-\(k\) spectrum. [S4:74–84]

In plain terms: the spectral classification reduces to the arithmetic
one, and once you know exactly which height-ideals are
stabilizer-invariant, the list of thick tensor ideals — a strict chain
of $n+2$ members from the whole category down to zero — follows.

## Graduate summary

**The arithmetic theorem (Chai's Hope).** For any open subgroup
$U\subseteq A_n^\times$ of the nonextended Morava stabilizer, the
proper prime ideals of $E_0$ stabilized by $U$ are precisely the height
ideals $0, I_1, \ldots, I_n$, and the $U$-invariant radical ideals are
precisely those plus the unit ideal $E_0$. The same list is invariant
under the full canonical $G_n$-action. This proves the invariant-ideal
assertion called "Chai's Hope" in the finite-residue-field formulation
of Barthel--Heard--Naumann, and extends that formulation from the full
stabilizer to every open subgroup [S4:26–38].

**The spectral theorem (Hovey--Strickland).** Over Honda Morava
$E$-theory with coefficient ring $E_0$, the thick tensor ideals of the
dualizable $K(n)$-local homotopy category $\mathcal D_{(n,p)}$ are
exactly the chain $\mathcal D_0 \supsetneq \mathcal D_1 \supsetneq
\cdots \supsetneq \mathcal D_n \supsetneq \mathcal D_{n+1}=0$, where
$\mathcal D_k$ is the thick tensor ideal generated by a finite
type-$k$ spectrum. There are $n+2$ such ideals, each independent of the
generator chosen, and the Balmer spectrum is the chain of $n+1$ proper
members [S4:74–84].

**The argument.** The load-bearing step is a *local orbit density*
result: a nonzero analytic germ at a characteristic-$p$ point of
formal height $h$ on the height locus $I_h=0$ cannot vanish on the
stabilizer orbit of that point. The proof builds the $\ell$-adic
functions from the etale Tate module specialized at nearby parameters,
shows their parameter differential is invertible (fixed-height
rigidity plus the Kodaira--Spencer map), and then, via stable-transport
comparisons of multiplication by $p$, turns a hypothetical vanishing
germ into a nonzero formal equation on the subgroup $S_x$. The
formal-closure argument produces a nonzero row of Honda
endomorphisms annihilating $S_x$, and the Fargues--Fontaine curve with
Le Bras's Banach--Colmez sheaf theorem turns that relation into a
vector-bundle relation whose degree-one determinant contradicts the
$\Q_p$-independence of the basis vectors. Characteristic-zero primes
are handled separately by the Gross--Hopkins period map, and the
established support comparison transfers the height chain to thick
tensor ideals and their Balmer spectrum [S4:162–242].

## History of the conjecture

The Hovey--Strickland conjecture is the dualizable $K(n)$-local
counterpart to the Hopkins--Smith thick-subcategory classification of
finite spectra, proposed in their study of Morava $K$-theory and
localization. Hovey--Strickland's Problem 16.8 also asks directly
about stabilizer-invariant primes and radical ideals of the
deformation ring. Chai studied the stabilizer action on the closed
fiber of Lubin--Tate space and proposed that invariant irreducible
formal loci should be height loci. Barthel, Heard, and Naumann proved
the Hovey--Strickland conjecture at height two for every prime and
established the general implication from Chai's invariant-ideal
assertion to the spectral classification, building on Mathew's
descent and Balmer's tensor-nilpotence criterion. Recent
generation-and-comparison results of Lee--Pstrągowski, and of
Balmer--Sanders, address related categories; the arithmetic theorem of
this manuscript classifies the invariant supports needed to determine
the entire ideal lattice [S4:94–158].

## Consequences

**Chai's invariant-ideal conjecture is resolved.** For any open
stabilizer subgroup at every prime and positive height, the
stabilizer-invariant prime and radical ideals of the Lubin--Tate
deformation ring are exactly the height ideals (plus the unit ideal),
proving "Chai's Hope" in the finite-residue-field formulation and
extending it from the full stabilizer to every open subgroup
[S4:26–38].

**The Hovey--Strickland conjecture is resolved.** The dualizable
$K(n)$-local category has exactly $n+2$ thick tensor ideals, forming a
strict chain from the whole category to zero, each independent of the
finite type-$k$ generator, and the Balmer spectrum is a chain of $n+1$
points [S4:74–84].

**The support map is identified.** Each height stratum of
$\Spec(E_0)$ maps to a single Balmer point, so the arithmetic
classification pins down the support map itself [S3:53–61].
