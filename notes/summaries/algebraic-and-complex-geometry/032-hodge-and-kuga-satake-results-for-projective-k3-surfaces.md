# 032 — Hodge and Kuga–Satake results for all projective K3 surfaces (Algebraic and complex geometry, 8 manuscripts, tier T0)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

Family 032 opens the catalog's Algebraic and complex geometry section, its third section [S2:140–142]. Its entry is a heading line carrying the family title and a one-paragraph summary, then one table row per manuscript — a link line and a blurb [S1:755–829]. The rows run products, all-surfaces, CM, locus, Weil classes, Gale, eightfolds, conditional reduction, so the CM blurb is the third [S1:782]:

> We prove the rational Hodge conjecture for complex abelian varieties with complex multiplication: every rational Hodge class on such a variety is a rational linear combination of algebraic cycle classes. As consequences, we obtain the generalized Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties, the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields, and the Hodge standard conjecture for abelian varieties in arbitrary characteristic.

The other seven blurbs, one line each in the same entry:

- Products of K3 surfaces [S1:764]: "We prove the rational Hodge conjecture for every finite product of projective complex K3 surfaces: every rational Hodge class is algebraic. The factors may be distinct or repeated, with no restrictions on their Picard numbers, periods, or endomorphism fields."

- Kuga–Satake algebraicity for every K3 surface [S1:773]: "We prove that the Kuga–Satake correspondence is algebraic for every smooth projective complex K3 surface. More precisely, the prescribed embedding of its transcendental cohomology into the cohomology of its Kuga–Satake abelian variety is induced by a rational algebraic cycle, with the fixed normalization and full even-Clifford target. The result also holds for isogenous Kuga–Satake models with the transported embedding."

- The K3 quadratic locus [S1:791]: "We prove the rational Hodge and generalized Hodge conjectures in every cohomological degree of every self-power of a projective complex K3 surface whose transcendental quadratic space, with its cup-product form, admits a rational isometric embedding in $`\mathbb U_{\mathbb Q}^{\oplus2}\perp\langle-1\rangle^4`$. This includes every ample *P*-polarized K3 surface, including all Picard jumps, for $`P=\mathbb U\oplus D_8(-1)\oplus D_4(-1)`$. On this locus we construct algebraic correspondences inducing every prescribed standard even-Clifford Kuga–Satake tensor. More generally, for any projective complex K3 surface, algebraicity of one exact standard even-Clifford Kuga–Satake tensor implies both conjectures for every self-power."

- Weil classes on abelian powers [S1:800]: "We prove the rational Hodge conjecture in every codimension on every self-power of a complex abelian sixfold with an imaginary-quadratic action and a compatible polarization whose rational homological Hermitian form is hyperbolic of signature $`(3,3)`$. We also prove it in every codimension on every self-power of a complex abelian variety of dimension at most five admitting an imaginary-quadratic action. Both results include nonsimple varieties and special periods with additional endomorphisms. The proof uses the companion theorem on the rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties."

- Abelian-cover Jacobians [S1:809]: "We prove the rational Hodge conjecture on every self-power of the Jacobian at each tensor Hodge-generic point of the full marked variation of a connected abelian cover of curves. This holds in every base genus and for every compatible branching pattern. For any CM abelian variety, the conclusion also holds for every self-power of its product with the Jacobian, on the same Hodge-generic locus. We also prove the conjecture on every self-power of a very general member of the full smooth labelled family of diagonal complete intersections cut out by at most two equations of a common degree, in every dimension and every degree at least two."

- Split abelian eightfolds [S1:818]: "We prove that every rational Weil class on a split abelian eightfold of Weil type is algebraic. The result holds for every imaginary quadratic field, every compatible polarization type, and every member of the split family, including those with additional endomorphisms. Thus the full two-dimensional rational Weil space in codimension four is generated by algebraic cycle classes."

- The conditional reduction [S1:827]: "For a polarized K3 surface whose primitive cohomology has full orthogonal Hodge group, one algebraic correspondence inducing a nonzero map from that cohomology to the second cohomology of an abelian variety suffices to recover the prescribed full Kuga–Satake correspondence. We give an equivalent condition using holomorphic one-forms on a generically finite surface cover. If this input holds very generally in a polarized component, the prescribed correspondence is algebraic throughout that component, for every choice of standard data on the transcendental part. The existence of the initial correspondence remains a hypothesis."

## Undergraduate summary

The family's headline theorem is the rational Hodge conjecture for abelian varieties with complex multiplication, and the CM manuscript states its own scope in one sentence [S4:25–28]:

> In particular, the rational Hodge conjecture holds in every codimension on every finite product of complex CM abelian varieties and on every power of such a product.

*Complex multiplication* is a strong symmetry condition on an abelian variety, and the manuscript's definition is a short paragraph [S4:10–14]; the closure property the theorem needs is the elementary one [S4:13–14]: "Finite products and powers of CM abelian varieties are again CM." The conjecture asks whether every rational Hodge class is a rational linear combination of classes of algebraic cycles, so the theorem is a statement about cycles, not about cohomology alone.

The other seven manuscripts move the same question to K3 surfaces, the other place where the conjecture is tractable. The products manuscript proves it for every finite product of projective K3 surfaces, and it is explicit that nothing is assumed about the factors [S10:24–28]:

> The factors may repeat, and no relation between their periods or endomorphism fields is assumed. The distinction matters: a product can contain Hodge tensors coupling different transcendental spaces, while a power of one surface can contain tensors beyond those generated by pairwise contractions. The proof treats both phenomena.

The all-surfaces manuscript proves that the Kuga–Satake correspondence — a construction that attaches an abelian variety to a K3 surface — is algebraic for every projective K3 surface, and its history section closes by naming the gap it closes [S14:186–188]: "Theorem 1 addresses this algebraicity problem for every projective K3 surface, in the fixed formulation stated above."

The proof sketch, in one paragraph: the CM manuscript reduces Hodge classes to Weil-type classes on auxiliary CM varieties, builds a four-factor class by pairing four degree-one classes on a surface sitting inside a product, produces the needed period on a compact arithmetic quotient of the complex two-ball, and uses Hecke operators and Frobenius slopes to turn a counting identity into an algebraic correspondence. The K3 manuscripts then take that theorem as an input, alongside the exact Kuga–Satake cycles the products manuscript builds for itself [S12:32–34].

## Graduate summary

The CM theorem is a four-factor switch theorem in disguise. The Deligne–André reduction is the entry point [S4:92–93]: "reduce these Hodge classes to Weil-type classes on auxiliary CM abelian varieties", and the manuscript is precise about what the reduction does not do [S4:98–100]: "This reduction identifies classes whose algebraicity would suffice; it does not supply their algebraic representatives in all dimensions." The gap between those two sentences is the paper.

The construction is dimensional. The class one needs is a four-factor tensor, and [S4:147–148]: "The geometric construction of a four-factor class takes place on a surface." Four degree-one classes are paired on a surface embedded in the product, the nonvanishing pairing is a surface integral of their cup product, and the manuscript's conclusion is [S4:157–159]: "The image of \(S\) in their product therefore gives an algebraic cycle whose pairing with the desired line is nonzero." What is left is arithmetic [S4:161–163]: "The remaining problem is thus to find four degree-one classes with both a nonzero surface period and the prescribed rational CM sources."

The answer is a Shimura-type period, built on a compact arithmetic quotient of the complex two-ball, whose unitary group is [S4:168–170]: "Its unitary group has signature \((2,1)\) at one distinguished embedding and is definite at all the other real places." Theta functions supply the one-forms, weak approximation moves them to the required CM places, and a rank-two theta tensor packages the wedges into one total two-form. The arithmetic input is a Hecke-counting identity [S4:211–213]: "Counting ordinary isogeny neighbors gives a polynomial relation between these operators and Frobenius." whose extreme slopes are then converted [S4:218–219]: "The Hecke projectors preserve the Hodge filtration, so weak admissibility turns these extreme slopes into pure Hodge types." The assembly closes in the last proof of its section [S5:99–105], on the scope clause [S5:104]: "Finite products and powers are CM, so the same conclusion covers them."

On the K3 side the two heavy manuscripts are explicit about borrowing. The products manuscript names its two theorem inputs [S10:99–106] — "Two companion results enter the final algebraic argument." — and states the boundary [S10:105–106]: "These are theorem inputs with the stated scope", leaving its own contribution as the exact Kuga–Satake cycles and the tensors that combine different surfaces. Its self-power corollary is the headline [S12:519–521]: "For every projective complex K3 surface $S$, the rational Hodge conjecture holds in every degree on every self-power $S^m$." The all-surfaces manuscript states its theorem as a cycle [S14:109–121] — "For every smooth projective complex K3 surface $S$, and for every fixed set of standard Kuga--Satake construction data as above, there is a cycle" — and derives it from the conditional reduction, whose full-group hypothesis it verifies very generally [S14:4987–4988]: "The full-group condition holds very generally in each polarized component." The locus manuscript is the conditional form of the same machinery, stated as a criterion [S15:156–157]: "Then rational HC and rational GHC hold in every degree of every self-power of $S$."

## History of the conjecture

The family's own genealogy is the products manuscript's history section. It starts from the conjecture itself [S11:2–3]: "The Hodge conjecture, formulated in Hodge's 1950 Congress address", and names why products are harder [S11:6–7]: "Products introduce a different problem: their Hodge classes include tensors in the transcendental cohomologies of the factors." The Kuga–Satake line begins [S11:13–14]: "Kuga and Satake associated an abelian variety to a polarized Hodge structure of K3 type", is developed next by [S11:16–18]: "Deligne developed this construction in families and used it in his proof of the Weil conjecture for K3 surfaces", and continues [S11:18–19]: "subsequently proved that the Kuga--Satake correspondence is motivated". The all-surfaces manuscript gives the same genealogy with the intermediate steps [S14:124–188], dating the construction [S14:126]: "Kuga and Satake introduced their construction in 1967".

The partial results before this release read as a list of hypotheses being removed one at a time. Buskin's theorem is the isometry case [S11:30–31]: "Buskin proved that every rational Hodge isometry between the second cohomologies of two projective K3 surfaces is algebraic", and the manuscript flags the restriction [S11:34–36]: "The isometry hypothesis is significant: arbitrary Hodge homomorphisms need not preserve the quadratic forms." Zarhin's computation of Hodge groups is the structural input [S11:39–40]: "Zarhin determined the Hodge groups of transcendental K3 cohomology in terms of its endomorphism field, which is either totally real or CM", and the CM case was the tractable one [S11:46–49]: "When the endomorphism field is CM, the invariants are generated in degree two". What remained for products is stated as a joint-group problem [S11:75–77]: "For a general list of K3 surfaces, a further issue is to control the joint Hodge group: algebraicity of the tensors on each individual factor does not by itself determine the mixed invariants."

On the CM side the history is a chain of reductions that ends in Milne's theorems, which the consequences section cites by name [S6:12–14]. The conditional-reduction manuscript is the family's record of what was still missing [S21:71]: "Constructing a very-general correspondence or surface cover with the stated property remains the external existence problem."

Prior (my training data, not this repo). Before this release, Kuga–Satake algebraicity was approached through motivicity: André proved the correspondence is motivated, which makes it absolutely Hodge, and absolute Hodge classes are not known to be algebraic. The gap between motivated and algebraic is exactly the gap the two Kuga–Satake manuscripts close, which is why the conditional-reduction manuscript's insistence that absolute Hodge classes do not supply its input is the most telling sentence in the family. On the CM side, the Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties was known for classes generated in low degree and for powers in special families — Zarhin's Hodge groups, the invariants computed in the CM case, Varesco's all-powers result for Picard-16 families — and Milne's 2002 paper reduced the Tate and Hodge standard conclusions to Hodge-conjecture inputs for abelian varieties. A proof of rational Hodge in every codimension for every CM abelian variety therefore lands on a prepared foundation, which is why the consequences section is corollaries rather than conjectures.

## Consequences now that the proof has been found

The CM manuscript's consequences section is three named conjectures, and it opens with the scope of the theorem it uses [S6:4–6]: "Theorem 1 applies to products of CM abelian varieties and therefore algebraizes rational Hodge correspondences between them." Its roadmap is three sentences, the generalized Hodge conclusion, the Tate and Hodge standard conclusions via Milne [S6:12–14], and a final application [S6:15–16]: "A final application uses CM transport to algebraize specialized Hodge classes on arbitrary good-reduction abelian varieties."

The generalized Hodge conclusion is stated for the class of varieties the theorem covers [S6:164–165]: "This includes every finite product of CM abelian varieties and every power of such a product." The Tate conclusion is where family 001 enters: the hypothesis clause is [S6:230–231]: "The theorem's hypothesis is supplied by Theorem 1." and the proof line is [S6:292–293]: "Apply Milne's Theorem 3.3 using Theorem 1." The Hodge standard conclusion is stated in the closing lines of its theorem [S6:286–288] and ends with the characteristic clause [S6:288]: "holds for abelian varieties in arbitrary characteristic."

The specialization remark is explicit about what it does not claim [S6:316–317]: "specialization statement for abelian varieties that need not be CM." and [S6:331–334]: "This application uses the CM transport, not the independent rationality proof of that paper, and is not an input to Theorem 1. It asserts algebraicity on the special fiber, not the Hodge conjecture for arbitrary complex abelian varieties."

The borrowing runs the other way as well. The Weil-classes manuscript names the CM theorem as its only Hodge-conjecture input [S16:284–285]: "We use the companion theorem that the rational Hodge conjecture holds for every complex CM abelian variety in every codimension". The Gale manuscript keeps its boundary explicit in the other direction [S17:314–316]: "This section fixes the notation needed by the determinant construction and records the precise companion results used in its Hodge-theoretic assembly." and [S17:318]: "No result on split Weil classes is used in this paper." The all-surfaces manuscript's dependency is the conditional reduction, and it records the step [S14:5006–5007]: "the prescribed Kuga--Satake correspondence is algebraic for every surface in that component."

The published consequence for the reader is that the Kuga–Satake correspondence is now algebraic without hypothesis [S14:109–121], that the products theorem covers every self-power [S12:517–522], and that the locus theorem extends to every cohomological degree of every self-power on its locus [S15:195–197]. It is one of the 21 triaged families whose claim shape matches three or more patterns [S23:515–517], and the ledger records it as resolution, classification and uniform [S24:32]. Our triage line for the family names its companions [S23:146–149]: "Companions: rational Hodge for arbitrary products of projective K3, Kuga–Satake algebraicity." and the 001 + 032 chain is recorded as a chain sitting entirely inside T0 [S23:501–504].

Absent by construction (T0). This family has no scope note, no Lean source, no comparator challenges, no main_results entry, and no published reasoning trace [S24:32]. Its tier is a statement about the ledger's evidence columns, not about the manuscripts, which are complete on their own terms.
