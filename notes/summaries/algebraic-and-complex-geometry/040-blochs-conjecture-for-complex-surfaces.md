## Undergraduate summary

For a smooth projective surface $S$, the Chow group
$\CH_0(S)$ of zero-cycles modulo rational equivalence has a
degree-zero part $\CH_0(S)^0$. The Albanese homomorphism
$\alb_S:\CH_0(S)^0\to\Alb(S)$ is always surjective. Bloch's
conjecture predicts it is an isomorphism when
$p_g(S)=h^0(S,\omega_S)=0$. The main theorem [S5:18–25]:

**The coefficient.** For $K^2\le8$, a lattice construction
with at most three point blowups provides a class $\alpha$
with $\alpha^2<0$, and the full relation gives
$0=c\alpha^2+\sum(\alpha H)^2$, proving $c>0$ [S5:166–181].
For $K^2=9$, the Cartwright–Steger comparison surface with
$p_g=q=1$ detects the coefficient via its holomorphic
two-form [S5:183–200].

## History of the conjecture

Mumford showed a nonzero holomorphic two-form obstructs
finite-dimensional parametrization of zero-cycles; Bloch's
conjecture predicts the converse. Bloch–Kas–Lieberman proved
the integral assertion for $\kappa(S)<2$, leaving the
general-type $p_g=q=0$ case [S5:55–65]. "Mumford showed that a nonzero holomorphic two-form prevents the"

Special-surface approaches include the Inose–Mizukami
group-action method, Bauer's treatment of Inoue surfaces,
Pedrini–Weibel's transcendental-motive reformulation, and
Voisin's family-based correspondence result [S5:67–92].

The proof mechanism uses virtual localization on a Quot
scheme, with the derived determinant constructions of
Toën–Vaquié and Schürg–Toën–Vezzosi, and the
nested-Hilbert-scheme recursion of Ellingsrud–Göttsche–Lehn
[S5:109–121].

## Consequences

**Bloch's conjecture in full.** For every smooth connected
projective complex surface with $p_g=0$, the Albanese
homomorphism on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an
isomorphism [S5:31–39]. No minimality, fundamental-group,
or surface-construction hypothesis is required.

**Chow motive consequences.** For $p_g=q=0$, every rational
Chow class on a power of $S$ or a Hilbert scheme of points
is determined by its cohomology class [S5:47–51].
