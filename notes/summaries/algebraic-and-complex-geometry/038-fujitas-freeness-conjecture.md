# 038 — Fujita's freeness conjecture

[Index page](../../index/algebraic-and-complex-geometry/038-fujitas-freeness-conjecture.md)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

The release's blurb [S1:1085]: "**038. Fujita's freeness conjecture.** Proves Fujita's freeness conjecture at its sharp bound in every dimension: for a smooth projective complex variety $X$ of dimension $n$ and an ample line bundle $L$, the adjoint $K_X+mL$ is globally generated for every integer $m\ge n+1$." The manuscript
title is "Fujita's freeness conjecture" [S3:1], and its abstract [S4:56–60] reads: "We prove Fujita's freeness conjecture. If $X$ is a smooth complex
projective variety of dimension $n$ and $L$ is an ample line bundle,
then $K_X+mL$ is globally generated for every integer $m\geq n+1$."

## Undergraduate summary

Fujita's freeness conjecture asks: for a smooth projective variety
$X$ of dimension $n$ and an ample line bundle $L$, must
$K_X+(n+1)L$ be globally generated? The bound $n+1$ is sharp —
on $\mathbb{P}^n$ with $L=\mathcal{O}(1)$, the adjoint
$K_X+nL$ has no nonzero sections [S4:83–90]. "The exponent is sharp: for $X=\mathbb P^n$ and $L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(1)$,
the bundle $K_X+(n+1)L$ is trivial, whereas $K_X+nL$ has no nonzero
sections. Global generation asks for a nonvanishing section at each
point; it does not assert separation of points or tangent directions,
or the very-ampleness part of Fujita's conjecture.
Theorem   also implies that $K_X+mL$ is globally generated
for every integer $m\geq n+1$, even when $L$ is not globally generated;
see Corollary  ." The
theorem [S4:70–77]:

> **theorem**
Let $X$ be a smooth connected projective variety over $\mathbb C$ of dimension
$n\geq 1$, and let $L$ be an ample line bundle on $X$. Then
\[
                    K_X+(n+1)L
\]
is generated by its global sections.
\endtheorem

## Graduate summary

**The method.** The proof uses the standard vanishing strategy:
construct controlled singularities at the prescribed point, reduce
a suitable center to a point, and lift a nonzero fiber value via
Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing [S4:113–128]. "The common vanishing strategy constructs controlled singularities at
the prescribed point, reduces a suitable center to a point, and then
lifts a nonzero fiber value  .
A recurring difficulty is that a positive-dimensional center can itself
be singular, and cutting it down costs positivity. Angehrn–Siu used
movement to a smooth point and semicontinuity. Fujita estimated ranks
of restriction maps to the exceptional divisor of a point blowup,
while Helmke bounded the multiplicities of log canonical centers
 .
Our argument controls a minimizing center by comparing the growth of
images of restriction maps with a tangential first variation. The
estimate is uniform over all integral subvarieties of a fixed dimension,
including singular subvarieties of unbounded degree. The final lift
uses the local discrepancy-and-vanishing mechanism of
\cite[\S1(1.6)]fujita1993; the minimizing construction produces the
configuration to which it applies." The new
ingredient is control of the minimizing center: the argument
compares growth of restriction-map images with a tangential first
variation, uniformly over all integral subvarieties of a fixed
dimension, including singular subvarieties of unbounded degree.

**The reduction to a point center.** The key proposition [S8:8–13]
shows that if $P$ is not generated at $x$, then for arbitrarily
large $m$ the infimum is attained by a minimizing pair whose center
is exactly $\{x\}$:

> **proposition**
Suppose that $P$ is not generated at $x$. There is a real number
$0<t<n+1$ such that, for arbitrarily large integers $m$, the infimum
$f_{m,t}(x)$ is attained and every minimizing monomial pair has center
exactly $\{x\}$.
\endproposition

This eliminates positive-dimensional centers, which are the
obstruction in all prior approaches [S4:113–128].

**The lift.** With a point center, the isolation argument [S7:4–9]
constructs an effective rational SNC divisor with the right
discrepancy configuration for Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing:

> We now use a point-centered minimizing pair to construct an effective
rational SNC divisor whose coefficient equals the log discrepancy on a
nonempty reduced divisor over $x$, and is strictly smaller on every
other component meeting that divisor. This local configuration will
allow Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing to lift a nonzero value in the fiber
of $P=K_X+(n+1)L$ at $x$.

The minimization section [S6:1–7] converts failure of generation
into a strict asymptotic inequality for ordinary orders, showing
that the lattice-counting equality case already permits a nonzero
fiber value to be lifted.

## History of the conjecture

The conjecture was proved in low dimensions: curves by
Riemann–Roch, surfaces by Reider, threefolds by Ein–Lazarsfeld
and Kawamata, fourfolds by Kawamata, fivefolds by Ye–Zhu.
In arbitrary dimension, Angehrn–Siu gave $m\ge(n^2+n+2)/2$;
Heier improved to $n^{4/3}$; Ghidelli–Lacini to
$n(\log\log n+2.34)$; Han to $1.776n$. Chan announced a proof
that was withdrawn [S4:92–111]. "For curves,
Riemann–Roch gives the sharp bound. The surface case follows from
Reider's theorem, and the threefold, fourfold, and fivefold cases were
proved by Ein–Lazarsfeld, Kawamata, and Ye–Zhu, respectively
 .
In arbitrary dimension, Angehrn–Siu obtained generation for
$m\geq (n^2+n+2)/2$  .
Helmke developed multiplicity estimates for the centers appearing in the
vanishing argument  ; Heier combined these
estimates with the Angehrn–Siu method to obtain a bound of order
$n^{4/3}$  .
Ghidelli–Lacini subsequently obtained the bound
$m\geq n(\log\log n+2.34)$ for $n\geq2$
\cite[Theorem 1.1]ghidellilacini2024.
Han's recent preprint gives $m\geq\lceil C_0n\rceil$, where
$C_0=1.77629988\ldots$ \cite[Theorem 1.1]han2026.
Chan announced a proof of the stronger intersection-number formulation
in \cite[Theorem 1.3]chan2024; that preprint was withdrawn after the
local extension argument did not guarantee the required vanishing order."

The vanishing strategy dates to Ein–Lazarsfeld and Kawamata.
The center-reduction problem — a positive-dimensional center can
be singular, and cutting it down costs positivity — is what all
prior approaches addressed by different means: Angehrn–Siu used
movement and semicontinuity, Fujita estimated restriction-map
ranks, Helmke bounded multiplicities [S4:113–128].

## Consequences

**The sharp bound in all dimensions.** Fujita's freeness
conjecture holds at its optimal exponent $n+1$ for every smooth
projective variety of every dimension [S4:70–77]. The bound is
sharp on projective space [S4:83–90].

**Uniform center control.** The minimizing-center argument is
uniform over all integral subvarieties, including singular ones
of unbounded degree, and does not require the center to be
smooth or of bounded degree [S4:113–128].
