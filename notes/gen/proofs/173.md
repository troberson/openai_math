# 173 — Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture

## Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture

**Match.** The single comparator target is proven as a `main_results` entry.

The preprint's main theorem [[2]] asserts that every nonempty finite oriented graph has a vertex v with |N₁⁺(v)| ≤ |N₂⁺(v)|: at least as many second out-neighbors (directed distance exactly two) as first out-neighbors. The scope note [S1:9] records the same, noting the second neighborhood excludes the vertex and first neighbors, and includes sinks.

The Lean declaration `exists_goodVertex` [S2:11–18] [[1]] states ∃ v, GoodVertex r v for any oriented relation on a nonempty finite type. The proof proceeds by contradiction: assuming no good vertex exists, it derives a counterexample, applies `counterexample_reduction` to extract a finite graph with strict subset growth, and obtains a contradiction via `Extremal.no_strictSubsetGrowth_of_pruning`. The single comparator [S1:15] (`SeymourSecondNeighborhood.lean`) is the shared target.
