# 347 — Counterexamples to strong forms of Arnold's fixed-point conjecture

## Three fixed points on the symplectic quadric threefold

**Match.** The scope note The critical-number form of Arnold's fixed-point conjecture predicts at least as many fixed points as the minimum number of critical points of a smooth function. The formalized counterexample is a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of the complex quadric threefold with exactly three fixed points, while every smooth function on that manifold has at least four critical points. At least one fixed point is degenerate. The separate nondegenerate Morse-number construction is not included. [S1:9] states the counterexample: a
Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of the complex quadric threefold with
exactly three fixed points, while every smooth function has at least
four critical points, with at least one fixed point degenerate. The
nondegenerate Morse-number construction is excluded.

The OAI proof lives in `lean/OAI/Geometry/Arnold/` (multiple files,
0 `sorry` total), constructing the degenerate Hamiltonian on the
quadric threefold $Q^3 \subset \mathbb{C}P^4$ explicitly and
verifying the three-fixed-point count against the Arnold lower bound
of four. The declaration `OAI.ArnoldCounterexample.main` is proven.
