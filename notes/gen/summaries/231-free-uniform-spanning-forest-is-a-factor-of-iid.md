## Undergraduate summary

A **uniform spanning forest** (USF) is a random forest (a graph with
no cycles) chosen uniformly from all spanning forests of a given
graph. The **free USF** is the version where the measure is
obtained by letting the graph grow and taking a limit. A
**factor of IID** means the forest can be produced by a
deterministic rule applied independently to each vertex's random
label, with no coordination between vertices. The result says: on
**every** infinite connected locally finite graph, the free USF
admits such a rule, and the rule is compatible with all graph
symmetries (isomorphism-equivariant) without picking a
distinguished starting point [S3:42–49, S2:547].

## Graduate summary

**Free USF as a factor of IID**
[S3:42–49, S2:547]. On every infinite connected locally
finite simple unweighted graph, the free uniform spanning forest
is a factor of independent identically distributed vertex
labels. One Borel isomorphism-equivariant rule works for all
such graphs and uses no root. This answers affirmatively the
general factor question for unimodular random graphs.

**Strongly Rayleigh and determinantal-process IID factors**
[S3:42–49, S2:547]. Every translation-invariant strongly
Rayleigh binary process indexed by a countable group is a factor
of IID, including invariant determinantal processes with Hermitian
positive-contraction kernels. The group-action conclusion requires
no amenability.

## History of the conjecture

The **factor-of-IID question** for uniform spanning forests asks
whether the free USF can be generated by a local, translation-
invariant rule from independent vertex noise. For
**amenable** graphs, this follows from existing results on
unimodular random graphs. The extension to **every** infinite
connected locally finite graph [S3:42–49] removes the
amenability hypothesis, and the rule is
isomorphism-equivariant with no root. The
**strongly Rayleigh** and **determinantal process** results
extend the factor-of-IID framework to a broader class of
correlated processes on countable groups, again without
amenability.

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** The free USF, the
unimodular random graph framework, and the factor-of-IID
concept are well-established. The amenability-free extension
and the strongly Rayleigh / determinantal-process factor
results are the novel contributions. I cannot confirm these
independently from training alone.

## Consequences

- **The universal free USF factor rule**
  [S3:42–49]: one Borel isomorphism-equivariant rule
  producing the free USF from IID vertex labels on every
  infinite connected locally finite simple unweighted graph,
  with no root.
- **The strongly Rayleigh factor result**
  [S3:42–49]: every translation-invariant strongly
  Rayleigh process on a countable group is a factor of IID.
- **The determinantal-process factor result**
  [S3:42–49]: invariant determinantal processes with
  Hermitian positive-contraction kernels on countable index
  sets are factors of IID, with no trace-class
  requirement.
