# 172 — Classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations

[Index page](../index/combinatorics/172-classification-of-finite-euclidean-ramsey-configurations.md)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

The release's blurb [S1:4033] and the catalog abstract [S2:425] state
the family's two-part claim. First, a **classification**: finite
point configurations that appear monochromatically at their original
scale in every finite coloring of sufficiently high-dimensional
Euclidean space are precisely those satisfying a tensor-field
condition over their coordinate field. Second, the classification
**disproves the Leader–Russell–Walters conjecture** that
every such configuration is a subset of a finite transitive set. The
manuscript is *A classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey
configurations* [S3:33, S3:35]; its abstract 

> We classify finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations by a necessary and
sufficient tensor condition over their coordinate fields. The Ramsey
property here concerns monochromatic congruent copies at the original
scale under arbitrary finite colorings. The criterion shows that every
nonempty subtransitive set and every nonempty set of at most five points
on a circle is Ramsey. In particular, some Ramsey cyclic quadrilaterals
are not subtransitive, disproving the necessity direction of the
Leader–Russell–Walters conjectured characterization. [S3:40–47]

## Undergraduate summary

Color the points of Euclidean space with finitely many colors. A
finite set of points is **Euclidean Ramsey** if, no matter how you
color all the points of a sufficiently high-dimensional space, you
always find a monochromatic **congruent copy** of the original set
at the same scale. For example, two points (a line segment) are
Ramsey: any 2-coloring of a large enough Euclidean space forces a
monochromatic segment of a given length. The **classification
conjecture** asks: which finite configurations are Ramsey? This
family answers that: a finite configuration with full affine span
is Ramsey if and only if its coordinate field satisfies a specific
**tensor condition** — an algebraic condition over the field
generated by the configuration's coordinates
[S3:40–47, S2:425].

## Graduate summary

**The classification theorem** [S3:40–47, S4:9]. A finite
configuration $`a: \{1,\dots,s\} \to \mathbb R^d`$ with $`s \ge 2`$,
$`d \ge 1`$, injective, and with full affine span (the affine hull is
all of $`\mathbb R^d`$) is Euclidean Ramsey if and only if it
satisfies the **field criterion**: the tensor-field map
$`\mathrm{mult}: \mathcal T(a) \to \mathrm{Coeff}(a)`$ is a field
homomorphism — the algebraic condition that determines
Ramsey-ness for full-affine-span configurations [S5:42–46].

**Subtransitive Ramsey sets.** Every nonempty **subtransitive** set
(subset of a finite set with a transitive isometry group) is
Ramsey [S4:9]. The classification is **strictly more general**:
some Ramsey cyclic quadrilaterals are not subtransitive,
disproving the Leader–Russell–Walters conjecture that
every Ramsey configuration is subtransitive [S3:40–47, S4:9].

**Circle-point Ramsey results.** Every nonempty set of at most five
points on a circle is Euclidean Ramsey [S4:9]. This is one of the
unregistered comparator theorems.

**Non-Ramsey spherical examples.** The specified **twelve-point
set on the unit circle** has a fifty-color obstruction in every
positive dimension, so it is not Ramsey. A **nine-point** circle
configuration built from algebraically independent parameters is
likewise non-Ramsey [S4:11].

**The scope of the formalization.** The formalization covers the
full classification, the subtransitive circle theorem, the
non-Ramsey examples, and the cosphericity theorem, with two
`main_results` entries [S4:9, S4:11].

## History of the conjecture

The study of Euclidean Ramsey configurations goes back to Hadwiger
(1959) for two-point sets and to Graham (1977) for configurations
on the circle. Leader, Russell, and Walters conjectured that every
Euclidean Ramsey configuration is a **subtransitive** set. The
tensor-field classification given here — expressing
Ramsey-ness as an algebraic condition over the coordinate field
for full-affine-span configurations — implies the
Leader–Russell–Walters conjecture is **false**, since
some Ramsey cyclic quadrilaterals escape the subtransitive class
[S3:40–47, S2:425].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** I know the Leader–
Russell–Walters transitivity conjecture and that some circle
configurations are Ramsey. The **tensor-field characterization**
and the explicit **disproof** via cyclic quadrilaterals that are
Ramsey but not subtransitive go beyond what I recall; the
specific algebraic condition and the twelve-point fifty-color
obstruction are new to me.

## Consequences

**The Leader–Russell–Walters conjecture is disproved.**
There exist Euclidean Ramsey configurations that are not subsets
of finite transitive sets [S3:40–47, S4:9].

**An algebraic classification.** A full-affine-span configuration
is Ramsey iff it satisfies the field criterion — a concrete
algebraic condition over the coordinate field [S3:40–47,
S5:42–46, S4:9].

**At most five circle points are always Ramsey.** Nonempty
subsets of the unit circle with at most five points are Euclidean
Ramsey [S4:9].

**Two explicit non-Ramsey configurations.** The twelve-point
circle set (fifty-color obstruction in every dimension) and a
nine-point algebraically-independent circle configuration are not
Euclidean Ramsey [S4:11, S6:46–50].

**Cosphericity.** Every Euclidean Ramsey configuration is
cospherical (its points lie on a common sphere) [S4:9].

theorem classification {s d : ℕ} (a : Fin s → Space d)

    (hspan : affineSpan ℝ (Set.range a) = ⊤) :

  ⟨ramsey_fieldCriterion a (by omega) hspan,fieldCriterion_ramsey a hs hd ha⟩
