# 033 — Campana's orbifold Iitaka conjecture and logarithmic subadditivity

[Index page](../index/algebraic-and-complex-geometry/033-campanas-orbifold-iitaka-conjecture-and-logarithmic-subadditivity.md)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

The release blurb 

> **033. Iitaka subadditivity, variation, and logarithmic additivity.** Proves Campana's orbifold Iitaka subadditivity conjecture for smooth Fujiki-class-$`C`$ manifolds with rational simple-normal-crossing boundaries. For projective fibrations $`f:U\to V`$ of smooth complex quasi-projective varieties with connected fibers, general fiber F, and $`\bar\kappa(V)\ge0`$, proves Popa's inequality $`\bar\kappa(U)\ge\kappa(F)+\max\{\bar\kappa(V),\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}\nolimits (f)\}`$, where variation measures the whole geometric generic fiber. [S1:807]

This family ships **five** manuscripts. Their abstracts:

Manuscript 1, orbifold and logarithmic subadditivity 

> We prove Campana's orbifold Iitaka subadditivity conjecture for rational
simple normal crossing boundaries on compact manifolds in Fujiki class
$`\mathcal C`$, including coefficient one. Ordinary and logarithmic
subadditivity follow. [S2:67–72]

Manuscript 2, logarithmic Iitaka dimension and whole-fiber variation


> We prove the logarithmic Iitaka–Viehweg inequality for projective
surjective morphisms with connected fibers between smooth complex
quasi-projective varieties whose base has nonnegative logarithmic
Kodaira dimension. The variation measures the birational field of
definition of the whole geometric generic fiber. This resolves
Popa's logarithmic variation conjecture positively. [S4:69–76]

Manuscript 3, the reverse logarithmic Kodaira inequality and
additivity — the one the scope note links 

> We prove the reverse logarithmic Kodaira inequality for a surjective
connected-fiber morphism $`f:(X,E)\to(Y,D)`$ of smooth projective
reduced simple-normal-crossing pairs, with
$`\mathrm{Supp}(f^*D)\subseteq\mathrm{Supp} E`$, such that $`X`$ and every boundary
stratum are smooth over $`Y\setminus\mathrm{Supp} D`$. Together with logarithmic
subadditivity, the inequality gives additivity, including both
negative-infinity cases. This resolves Popa's logarithmic additivity
conjecture positively in the projective reduced-SNC, stratum-smooth setting. [S6:76–85]

Manuscript 4, projective Hodge lines and ordinary Iitaka
subadditivity 

> We prove the ordinary Iitaka subadditivity conjecture for surjective
projective morphisms with connected fibers between smooth connected
projective varieties over algebraically closed fields of characteristic
zero. If $`F`$ is the geometric generic fiber of $`f:X\to Z`$, then
$`\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(F)+\kappa(Z)`$. [S7:53–59]

Manuscript 5, b-semiampleness for compact log-smooth Kähler
fibrations 

> We prove the compact log-smooth Kähler case of b-semiampleness. Let
$`f:Y\to X`$ be a surjective holomorphic map with connected fibers between
smooth compact connected Kähler manifolds, and let $`\Delta`$ be an effective
rational divisor with simple normal crossing support and coefficients in
$`[0,1]`$, with $`K_Y+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb Q}f^*L`$ for $`L\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb Q}`$. There is a
smooth compact Kähler modification $`S\to X`$ for which the threshold-moduli
line satisfies $`M_{S_1}=\nu^*M_S`$ in $`\mathrm{Pic}(S_1)_{\mathbb Q}`$ for every smooth compact
Kähler modification $`\nu:S_1\to S`$, and some positive multiple of $`M_S`$ is
represented by a holomorphic line bundle generated by global sections.
Horizontal components of coefficient one are allowed; neither projectivity
nor a Campana orbifold Iitaka hypothesis is assumed. [S8:56–68]

## Undergraduate summary

The "Kodaira dimension" of a variety is a number that measures how
large its canonical divisors can get — how close the variety is to
having "many" holomorphic differential forms. For a fibration (a map
from one variety to another whose fibers form a family), a natural
question is how the dimension of the total space compares to the
dimensions of a fiber and the base. "Subadditivity" says the total
dimension is at least the fiber's plus the base's.

This family tackles several versions of that question at once, from
the classical projective/ordinary setting (manuscript 4) up to the
modern **orbifold** and **logarithmic** settings, where a boundary
divisor (a distinguished collection of subvarieties) is kept track of
and the dimension is measured relative to it. The headlines are
Campana's orbifold Iitaka subadditivity conjecture (manuscript 1) and
Popa's inequality, which adds a "variation" term measuring how much
the *whole* generic fiber moves (manuscript 2). Manuscript 5 proves a
Kähler-theoretic semiampleness input the others use.

## Graduate summary

The five manuscripts form a chain: the Kähler input (5) feeds the
ordinary projective subadditivity (4), which feeds the logarithmic and
orbifold statements (1, 2, 3).

**Orbifold subadditivity.** 

> **Theorem 1.1**
Let $`X`$ be a smooth compact connected complex manifold in Fujiki
class $`\mathcal C`$, let $`\Delta`$ be a rational SNC boundary, and
let $`f:X\to Y`$ be a surjective holomorphic map with connected
fibers onto a normal compact irreducible complex space.
For a very general smooth fiber $`F`$, put $`\Delta_F=\Delta|_F`$.
Then
$$\kappa(X,K_X+\Delta)
 \geq\kappa(F,K_F+\Delta_F)+\kappa(f\mid\Delta).$$ [S3:135–146]

For a smooth compact complex manifold $`X`$ in Fujiki class $`\mathcal C`$
with a rational SNC boundary $`\Delta`$, and a surjective connected-fiber
map $`f : X \to Y`$, the orbifold Iitaka dimension satisfies
$`\kappa(X, K_X + \Delta) \ge \kappa(F, K_F + \Delta_F) + \kappa(f \mid
\Delta)`$ for a very general smooth fiber $`F`$. This is the positive
resolution of Campana's orbifold Iitaka subadditivity conjecture, and the
ordinary and logarithmic subadditivity statements follow from it.

**Popa's logarithmic variation.** 

> **Theorem 1.1**
Let $`f:U\to V`$ be a projective surjective morphism with connected fibers
between smooth connected quasi-projective complex varieties.
Assume $`\bar\kappa(V)\geq0`$, and let $`F`$ be its geometric generic fiber.
Then
$$\bar\kappa(U)\geq\kappa(F)+\max\{\bar\kappa(V),\mathrm{Var}(f)\}.$$ [S5:38–46]

For a projective connected-fiber morphism $`f : U \to V`$ between smooth
quasi-projective varieties with $`\bar\kappa(V) \ge 0`$ and geometric
generic fiber $`F`$,
$`\bar\kappa(U) \ge \kappa(F) + \max\{\bar\kappa(V), \mathrm{Var}(f)\}`$, where
$`\mathrm{Var}(f)`$ is the logarithmic Kodaira dimension of the *whole* geometric
generic fiber (its birational field of definition). This is the
logarithmic Iitaka–Viehweg inequality and resolves Popa's
variation conjecture.

**The reverse inequality and additivity.** [S6:76–85]: manuscript 3
proves the reverse logarithmic Kodaira inequality
$`\kappa(X, L_X) \le \kappa(Y, L_Y) + \kappa(F, L_F)`$ for
stratum-smooth reduced-SNC pairs, and combining it with subadditivity
gives full logarithmic additivity, including both $`-\infty`$ cases — a
positive resolution of Popa's logarithmic additivity conjecture in the
projective reduced-SNC, stratum-smooth setting.

**The Kähler input.** [S8:56–68]: manuscript 5 proves the compact
log-smooth Kähler case of b-semiampleness, the line-bundle semiampleness
statement (with coefficient-one horizontal components allowed and no
projectivity hypothesis) that the Hodge-line arguments downstream rely
on.

## history of the conjecture

Prior (my training data, not this repo): the ordinary Iitaka
subadditivity conjecture is a long-standing problem in birational
geometry, proved in various special cases (Viehweg, Campana) and in the
projective case by work building on the logarithmic Kodaira dimension
machinery. Campana extended the question to *orbifold* pairs (where the
boundary carries a multiplicity), and Popa formulated logarithmic
versions with a precise *variation* term — the conjecture that
$`\bar\kappa(U) \ge \kappa(F) + \max\{\bar\kappa(V), \mathrm{Var}(f)\}`$, where the
variation is defined from the field of definition of the geometric
generic fiber. The negative-fiber branch (fiber of Kodaira dimension
$`-\infty`$ forcing the total to be $`-\infty`$ and all positive-degree
pluriform sections to vanish) is the degenerate case that the formal
comparison targets; it is the branch where the additivity statement
collapses to a vanishing theorem.

## Consequences

**Campana's orbifold Iitaka subadditivity conjecture** for rational SNC
boundaries in Fujiki class $`\mathcal C`$, with ordinary and logarithmic
subadditivity as consequences [S2:67–72].

variation conjecture positively [S4:69–76].

**Logarithmic additivity, including both $`-\infty`$ cases**, resolving
Popa's logarithmic additivity conjecture in the
projective reduced-SNC, stratum-smooth setting [S6:76–85].

**Ordinary Iitaka subadditivity** in the projective characteristic-zero
