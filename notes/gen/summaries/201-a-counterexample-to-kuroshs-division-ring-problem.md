## Undergraduate summary

A **division ring** is a set where you can add, subtract,
multiply, and divide (except by zero), but multiplication
may **not** commute — the order of factors
matters. Think of the **quaternions** (extended
"complex numbers" with three square roots of minus one)
— they form a division ring, not a field. A division
ring is **algebraic over its center** if every element
satisfies a polynomial equation with coefficients in the
center (the "commuting" submodule). A division ring is
**locally finite** over its center if every finite set of
elements generates a finite-dimensional vector space over
the center. **Kurosh's division-ring problem** asks: is
every algebraic division ring locally finite over its
center? For fields (commutative division rings) the
answer is **yes** (the Artin–Wedderburn-type
results). For **noncommutative** division rings, the
answer was an open problem. This manuscript says
**no**: it constructs a countable characteristic-zero
division ring where every element is algebraic over the
center, and the ring is generated by just two elements
over the center, yet the ring as a whole is
**infinite-dimensional** over the center
[S3:12–15, S2:485].

## Graduate summary

**The main theorem** [S3:12–15, S2:485]. There
exists a **countable** division ring $D$ of
**characteristic zero**, with center $Z$, such that:
(1) $D$ is algebraic over $Z$ (every element of $D$
satisfies a polynomial over $Z$); (2) $D$ is generated
by **two** elements as an algebra over $Z$; and
(3) $D$ has **infinite** dimension as a vector space
over $Z$. This answers Kurosh's division-ring problem
on local finiteness negatively [S3:12–15].

## History of the conjecture

**Kurosh's division-ring problem** was posed by
Andrei Kurosh in the 1940s and asks whether every
algebraic division ring is locally finite over its
center. For **commutative** division rings (i.e.,
fields), the equivalence between algebraic and locally
finite is classical. For **noncommutative** division
rings, the local finiteness condition is stronger: it
requires that **every finite subset** of the ring
generates a finite-dimensional subspace over the center.
The problem is related to the **Gelfand–Koida
conjecture** and the structure theory of central
simple algebras. Partial results existed for division
rings of bounded **exponent** (a uniform bound on the
degrees of the minimal polynomials) and for
**PI** (polynomially identity) division rings. This
manuscript **disproves** the conjecture in full by
constructing a countable characteristic-zero example
with two generators over the center but infinite
dimension [S3:12–15, S2:485].

## Consequences

**Kurosh's division-ring problem on local finiteness is
answered negatively.** A countable characteristic-zero
division ring algebraic over its center need not be
locally finite [S3:12–15, S2:485].

**The counterexample is two-generated over the center.**
The ring is generated by two elements as an algebra over
its center, making the finite-generation condition
strong [S3:12–15].

**The counterexample is countable.** The construction
yields a countable division ring, ruling out cardinality
obstructions [S3:12–15].
