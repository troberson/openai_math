## Undergraduate summary

In a **polynomial ring**, you write expressions with
several variables multiplied and added together. A
**homogeneous ideal** is a collection of polynomials
where every polynomial has the same "total degree"
(the sum of the exponent powers in each term). The
**Hilbert function** of an ideal tells you, for each
degree $d$, how many independent degree-$d$ polynomials
are left after dividing out by the ideal. The
**lex-plus-powers conjecture** says: among all monomial
ideals (ideals generated by monomials) that contain the
same pure powers $x_1^{a_1}, \ldots, x_n^{a_n}$, there
is one — the **lex-plus-powers** ideal
— whose Hilbert function is the **largest**
possible. This means it "kills off" the most polynomials
at every degree. The
**Eisenbud–Green–Harris conjecture** is a
related but slightly different statement about which
ideals maximize the Hilbert function. Both conjectures
were open for general homogeneous ideals containing
regular sequences of arbitrary length. This manuscript
pair **proves** both in characteristic zero
[S3:46–51, S4:63–66, S2:483].

## Graduate summary

**The lex-plus-powers Betti theorem**
[S3:46–51, S2:483]. Let $R$ be a polynomial ring
over a characteristic-zero field and let
$\mathfrak{a} \subset R$ be a homogeneous ideal
containing a homogeneous regular sequence
$f_1, \ldots, f_r$ of degrees $a_1, \ldots, a_r$ with
each $a_i \geq 2$. Then the lex-plus-powers ideal
$\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbf{a}}$ (the monomial ideal
generated by all monomials $\mathbf{x}^\mathbf{d}$
with $d_i \geq a_i$ for some $i$) has the same Hilbert
function as $R/\mathfrak{a}$, and
$\beta_{i,j}(R/\mathfrak{a}) \leq
\beta_{i,j}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbf{a}})$ for every
homological degree $i$ and internal degree $j$
[S3:46–51].

**The Eisenbud–Green–Harris theorem**
[S4:63–66, S2:483]. Under the same hypotheses,
$R/\mathfrak{a}$ has the Hilbert function of the
monomial ideal $R/\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbf{a}}$ containing
the corresponding pure powers [S4:63–66].

## History of the conjecture

The **Eisenbud–Green–Harris conjecture**
(1992) and the **lex-plus-powers conjecture** were
central open problems in commutative algebra and
combinatorial commutative algebra. They generalize
**Macaulay's theorem** (1898), which determines the
maximum Hilbert function of a homogeneous ideal in a
polynomial ring over any field. Macaulay's theorem
applies to **unrestricted** homogeneous ideals; the
Eisenbud–Green–Harris and lex-plus-powers
versions apply to ideals **containing a regular
sequence** (a "generic" set of generators of minimal
length). The characteristic-zero restriction is natural
because the proofs use **division-coefficient forms**
— a characteristic-zero algebraic structure that
encodes the interaction of the regular sequence with the
monomial initial ideal. The two manuscripts provide
complementary attacks: the first proves the full
graded-Betti strengthening for the Artinian case, and
the second proves the Hilbert-function form via a
commuting division-coefficient form
[S3:46–51, S4:63–66, S2:483].

## Consequences

**The Eisenbud–Green–Harris conjecture is
proved in characteristic zero.** For homogeneous ideals
containing a regular sequence of degrees at least two,
the Hilbert function equals that of the corresponding
lex-plus-powers monomial ideal [S4:63–66,
S2:483].

**The lex-plus-powers Betti theorem is proved.** The
lex-plus-powers ideal has no smaller graded Betti
numbers than any homogeneous ideal containing the
same regular sequence [S3:46–51, S2:483].

**The result covers arbitrary regular-sequence
length.** No upper bound on the number of generators is
imposed [S3:46–51, S4:63–66].
