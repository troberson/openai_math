# 200 — Eisenbud–Green–Harris and lex-plus-powers in characteristic zero

[Index page](../index/algebra/200-eisenbud-green-harris-and-lex-plus-powers-in-characteristic-zero.md)

## Original CONTENTS.md summary

The release's blurb [S1:4575] and the catalog abstract [S2:481] state
the family's claim: the Eisenbud–Green–Harris and lex-plus-powers
conjectures over every characteristic-zero field — any homogeneous ideal
containing a regular sequence, of arbitrary length and degrees at least
two, admits a lex-plus-powers ideal with the same Hilbert function and
no smaller graded Betti numbers. The family ships two companion
manuscripts. Manuscript 1's abstract "We prove the Eisenbud–Green–Harris and lex-plus-powers conjectures over every
characteristic-zero field for homogeneous regular sequences of any positive
length, with degrees at least two. For every homogeneous ideal containing such
a sequence, the corresponding lex-plus-powers ideal has the same Hilbert
function and at least as large a graded Betti number in every homological and
internal degree. [S3:46–51]" and
manuscript 2's "We prove the Eisenbud–Green–Harris conjecture over every characteristic-zero
field for homogeneous regular sequences of any positive length, with degrees
at least two. Thus every homogeneous ideal containing such a sequence has the
Hilbert function of a monomial ideal containing the corresponding pure powers. [S5:63–66]"

## Undergraduate summary

The Eisenbud–Green–Harris conjecture predicts that a regular sequence
can be replaced by the corresponding pure powers without changing the
Hilbert function of a containing ideal; the lex-plus-powers conjecture
is the same prediction strengthened to graded Betti numbers
[S4:4–38]. Over $\mathbb C$, manuscript 2 constructs commuting linear forms
with division-algebra coefficients that realize a monomial ideal with
the same Hilbert function [S6:5–31]:

> **theorem**
Let $n\ge1$, let $2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_n$, and let
$f_1,\ldots,f_n$ be a homogeneous regular sequence in
$\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with $\deg f_i=a_i$. There exist a finitely
generated field extension $k/\mathbb C$, a finite-dimensional central division
$k$-algebra $\Delta$, and a commutative graded $k$-subalgebra
\[
 k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\subseteq A\subseteq\Delta[x_1,\ldots,x_n]
\]
generated by finitely many pairwise commuting linear forms, with
distinguished $t_1,\ldots,t_n\in A_1$, such that
$$\label{eq:construction-triangular}
 t_i^{a_i}=c_i f_i+\sum_{l<i}B_{il}f_l+\sum_{d>i}C_{id}t_d
 \qquad(1\le i\le n).$$
Here $c_i\in k^\times$, $B_{il}\in A_{a_i-a_l}$, and
$C_{id}\in A_{a_i-1}$. Moreover, $A$ is finite over $k[x]$, and
\[
 \{t_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots t_n^{\alpha_n}:
             \alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\}
\]
is a basis of the full polynomial algebra $\Delta[x]$ as a left
$\Delta$-vector space. [S6:33–56]

and a least-exponent argument then gives the Artinian EGH statement in
manuscript 2 [S6:58–72]:

> **corollary**
Let $n\ge1$ and $2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_n$. Suppose that a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ contains a regular sequence $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ with $\deg f_i=a_i$. There is a monomial ideal $J\subseteq\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ such that
\[
 x_i^a_i\in J\quad(1\le i\le n),
 \qquad
 H_S/J(d)=H_S/I(d)\quadfor every d\ge0.
\] [S6:65–72]

Manuscript 1 sharpens the conclusion to Betti numbers. Its main
theorem, for a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$
containing a regular sequence of degrees $2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_n$,
produces a single lex-plus-powers ideal $J$ with
$\beta^S_{p,j}(S/I)\le\beta^S_{p,j}(S/J)$ for every homological and
internal degree 

> **Theorem 1.1**
For every \(n\ge1\), every ordered degree sequence
\(2\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_n\), and every homogeneous ideal
\(I\subseteq\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) containing a regular sequence of these
degrees, the spaces in   exist and
\(J=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}J_d\) is a homogeneous ideal. For this single ideal,
\[
 \beta^S_p,j(S/I)\le\beta^S_p,j(S/J)
 \qquadfor every \(p\ge0\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z\).
\]
There is no restriction on the degrees or number of the additional
generators of \(I\). [S4:41–53]

## Graduate summary

**The division-coefficient reduction.** The common engine behind both
statements is the commuting-form construction of manuscript 2
[S6:33–56]: a finite-dimensional central division $k$-algebra $\Delta$
and pairwise commuting linear forms $t_i$ satisfying triangular power
identities, whose ordered monomials form a left-$\Delta$ basis of the
*full* polynomial algebra $\Delta[x]$ — not just the complete-
intersection quotient. That full basis is what manuscript 1 uses for
resolution transfer [S4:164–227]: a minimal $S$-resolution of $S/I$
becomes a minimal $T$-resolution of $N/IN$ (every Betti number scaled by
the same factor), and a filtered Koszul comparison cancels the scale to
give a monomial ideal $M\supseteq P$ with equal Hilbert function and no
smaller Betti numbers. Root-of-unity distractions, transfers inside the
exponent box, and an endpoint decomposition on the $z=x_n$ slices then
compare the Betti numbers against the prescribed lex-plus-powers ideal
[S4:164–227].

**From $\mathbb C$ to characteristic zero.** Both manuscripts state the
Artinian result over $\mathbb C$ first and then remove the two restrictions by
reduction: Caviglia–Maclagan's Artinian reduction for EGH and
Caviglia–Kummini's for lex-plus-powers Betti numbers, together with a
finite coefficient-field descent, give the stated characteristic-zero
forms for regular sequences of any positive length
[S4:55–96, S6:74–110].

## History of the conjecture

The EGH Hilbert-function conjecture arose in Eisenbud, Green, and
Harris's higher Castelnuovo work, and the lex-plus-powers strengthening
is generally attributed to Charalambous and Evans. The
prescribed-powers Betti comparison developed through Mermin–Peeva–
Stillman (squares of the variables), Murai (strongly stable plus powers
over any field), and Mermin–Murai (a monomial regular sequence, over
every field, with the reduction to a Hilbert-matching monomial ideal
isolated as a conjecture) [S4:105–159]. For nonmonomial regular
sequences the known ranges imposed growth conditions on the degrees —
Caviglia–Maclagan, Caviglia–De Stefani, and the characteristic-zero
Betti theorem of Caviglia–Sammartano — or split the forms into linear
factors; the present theorems impose no such condition
[S4:105–159]. Manuscript 2's earlier-results list covers the degree-
only and structure cases (Chen, Cooper, Chong, Abedelfatah,
Kuzmanovski) [S6:122–148], and its proof strategy combines binary
addition matrices from a curve of the form $z^b=u^b+v^b$ with a
division-algebra extension proved by rank-divisibility over Picard
varieties [S6:154–284].

## Consequences

**The full characteristic-zero statement.** Both conjectures hold over
every characteristic-zero field for homogeneous regular sequences of any
positive length with degrees at least two: a single lex-plus-powers
ideal has the same Hilbert function and no smaller graded Betti number,
with no restriction on the ideal's additional generators
[S4:55–96, S6:74–110]. The corollaries also cover non-Artinian
quotients and non-algebraically-closed fields.

**The monomial-reduction conjecture, resolved.** The Mermin–Murai
reduction to a Hilbert-matching monomial ideal with no smaller graded
Betti numbers — their Conjecture 10.2 — is obtained from the division
coefficients, and the subsequent monomial comparison is proved
[S4:105–159, S4:164–227].

**Betti numbers, not just Hilbert function.** Manuscript 1's
conclusion is strictly stronger than the EGH Hilbert-function statement:
the Betti inequalities are the new content, and the Hilbert function is
a corollary [S4:41–53].
