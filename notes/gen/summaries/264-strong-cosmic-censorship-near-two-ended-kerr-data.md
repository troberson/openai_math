## Undergraduate summary

The **cosmic censorship conjecture** is the idea that the "weird"
parts of a gravitational collapse — singularities where the
laws of physics break down — are hidden behind black-hole
horizons and cannot be seen by a distant observer. The **strong**
version says that the future of a generic spacetime cannot be
extended beyond a certain "causal boundary" (the Cauchy
horizon) in a way that preserves the equations — the
singularity is unavoidable, not an artifact of a special
solution. **Kerr** is the rotating black-hole solution: unlike
the non-rotating (Schwarzschild) case, Kerr has an inner
horizon, and it was long thought that small perturbations might
let signals leak through the inner horizon, violating strong
censorship. These three manuscripts prove the **local** strong
censorship statement near a fixed rotating (subextremal,
i.e. slowly rotating) Kerr black hole: for a "generic"
(open-and-dense) set of nearby initial data, the maximal
development has **no** future extension of various low-regularity
types [S1:6620, S2:619].
