## Undergraduate summary

Physics has two equally good ways to describe a kind of theory called
**two-dimensional conformal field theory**. One is **vertex operator
algebras** (VOAs), which record the algebraic rules the building-block
"fields" of the theory obey. The other is **conformal nets**, which
assign a collection of measurable observables to each interval of a
circle. For decades, mathematicians suspected the two descriptions
were two faces of the same object — the "strong-locality
conjecture." This paper proves the suspicion in the cleanest
important case: when the VOA is **simple**, **unitary** (its energy
comes from a genuine physical inner product), and **strongly rational**
(the module category is as tame as it gets). It shows such a VOA
produces a conformal net that is **completely rational** [S3:45–47]
— and, crucially, that the two descriptions match not just as
categories of objects but as *braided* unitary tensor categories.
