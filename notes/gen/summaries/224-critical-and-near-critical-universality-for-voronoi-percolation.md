## Undergraduate summary

**Voronoi percolation** is a model of phase transition built from a random
tessellation of the plane (a Voronoi diagram). Each cell is "infected"
(open) with some probability, and we study whether a path of open cells
crosses from one side of a region to the other. At the **critical
probability**, the crossing behavior becomes universal: it depends only
on the geometry of the region, not on the details of the tessellation.
**Cardy's formula** (1984) gives the exact crossing probability for the
triangular lattice at criticality. This family proves that the same
formula holds for Voronoi percolation [S5:56–58, S2:533], and
goes further to show that the **near-critical** (slightly off
criticality) behavior is also universal [S3:37–43].

## Graduate summary

**Cardy's formula for Voronoi percolation**
[S5:56–58, S2:533]. The annealed crossing probability in
critical planar Poisson–Voronoi percolation equals the
triangular-lattice conformal crossing function in every bounded
Jordan quadrilateral. This proves the annealed
crossing-probability form of the conformal-invariance conjecture
for this model.

**Pivotal amplitude** [S4:44–49, S2:533]. The expected
number of color-pivotal cells for a unit-square crossing in
critical Poisson–Voronoi percolation is asymptotic to a
positive constant times $`\varepsilon^{-3/4}`$, where the point
intensity is $`\varepsilon^{-2}`$. No rate of convergence in Cardy's
formula is required. This normalization constant is the key
ingredient for the quenched universality result.

**Quenched near-critical universality**
[S3:37–43, S2:533]. Taking Cardy's formula as input, the
conditional joint near-critical crossing-threshold laws for
rational polygonal quadrilaterals converge in probability over
tessellations to the same law as on the triangular lattice,
under the monotone coupling. Each model is normalized by its
own expected number of color-pivotal sites for a unit-square
crossing.

## History of the conjecture

**Cardy's formula** (Cardy, 1984) is the conformal mapping
formula for crossing probabilities in 2D percolation at
criticality. It was first proved rigorously for the
triangular lattice by Smirnov (2001), who showed that the
conformal limit of the lattice crossing probability is
Cardy's function. The **Poisson–Voronoi model**
replaces the regular triangular lattice with a random
tessellation generated by a Poisson point process. The
conformal invariance of this model was a long-standing open
problem because the random geometry of the tessellation
breaks the exact lattice structures used in Smirnov's
proof. This family resolves it [S5:56–58] and extends
to the near-critical regime [S3:37–43].

**Prior (my training data, not this repo).** Cardy's
formula, Smirnov's proof for the triangular lattice, and
the general conformal invariance program for 2D critical
models are standard. The extension to the random Voronoi
geometry and the quenched near-critical universality are
the novel contributions. I cannot confirm these
independently from training alone.

## Consequences

- **Cardy's formula for Voronoi percolation**
  [S5:56–58]: the annealed crossing probability in
  every bounded Jordan quadrilateral equals the
  conformal-invariant reference function.
- **The pivotal amplitude**
  [S4:44–49]: the expected pivotal-cell count scales
  as $`\varepsilon^{-3/4}`$, providing the normalization for the
  quenched statement.
- **Quenched near-critical universality**
  [S3:37–43]: conditional on the environment, the
  near-critical threshold laws match the triangular-lattice
  reference, in environment probability.
