## Undergraduate summary

A "log Calabi–Yau pair" $(X,D)$ is a normal space with
$K_X+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0$ — some positive multiple of the log canonical
divisor is linearly trivial. The "log abundance" conjecture asks
whether that multiple can be chosen so that the line bundle is actually
generated by global sections (semiample). The theorem 

> **theorem**
Assume Assumption  . Let \(X\) be a normal irreducible
compact Kähler complex analytic space, and let \(\Delta\geq0\) be a rational
divisor such that \((X,\Delta)\) is log canonical and \(K_X+\Delta\) is
rational Cartier. If \(K_X+\Delta\) is analytically nef, then it is
semiample. Explicitly, there is an integer \(m>0\) such that
\(m(K_X+\Delta)\) is Cartier and
\[
 H^0\bigl(X,\mathcal O_X(m(K_X+\Delta))\bigr)\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathcal O_X
 \longrightarrow \mathcal O_X(m(K_X+\Delta))
\]
is surjective at every point of \(X\). [S5:56–68]

The assumption is logarithmic Iitaka subadditivity for smooth
projective varieties with reduced boundary — a condition that has been
proved in the cases where the log canonical divisor of the very general
fiber is already abundant [S5:111–117].

**The uniform index theorem.** The companion manuscript proves the
finite-rational-coefficient slc index conjecture: for every dimension
$d\ge4$ and finite rational coefficient set $\Phi$, there is a common
trivializing multiple 

> **Theorem 1.1**
For every integer $d\ge4$ and every finite set
$\Phi\subset[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q$, there is an integer $a(d,\Phi)>0$ with the
following property. Let $(X,B)$ be a connected projective equidimensional
semi-log-canonical log Calabi–Yau pair of dimension $d$ over $k$, with
every nonzero coefficient of $B$ in $\Phi$. Then
\[
            a(d,\Phi)(K_X+B) is Cartier,
        \qquad \mathcal O_X\bigl(a(d,\Phi)(K_X+B)\bigr)\simeq\mathcal O_X.
\] [S8:29–39]

The bound is independent of the number of components [S8:41–47].
"The bound is independent of the number of irreducible components of $X$
and of the number of log canonical strata on their birational models.
It is a common trivializing multiple, so it clears the local Cartier
indices and kills the remaining global torsion at the same time.
Together with the theorem in dimensions at most three of
\citet[Corollary 1.6]JiangLiu, this proves the finite-rational-coefficient
slc index conjecture \citep[Conjecture 1.5]JiangLiu. [S8:41–47]"

## Graduate summary

**The birational intermediate.** The proof does not go directly from
nef to semiample. It passes through a stronger birational statement:
every pseudo-effective smooth log adjoint has a semiample positive part
and an effective rational fixed divisor equal to its analytic
divisorial negative part . "We prove log abundance for compact Kähler spaces under logarithmic Iitaka
subadditivity for smooth projective varieties with reduced boundary. The
argument passes through a stronger birational statement: every
pseudo-effective smooth log adjoint has a semiample positive part and an
effective rational fixed divisor equal to its analytic divisorial negative
part. This form both supports the induction and retains the line-bundle
information needed for generation on the original space. [S5:10–16]" This decomposition both
supports the induction dimension by dimension and retains the
line-bundle information needed for generation on the original space.

**The induction step.** The key mechanism is a covering-family
construction: pseudo-effectivity and an intersection argument isolate
positive-dimensional boundary fibers, a local root construction
separates positive and negative divisor supports, an SNC
Hodge-module calculation kills every finite-order obstruction to
lifting, and a Baire argument (using compactness of cycle-space
components) produces a covering family . "Pseudo-effectivity of the canonical pullback and an intersection argument
isolate positive-dimensional boundary fibers away from components whose
signed coefficients are nonpositive. A local root construction adapted from
\cite[Proposition 3.3]LI separates the positive and negative divisor
supports. An SNC Hodge-module calculation extends the lifting method of
\cite[Sections 10–12]BL to residual poles and kills every finite-order
obstruction to lifting such a fiber. Douady space and Artin approximation
give compact deformations leaving the boundary; compactness of the cycle-space
components and relative compactness of bounded-volume cycles allow a Baire
argument to produce a covering family. Simplicity
forces the actual nef line to be torsion. The program comparison and
Lemma   then permit subtraction of the added boundary,
yielding the inductive decomposition. [S5:226–238]"

**The Iitaka subadditivity input.** The assumption is used in the
reverse direction from the known cases: the projective companion
derives good models for pseudo-effective projective lc adjoints from
the subadditivity assumption, and those good models anchor the
Kähler induction [S5:111–117].

## History of the conjecture

The compact Kähler abundance problem builds on the projective
minimal model program extended to the non-algebraic setting.
Demailly–Peternell established foundational results; Höring–Peternell
constructed minimal models for normal $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial terminal
compact Kähler threefolds. Campana–Höring–Peternell treated canonical
abundance, and Das–Ou established log abundance for threefolds
. ". Höring and Peternell constructed minimal models
for normal \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial terminal compact Kähler threefolds with pseudo-effective canonical class
 . Campana, Höring, and Peternell treated canonical abundance
 , and Das and Ou
established log abundance for compact Kähler log canonical threefolds [S5:94–98]"

The Iitaka subadditivity conjecture of Fujino (2017, corrected 2020)
is the key input. Hashizume proved the reduced-SNC version under an
abundance hypothesis on the very general fiber [S5:111–117].

## Consequences

**Full log abundance in every dimension.** Under the Iitaka
subadditivity assumption, every analytically nef log canonical class
on a normal compact Kähler space is semiample, in every dimension and
including the zero-boundary case [S5:56–68].

**The slc index conjecture.** The uniform index theorem resolves the
finite-rational-coefficient semi-log-canonical index conjecture in
dimension at least 4, completing the picture with the dimension
$\le3$ result of Jiang–Liu [S8:29–39].

**A 14-manuscript program.** The remaining manuscripts supply the
individual inputs: good minimal models for fourfolds [S9],
characteristic-zero abundance [S10], minimal metrics and
interior injectivity [S11], fourfold nonvanishing [S12], section
lifting [S13], Schnell fiber spaces [S14], and the uniform
Iitaka-fibration machinery [S15]–[S20].
