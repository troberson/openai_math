## Undergraduate summary

The Grothendieck–Teichmüller (GT) Lie algebra encodes the
infinitesimal symmetries of algebraic fundamental groups — roughly,
the hidden algebra behind the braid group and the fundamental group of
the three-punctured line. Deligne and Drinfeld predicted an explicit
description of its rational form: it should be a *free* Lie algebra
generated by one element in each odd weight 3, 5, 7, ….

This paper proves that prediction. The rational GT Lie algebra,
equipped with the Ihara bracket, is freely generated by one element in
each odd weight at least three, and the description extends to a
continuous isomorphism after completing by weight.

The proof works with a concrete "equation space" $`W`$: the set of
solutions to antisymmetry, a three-term relation, and the four-strand
pentagon equation, with the Ihara bracket. The main statement


> **theorem**
There are homogeneous elements
$`\sigma_{2k+1}\in W_{2k+1}`$, one for each $`k\ge1`$, for which
$$\mathrm{Lie}_{\mathbb Q}\langle e_3,e_5,e_7,\ldots\rangle
 \longrightarrow (W,\{\, ,\,\}),\qquad
 e_{2k+1}\longmapsto\sigma_{2k+1},$$
is an isomorphism of graded Lie algebras.
In particular, $`W`$ is closed under the bracket
 . The induced map on completions by weight is a
continuous graded isomorphism. [S3:50–62]

## Graduate summary

The conjecture has three parts, and the theorem delivers all three
together: existence of the odd-weight generators, freeness of the
Lie algebra they generate, and exhaustion of the equation space
[S3:80–82].

**Main theorem.** 

> **theorem**
There are homogeneous elements
$`\sigma_{2k+1}\in W_{2k+1}`$, one for each $`k\ge1`$, for which
$$\mathrm{Lie}_{\mathbb Q}\langle e_3,e_5,e_7,\ldots\rangle
 \longrightarrow (W,\{\, ,\,\}),\qquad
 e_{2k+1}\longmapsto\sigma_{2k+1},$$
is an isomorphism of graded Lie algebras.
In particular, $`W`$ is closed under the bracket
 . The induced map on completions by weight is a
continuous graded isomorphism. [S3:50–62]

The generators $`\sigma_{2k+1}`$ are not canonical; the assertion is
that no additional Lie relations hold among them and that they span
every solution of the antisymmetry, three-term, and pentagon
equations [S3:64–67].

**Context.** The equation space $`W`$ arises from Drinfeld's
associators: the four-strand pentagon equation is the compatibility
between reassociation and braiding, and the Ihara bracket is the
infinitesimal version of the associator group law [S3:69–78]. Brown's
work on mixed Tate motives proves the motivic Galois group is free on
generators in weights $`3,5,7,\ldots`$, which already supplies a free Lie
subalgebra of $`W`$; the paper's content is showing that subalgebra is
all of $`W`$ [S3:84–90].

**Proof strategy.** The argument runs through eight sections: integral
Lie-algebra foundations and a special identity; a filtered degeneration
of the pentagon; deletion operators whose leading projection is
injective; an image estimate; a categorical reformulation; a holonomy
computation; and a weight-by-weight induction that pins the
generators [S2:12–19].

## history of the conjecture

Prior (my training data, not this repo): the Grothendieck–Teichmüller
group and its Lie algebra were introduced by Drinfeld (1990) as the
symmetry group of his associators, motivated by the problem of
classifying braid-group representations. The explicit freeness
prediction for the rational form — one generator in every odd weight
$`\ge 3`$ — combines Ihara's odd-weight existence result (credited in
Drinfeld's Proposition 6.3) with Brown's computation of the motivic
Galois group of the three-punctured line over $`\mathbb{Z}`$. The
open content was the *exhaustion* step: that the motivic free Lie
subalgebra already fills the associator equation space, with no
additional relations.

## Consequences

**The rational GT Lie algebra has an explicit presentation.**
Freely generated by one element in each odd weight $`\ge 3`$ with the
Ihara bracket, which closes the gap between the motivic description
and the associator description.

**A continuous isomorphism after weight completion.** The generators
extend past the graded level to a homeomorphism of filtered
completions, so the statement is stable under the topology the
representation theory of braid groups actually uses [S3:60–61].
